\section{Introduction}

A spectral representation compresses a graph into a small number of
coordinates per vertex. The usual next step is to group these coordinates
by geometric proximity. We instead ask a likelihood question: can the
retained spectral information be used to compare the Bernoulli connection
patterns of the candidate communities?

The basic construction is simple. Approximate the adjacency matrix by
its rank-\(K\) spectral reconstruction, sum the reconstructed entries
within each current community, and insert these real-valued block counts
into the Bernoulli row likelihood. Alternate estimation of the block
probabilities with likelihood-based label updates. This spectrum-only
version introduces the method without degree normalization,
regularization, or an observed-degree correction.

The general sparse classifier retains this likelihood principle but
adds two explicit operations. High-degree regularization stabilizes the
spectral representation, and the original degree vector restores the
exact total number of incident edges in each reconstructed row. These
operations play different roles: regularization controls the spectrum;
degree correction preserves a measured component of the likelihood
evidence. They remain part of the general algorithm even though they
are not needed to explain the initial spectrum-only construction.

Our main theorem concerns this complete degree-plus-spectrum classifier.
For arbitrary fixed \(K\), comparable community sizes and the positive
full-rank block-probability class specified below, its expected
misclassification proportion is at most a constant times the error of
the true-parameter incident-row oracle. The result holds for every
\(np^*\to\infty\), subject to \(n(p^*)^2\le C_1\); it imposes no logarithmic
lower bound on expected degree. The spectrum-only version is a separate
motivating procedure, not a replacement for the estimator covered by
this theorem.

Sharp testing bounds and rate-optimal community recovery are developed
by Zhou and Li~\cite{zhouli}; spectral clustering guarantees are studied
by Zhang~\cite{zhang}. Our analysis combines sparse graph
regularization~\cite{llv2017}, local likelihood comparisons, and
truncated spectral-row energy. At larger degree scales we also use a
bounded-entry matrix norm inequality~\cite{bvh}. Rather than requiring
every spectral row to be uniformly regular, the proof controls the
average classification risk and the probability that a tested row
encounters exceptional spectral coefficients.

After defining one common model and oracle, Section~\ref{sec:simple}
explains the spectrum-only version. Section~\ref{restore:algorithm}
gives the complete sparse algorithm and the exact relation between the
two reconstructions. Section~\ref{sec:main-result} states its oracle-risk
guarantee. All proofs required for that guarantee are included in the
appendices of this article.

\section{Model and the incident-row oracle}\label{sec:statements}

There are \(n\) vertices and a fixed number \(K\ge2\) of communities.
Following the notation of Zhou and Li~\cite{zhouli}, let
\(z\in[K]^n\) denote the labels and \(P\) the block-probability matrix.
Write \(n_a=\#\{i:z_i=a\}\). The symmetric adjacency matrix \(A\)
has zero diagonal and independent upper-triangular entries satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mean-model}
 A_{ij}\sim\Ber(P_{z_i z_j}),\qquad
 i<j.
\end{equation}
Following Zhou and Li~\cite{zhouli}, define the maximum connection
probability by
\[
 p^*=\max_{a,b\in[K]}P_{ab},\qquad d=np^*.
\]
The abbreviation $d$ is used repeatedly in the proofs; it is not an
additional model parameter. For fixed $\beta\ge1$ and $\omega>1$, assume
\begin{equation}\label{eq:structural}
 n_a\ge\frac{n}{\beta K},\qquad P=P^\top,\qquad
 0<\frac{p^*}{\omega}\le P_{ab}\le p^*,\qquad
 \sigma_K(P)\ge c p^*.
\end{equation}
Here $\sigma_1(P)\ge\cdots\ge\sigma_K(P)$ are the singular values
of the $K$-by-$K$ matrix $P$, and the positive constant $c$ in the
last condition is fixed. Thus $\beta$ measures community imbalance,
$\omega$ bounds the ratio of the largest and smallest connection
probabilities, and the last condition keeps the normalized signal
nondegenerate. Only the displayed lower bound on community sizes is
needed. The matrix $P/p^*$ may vary with $n$ inside this class.
Constants in bounds may depend on fixed $K,\beta,\omega$, the fixed
lower bound on $\sigma_K(P)/p^*$, and the stated upper bound for
$n(p^*)^2$, but not on $n$. Elsewhere $c,C$ denote positive constants
whose values may change between occurrences.

For a partition \(\widehat z\), define
\[
 \Mis(\widehat z,z)=\frac1n\min_{\pi\in S_K}
       \sum_i\ind{\pi(\widehat z_i)\ne z_i}.
\]
Here $S_K$ is the set of permutations of $[K]$.
The oracle knows \(P\) and all labels except the tested label. At vertex
\(i\) it maximizes the exact Bernoulli log likelihood
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sbm-oracle}
 L_i(a)=\sum_{j\ne i}
 \{A_{ij}\log P_{az_j}+(1-A_{ij})\log(1-P_{az_j})\}.
\end{equation}
It uses uniform candidate weights and a fixed rule for ties. An exact
global community-size constraint is not used to infer the missing
label. Its average error probability is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:oracle-risk}
 R_{\mathrm{oracle}}
 =\frac1n\sum_i\PP\!\left\{\argmax_{a\in[K]}L_i(a)\ne z_i\right\}.
\end{equation}
This is the oracle used throughout the article.

\section{The spectrum-only likelihood construction}\label{sec:simple}

We first describe the likelihood idea using only an eigensystem of the
original adjacency matrix. No observed degree vector is supplied to the
decoder in this section. The notation \(U,\Lambda\) here refers to the
\(K\) eigenpairs of \(A\) with largest absolute eigenvalues:
\[
 AU=U\Lambda,\qquad U^\top U=I_K,\qquad
 A_K=U\Lambda U^\top.
\]
Eigenvalues are selected by magnitude, so informative negative
eigenvalues are retained as well. Remove the diagonal by setting
\(F^{(0)}_{ii}=0\) and \(F^{(0)}_{ij}=(A_K)_{ij}\) for \(j\ne i\).
The superscript \((0)\) denotes only this spectrum-only construction.

\subsection{From spectral coordinates to block evidence}

For current labels \(e\in[K]^n\), define the self-excluded community
sizes and reconstructed block counts by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:simple-counts}
 n_c^{(-i)}(e)=\#\{j\ne i:e_j=c\},\qquad
 b_{ic}^{(0)}(e)=\sum_{j\ne i:e_j=c}(A_K)_{ij}.
\end{equation}
These quantities can be evaluated from the retained eigenpairs:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:simple-factor-counts}
 b_{ic}^{(0)}(e)
 =U_{i\cdot}\Lambda
       \left(\sum_{j:e_j=c}U_{j\cdot}\right)^\top
       -(A_K)_{ii}\ind{e_i=c}.
\end{equation}
Thus the decoder does not need a second lookup of the incident edges.
The counts need not be integers or nonnegative; they are reconstructed
linear statistics, not independently observed Bernoulli data.

If the other labels and the block probabilities were known, the oracle
score in \eqref{eq:sbm-oracle} could be written using the actual incident
block counts. The spectrum-only score keeps the same form and substitutes
\eqref{eq:simple-counts} and fitted probabilities:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:simple-score}
 L_i^{(0)}(a)
 =\sum_{c=1}^K\left\{
 b_{ic}^{(0)}(e)\log\widehat P_{ac}^{(0)}
 +\bigl[n_c^{(-i)}(e)-b_{ic}^{(0)}(e)\bigr]
                   \log(1-\widehat P_{ac}^{(0)})\right\}.
\end{equation}
The selected community maximizes this score. Both the edge and nonedge
terms are retained. In particular, the rule compares connection
probabilities rather than Euclidean distances between spectral points.
Since \(F^{(0)}\) is reconstructed and may be signed,
\eqref{eq:simple-score} is a Bernoulli \emph{working criterion}; it is
not a claim that its reconstructed entries are independent observations.

\subsection{A finite spectrum-only procedure}

The following specification makes the simple version explicit. Normalize
nonzero rows of \(U\) to unit length and give each zero row a fixed unit
direction. Select a remaining row with the largest number of remaining
rows within distance \(1/8\), remove rows within distance \(1/4\), and
repeat for \(K\) representatives. Assign every row to its nearest
representative. All ties are deterministic. This is the same radius-count
initialization used later, now applied to the unregularized eigensystem.

For labels \(e\), let \(\mathcal I_{ab}(e)\), \(a\le b\), be the set
of ordered off-diagonal pairs whose unordered label pair is \(\{a,b\}\).
For definiteness, use the spectrum-only clipping level \(n^{-2}\) and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:simple-fit}
 \widehat P_{ab}^{(0)}=\widehat P_{ba}^{(0)}
 =\operatorname{clip}_{[n^{-2},\,1/2]}
   \left(\frac{\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal I_{ab}(e)}F^{(0)}_{ij}}
                   {|\mathcal I_{ab}(e)|}\right).
\end{equation}
For \(n\ge K+1\), the clipping interval is nonempty. It keeps the
logarithms finite without using raw degrees or the unknown probability
scale. Fit \eqref{eq:simple-fit}, update all labels simultaneously using
\eqref{eq:simple-score}, and repeat for a specified finite positive
number of updates. A fixed fallback partition handles empty pair sets,
insufficient representatives, or other undefined operations. No correct
initial labels or true block probabilities are inputs.

The block fit is the exact maximizer of its clipped Bernoulli working
criterion, even for signed reconstructed totals: the derivative of
\(B\log q+(N-B)\log(1-q)\) has the sign of \(B/N-q\).
This coordinatewise fact does not assert monotonicity or convergence of
the simultaneous label updates. Nor is the constant-oracle-risk theorem
below asserted for this unregularized, uncorrected procedure. Its purpose
is to expose the likelihood construction before specifying the extra
operations used in the general sparse guarantee.

\section{The general degree-plus-spectrum algorithm}
\label{restore:algorithm}

The input is \(A\) and \(K\). Fix a sufficiently large constant
\(b_0>0\) and a sufficiently small constant \(c_0>0\), depending
only on the structural class. All choices and
ties below are deterministic. A fixed fallback partition defines the
algorithm whenever a required operation is undefined.
The spectrum-only construction uses a low-rank approximation both for
connection structure and for each row's total mass. These need not be
equally accurate. The general version keeps the observed total mass
exact and regularizes the spectral calculation. The encoder reads the
adjacency matrix; after encoding, the decoder uses only the original
degree vector and the retained eigenpairs. Every step below is part of
the algorithm covered by the main theorem. The likelihood updates are
ordinary SBM updates, not degree-parameter profiling.

\paragraph{Adjacency spectrum.}
Write
\[
 \mathbf d=A\one,\qquad D_i=\mathbf d_i,\qquad
 \bar D=\frac1n\sum_iD_i.
\]
The all-zero graph has the fixed output. Otherwise put
\(\tau=b_0\bar D\). The only spectral preprocessing uses the diagonal
matrix \(W=\diag(W_i)\), where
\[
 W_i=\begin{cases}
  1,&D_i\le\tau,\\
  \sqrt{\tau/D_i},&D_i>\tau.
 \end{cases}
\]
Thus edges between vertices below the threshold keep their original
weights. Let \(\widetilde A=WAW\), and retain the raw degrees and
the \(K\) eigenpairs with largest absolute eigenvalues:
\[
 U^\top U=I_K,\qquad \widetilde A U=U\Lambda.
\]
Only \((\mathbf d,U,\Lambda)\) are used after this step; \(\tau,W\)
are recovered from \(\mathbf d\). All informative eigenvalues are
retained by magnitude, allowing either sign.

\paragraph{Spectral reconstruction and degree correction.}
Write the rank-\(K\) reconstruction on the original adjacency scale as
\[
 F=W^{-1}U\Lambda U^\top W^{-1}.
\]
When the cap is inactive, \(W=I\) and
\(F=A_K=U\Lambda U^\top\). Set the diagonal to zero and distribute
each row's degree discrepancy equally over its off-diagonal entries:
\begin{equation}\label{restore:reconstruction}
 \widehat A_{ii}=0,\qquad
 \widehat A_{ij}=F_{ij}
      +\frac{D_i-\sum_{k\ne i}F_{ik}}{n-1},\quad j\ne i.
\end{equation}
Thus \(\widehat A\one=\mathbf d\) exactly.
The matrix \(\widehat A\) can be signed and need not be symmetric. Its entries
are not clipped. The formula is computed entirely from the retained
quantities. In particular, an isolated vertex has a zero reconstructed
row and does not stop classification of other vertices.


\paragraph{Relation to the simple block counts.}
For any fixed partition \(e\), write
\[
 b_{ic}^{F}(e)=\sum_{j\ne i:e_j=c}F_{ij},\qquad
 \delta_i=D_i-\sum_{j\ne i}F_{ij}.
\]
The degree-preserving counts satisfy the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:count-bridge}
 \widehat b_{ic}(e):=\sum_{j\ne i:e_j=c}\widehat A_{ij}
 =b_{ic}^{F}(e)+\frac{n_c^{(-i)}(e)}{n-1}\delta_i,
 \qquad \sum_c\widehat b_{ic}(e)=D_i.
\end{equation}
When the cap is inactive, \(b_{ic}^{F}(e)=b_{ic}^{(0)}(e)\).
The second term in \eqref{eq:count-bridge} is still present: an inactive
cap does not imply that degree correction is inactive.
At the same fixed labels and fitted probability matrix, replacing
\(b^F\) by \(\widehat b\) changes the score contrast between candidates
\(a\) and \(b\) by exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:score-bridge}
 \delta_i\sum_{c=1}^K\frac{n_c^{(-i)}(e)}{n-1}
 \log\frac{\widehat P_{ac}(1-\widehat P_{bc})}
               {\widehat P_{bc}(1-\widehat P_{ac})}.
\end{equation}
This identity shows where the retained degree enters the LRT. It compares
one update at fixed inputs, not the final outputs of two differently
fitted algorithms. The spectral reconstruction supplies block structure;
the correction supplies the exact row total.

\paragraph{Initialization.}
Normalize each nonzero row of \(U\) to unit length; assign a fixed unit
direction to a zero row. Among the remaining rows, select one having
the largest number of remaining rows within distance \(1/8\), and
remove all rows within distance \(1/4\) of it. Repeat for \(K\) centers,
then assign every row to its nearest center. If fewer than \(K\)
centers can be selected, use the fixed fallback. This is a finite
procedure using \(O(Kn^2)\) distance comparisons.

\paragraph{Block fitting and classification.}
Given current labels \(\widehat z\), for \(a\le b\) let
\(\mathcal I_{ab}(\widehat z)\) contain all ordered pairs \(i\ne j\)
whose unordered label pair is \(\{a,b\}\). The symmetric fitted
matrix is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block-fit}
 \widehat P_{ab}=\widehat P_{ba}
   =\operatorname{clip}_{[c_0\bar D/n,\,1/2]}
     \left(\frac{\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal I_{ab}(\widehat z)}
                     \widehat A_{ij}}
                  {|\mathcal I_{ab}(\widehat z)|}\right).
\end{equation}
This maximizes the ordered-pair Bernoulli working criterion with a
symmetric block probability. The fit is interior on the event used in
the proof. Update all labels simultaneously by maximizing
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fitted-row-score}
 \widehat L_i(a)=\sum_{j\ne i}
 \{\widehat A_{ij}\log\widehat P_{a\widehat z_j}
   +(1-\widehat A_{ij})\log(1-\widehat P_{a\widehat z_j})\}.
\end{equation}
In particular, the full nonedge term is retained. There is no target
degree parameter to profile in this ordinary SBM algorithm.

Perform at least one block fit and likelihood update. The output may
follow any finite number of these hard refits, including finite
data-dependent stopping. Empty fitted groups or undefined operations
use the stated fallback; the proof shows they do not occur on the
aggregate event. No leave-one-out graph, true label or true model
parameter is an algorithm input.

\section{Oracle-risk guarantee}\label{sec:main-result}

\begin{theorem}\label{restore:sbm-theorem}
Under \eqref{eq:mean-model}--\eqref{eq:structural}, suppose
\[
 d=np^*\longrightarrow\infty,\qquad n(p^*)^2\le C_1<\infty.
\]
For sufficiently large fixed \(b_0\) and sufficiently small fixed
\(c_0>0\), the algorithm in
\cref{restore:algorithm}, after any finite positive number of the
specified likelihood updates, satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:target}
 \E\Mis(\widehat z,z)\le C R_{\mathrm{oracle}}
\end{equation}
for all sufficiently large \(n\). The same bound holds for finite
data-dependent stopping. The constant \(C\) is independent of \(n\)
and of the number of refits.
\end{theorem}

There is no condition of the form \(np^*\ge c\log n\).
The assumptions allow general symmetric positive block profiles and
comparable, unequal community sizes. They do not require a planted
partition or equal expected degrees. The condition \(n(p^*)^2\le C_1\)
is retained as an upper sparsity assumption.

\paragraph{Scope of the probability assumptions.}
The absence of a logarithmic density floor permits, for example,
\(np^*=\log\log n\), and indeed any slower divergence. It does not remove
the separate structural condition \(P_{ab}\ge p^*/\omega\) or the lower
singular-value condition in \eqref{eq:structural}. The algorithm does
not require the true \(P\) as input, but the oracle-risk theorem is
uniform only over its stated positive, well-conditioned class. We do
not claim this theorem for arbitrary zero entries or vanishing relative
signal separation. The upper condition \(n(p^*)^2\le C_1\) is likewise
retained; it is not a lower density requirement.

The bound compares the two error probabilities at each \(n\), with a
constant independent of \(n\). It asserts a bounded risk ratio;
it does not assert that the ratio tends to one. Since a nonzero
misclassification proportion is at least \(1/n\), the theorem also
implies exact recovery whenever \(nR_{\mathrm{oracle}}\to0\).

\subsection{Proof structure}

Aggregate adjacency estimates give the spectral initializer and an
invariant \(O(n/d)\) error basin. Uniform block fitting and a local
leave-one-out comparison reduce fitted contrasts, up to an \(O(1)\)
remainder, to independent incident-row scores. Relative to the true
Bernoulli contrast, their coefficient errors have bounded tilted mean
and quadratic mass.

A truncated spectral-row energy bound controls exceptional rows in
average risk. Paying only for exceptional coefficients actually hit
by the tested row, the probability comparison bounds the fitted
classifier's risk by a constant times the oracle risk.
The appendices prove these estimates for every \(d\to\infty\),
without requiring a graph-wide bound on the largest spectral row.

The companion notes contain degree-corrected SBM extensions,

\section{Discussion}

The two presentations separate the statistical idea from the operations
needed for the general sparse result. The spectrum-only construction
shows how a spectral representation yields block evidence for a
Bernoulli LRT. The complete classifier adds high-degree regularization
and exact observed-degree preservation, while retaining the same
edge-and-nonedge likelihood principle. Its theorem compares expected
misclassification directly with \(R_{\mathrm{oracle}}\), without a
logarithmic lower bound on \(np^*\). All of the complete algorithm and
its supporting proofs remain in this ordinary-SBM article.
