\section{Main recovery results}
\label{sec:main-results}

This section states the oracle benchmark, full-graph spectral approximation, and end-to-end guarantee for every fixed $K$. The model satisfies \eqref{eq:density-condition}--\eqref{eq:model-conditions}. The oracle calculations require distinct block rows; spectral conditioning supplies a uniformly separated $K$-dimensional signal.

\subsection{Spectral information and likelihood contrasts}

For communities $a\ne b$, define the block-constant Bernoulli contrast
and the nonedge offset by
\begin{align}
 w^{ab}_j
 &=\log\frac{p_{aj}(1-p_{bj})}{p_{bj}(1-p_{aj})},\label{eq:oracle-contrast}\\
 c_i^{ab}
 &=\sum_{j\ne i}\log\frac{1-p_{aj}}{1-p_{bj}}.
\end{align}
The oracle incident-edge log-likelihood ratio is
\begin{equation}
 L_i^{ab}=A_iw^{ab}+c_i^{ab},\label{eq:oracle-llr}
\end{equation}
where $A_i$ is row $i$ of $A$.  Its diagonal coordinate contributes zero.

\begin{proposition}[Exact preservation of retained contrasts]
\label{prop:exact-preservation}
Let $U$ contain any orthonormal collection of eigenvectors of the
symmetric matrix $A$, let $\Lambda$ contain their eigenvalues, and set
$\Pi=UU^\top$ and $\widetilde A=U\Lambda U^\top$.
Then $\widetilde A=A\Pi$ and, for every vector $w$,
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde A_iw-A_iw=A_i(\Pi-\mathrm{Id})w.\label{eq:preservation-error}
\end{equation}
In particular, $w\in\operatorname{col}(U)$ implies exact preservation.
Moreover, if $P$ is nonsingular, then the rank-$K$ comparison matrix
$ZPZ^\top$ has column space
$\operatorname{col}(Z)$, and every $w^{ab}$ belongs to this population
signal space.
\end{proposition}

The proposition explains the likelihood interpretation without requiring
the eigendecomposition itself to compute $\log P$.  The eigendecomposition
retains directions; the likelihood decoder assigns the appropriate
contrast coefficients to those directions.  It also states the precise
limitation: the empirical retained space need not equal the population
space.  The actual no-self-loop mean is
$\mathbb E[A\mid z]=ZPZ^\top-\operatorname{diag}(P_{z_i z_i})$;
the rank-$K$ statement concerns $ZPZ^\top$, not the no-self-loop mean.

The first-order sparse Bernoulli contrast is
$v^{ab}_j=\log(p_{aj}/p_{bj})$, with score
\begin{equation}
 L_{i,\mathrm{lin}}^{ab}
 =\sum_{j\ne i}A_{ij}v^{ab}_j
  -\sum_{j\ne i}(P_{a,z_j}-P_{b,z_j}).
 \label{eq:oracle-linear}
\end{equation}
The following result makes its use in a sparse working score precise.

\begin{lemma}[Sparse Bernoulli likelihood expansion]
\label{lem:sparse-expansion}
There is a constant $C$ depending only on the fixed probability-ratio bounds such that, for
all sufficiently large $n$, every $i$, and every $a\ne b$,
\begin{equation}
 |L_i^{ab}-L_{i,\mathrm{lin}}^{ab}|
 \le C\{p\deg(i)+np^2\}.\label{eq:sparse-expansion-error}
\end{equation}
For every fixed $H>0$ there is a constant $D_H$ such that
$\Pr\{\deg(i)>D_H np\}\le e^{-H np}$ uniformly in $i$.
Consequently, the correction is $O(np^2)=o(np)$ outside an event of probability at most $e^{-H np}$. It need not be $o(1)$. The condition $p\to0$ is used for this sparse-score approximation.
\end{lemma}

\subsection{The oracle benchmark}

We use Zhou--Li's profile and Chernoff notation. For $\alpha\in[0,1]$, define
\begin{align}
 D_\alpha(p_{a,-i}\Vert p_{b,-i})
 &=-\sum_{j\ne i}\log\!\left[
 p_{aj}^{1-\alpha}p_{bj}^{\alpha}
 +(1-p_{aj})^{1-\alpha}(1-p_{bj})^\alpha\right],\label{eq:chernoff-information}\\
 \alpha^*_{i,ab}
 &\in\argmax_{\alpha\in[0,1]}D_\alpha(p_{a,-i}\Vert p_{b,-i}),
 \qquad D^*_{i,ab}=D_{\alpha^*_{i,ab}}(p_{a,-i}\Vert p_{b,-i}),\\
 I_n&=\min_{i,\ a\ne b}D^*_{i,ab}.
 \label{eq:exact-information}
\end{align}
Here $p_{a,-i}$ omits the absent self-loop coordinate. The notation $I_n$ is our shorthand for the minimum exact pairwise information; it is not a second definition of a rate.

Under \eqref{eq:density-condition}--\eqref{eq:model-conditions}, $I_n\asymp np$ and $I_n=\Omega(\log n)$. The normalized matrix may vary with $n$, so a limiting value of $I_n/\log n$ is not required. Zhou and Li~\cite{zhouli2020} studied optimal error rates for SBM community detection; we use their information notation and leading exponent.

\begin{lemma}[Leading oracle error exponent]
\label{lem:oracle-exponent}
Consider the binary experiment in which $z_i\in\{a,b\}$ has equal prior
probabilities and all other labels and $P$ are known.  Its Bayes risk
$R_{i,ab}^{\mathrm{or}}$ satisfies, uniformly in $i$,
\begin{equation}
 R_{i,ab}^{\mathrm{or}}=\exp\{-(1+o(1))D^*_{i,ab}\}.
 \label{eq:oracle-risk}
\end{equation}
The same leading exponent holds for fixed prior probabilities bounded
away from zero and one.
\end{lemma}

\subsection{Transfer to estimated spectral scores}

The theorem below accommodates scores estimated from the entire graph,
so it does not assume independence of a node's score and its incident
edges.  It makes the required rare-event control explicit.

\begin{theorem}[Likelihood-ratio exponent transfer]
\label{thm:exponent-transfer}
Let $\widehat\ell_i(a)$ be estimated community scores, after one common
alignment of their labels with the population labels, and classify by
$\widehat z_i\in\arg\max_a\widehat\ell_i(a)$.
Suppose there are $\tau_n=o(I_n)$ and $\delta_n\ge0$ such that, for every
$i$, an event $G_i$ satisfies
\begin{align}
 \Pr(G_i^c)&\le\delta_n,\label{eq:transfer-failure}\\
 \max_{a\ne b}
 |\widehat\ell_i(a)-\widehat\ell_i(b)-L_i^{ab}|
 &\le\tau_n\quad\text{on }G_i,\label{eq:transfer-score}\\
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{-\log\delta_n}{I_n}&\ge1,
 \label{eq:transfer-tail}
\end{align}
where $-\log0=+\infty$.
Then the permutation-invariant error fraction obeys
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb E\,\Mis(\widehat z,z)
 \le\exp\{-(1+o(1))I_n\}.
 \label{eq:conditional-rate}
\end{equation}
When $\liminf_{n\to\infty}I_n/\log n>1$, this implies
$\Pr\{\Mis(\widehat z,z)>0\}\to0$.
\end{theorem}

The condition on $\delta_n$ is essential.  An error approximation that
holds with probability $1-o(1)$ can still fail much more frequently than
the oracle makes an error, and therefore need not preserve its exponent.
The theorem asserts an upper bound on the recovery error.  In the binary
oracle experiment, data processing and Lemma~\ref{lem:oracle-exponent}
also give the matching leading lower bound for any decoder using only
the graph's spectral summary.

For the working scores actually fitted in Section~2, set
$\widehat w^{ab}_j=\log(\widehat p_{aj}/\widehat p_{bj})$ and
$\widehat c^{ab}=-\sum_j(\widehat p_{aj}-\widehat p_{bj})
+\log(\widehat\pi_a/\widehat\pi_b)$.
Writing $q_i=\widetilde A_i+(q_i-\widetilde A_i)$ yields the exact
decomposition
\begin{align}
 \widehat\ell_i(a)-\widehat\ell_i(b)-L_i^{ab}
 ={}& A_i(\Pi-\mathrm{Id})w^{ab}\notag\\
 &+\widetilde A_i(\widehat w^{ab}-w^{ab})\notag\\
 &+(q_i-\widetilde A_i)\widehat w^{ab}
   +(\widehat c^{ab}-c_i^{ab}).\label{eq:four-errors}
\end{align}
Thus projection, estimated contrasts, clipping, and offsets must all be
controlled at the scale in Theorem~\ref{thm:exponent-transfer}.
The sum includes the reconstructed diagonal used by the implemented
working score; its discrepancy with the oracle's excluded diagonal is
included in the displayed decomposition.  Neither average reconstruction accuracy
nor objective convergence removes these terms.


\subsection{A local recovery theorem for the empirical spectral fit}
The following approximation controls the empirical reconstruction computed from the whole graph. Set $\Pi_*=ZN^{-1}Z^\top$ and
\[
 q^0=\max\{A\Pi_*,\varepsilon_n\},\qquad
 q=\max\{U\Lambda U^\top,\varepsilon_n\},
\]
with the observable floor from \eqref{eq:profiles}. The comparison $q^0$ uses the true memberships only in the analysis.

\begin{lemma}[Full-graph spectral approximation]
\label{lem:full-spectral}
Under the model above with sufficiently small fixed $c_0>0$, for every fixed $H>0$ there are constants $c_H,C_H>0$ and a deterministic sequence $a_n\to0$ such that, with probability at least $1-O(e^{-H np})$,
\begin{align}
 \max_i\|q_i-q_i^0\|_1&\le a_n np,&
 c_Hp\le q_{ij}&\le C_Hp,\label{eq:full-spectral-close}\\
 \frac1{n^2p}\sum_i\|q_i-p_{z_i,*}\|_1&\le a_n.
 \label{eq:average-profile-close}
\end{align}
The reconstruction uses the empirical eigenpairs of the whole graph. No row-independent replacement or extra leave-one-out computation is required by the algorithm.
\end{lemma}

The entrywise eigenspace theorem of Abbe et al.~\citep{abbe2020}, the bounded-entry norm tail of Bandeira and van Handel~\citep{bvh2016}, and a direct Bennett row bound supply these inputs. The appendix verifies the assumptions at failure probability $e^{-H np}$. This confidence scale is needed throughout the range $p=\Omega(\log n/n)$.

\begin{theorem}[Local empirical spectral EM attains the leading exponent]
\label{thm:local-spectral-em}
Use the model and admissible observable floor of Lemma~\ref{lem:full-spectral}. Let the initial responsibilities $R^{(0)}=(R^{(0)}_{ia})$ have nonnegative rows summing to one. They may depend on the entire graph. Suppose that, after a common label alignment, for deterministic $\gamma_n\to0$ and $\delta_n\ge0$,
\begin{equation}
 \Pr\!\left\{\frac1n\sum_{i,a}|R^{(0)}_{ia}-Z_{ia}|>\gamma_n\right\}
 \le\delta_n,\qquad
 \liminf\frac{-\log\delta_n}{I_n}\ge1.
 \label{eq:local-initialization}
\end{equation}
Perform at least one responsibility-weighted profile M-step on $q$, followed by the score classification in \eqref{eq:decode}. Then
\begin{equation}
 \E\Mis(\widehat z,z)\le e^{-(1+o(1))I_n}.
 \label{eq:local-em-rate}
\end{equation}
The same bound holds after any further exact EM updates and any finite, possibly data-dependent stopping time after the first M-step. The weights are updated either unconstrained or by the exact simplex optimizer with a fixed floor $f\in[0,1/K)$. If a component has zero responsibility mass, retain its previous profile; on the weak-start event this case does not occur for large $n$. When $\liminf_{n\to\infty}I_n/\log n>1$, the output exactly recovers all labels with probability tending to one.
\end{theorem}

The local theorem controls dependent reconstructed profiles and freely fitted component means. The next theorem establishes its initialization condition through the repeated-replacement certificate.

\subsection{End-to-end recovery for every fixed order}
\label{sec:end-to-end}
\begin{theorem}[Spectral likelihood decoding for arbitrary fixed $K$]
\label{thm:certified-likelihood}
For every fixed integer $K\ge2$, under \eqref{eq:density-condition}--\eqref{eq:model-conditions} and sufficiently small fixed $c_0>0$, Algorithm~1 satisfies
\[
 \E\Mis(\widehat z,z)\le e^{-(1+o(1))I_n}.
\]
If $\liminf I_n/\log n>1$, it recovers all labels with probability tending to one. The growing initialization, replacements, and refinement access only $(U,\Lambda)$. Every proposal begins with uniform weights and may use any prescribed finite number of exact EM updates. No assumed weak initializer or global mixture optimum is required. A polynomial EM iteration cap gives polynomial runtime.
\end{theorem}

The guarantee covers the repeated replacement rule in Section~\ref{sec:likelihood-safeguard}. It follows from the deterministic certificate in Lemma~\ref{lem:iterative-swap-certification}, then the mandatory final M-step and Theorem~\ref{thm:local-spectral-em}. The original growing branch is retained as its initializer. A separate appendix result records the guarantee for that branch alone when $K=2$.
