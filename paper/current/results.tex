\section{Main results in logarithmic density}
\label{sec:main-results}

This section gives the exact contrast-preservation identity, the oracle benchmark, an abstract exponent-transfer theorem, and a local recovery theorem for the actual empirical spectral EM fit. It then establishes end-to-end recovery for the original two-community growing branch and for the complete general-$K$ likelihood-restart decoder. A separate corollary records a spectral warm-start alternative.

Throughout this section, $K$ is fixed, $n_a/n\to\pi_a>0$, and
$d_n\to\infty$ with $d_n/n\to0$. Unless stated otherwise,
$(n/d_n)P$ is a fixed symmetric nonsingular matrix whose entries lie in a
fixed compact subset of $(0,\infty)$. The logarithmic-density regime is
$d_n=\log n$. The probabilistic oracle calculations do not require
nonsingularity, but require distinct block rows when all communities are
to be classified.

\subsection{Spectral information and likelihood contrasts}

For communities $a\ne b$, define the block-constant Bernoulli contrast
and the nonedge offset by
\begin{align}
 w^{ab}_j
 &=\log\frac{p_{aj}(1-p_{bj})}{p_{bj}(1-p_{aj})},\label{eq:oracle-contrast}\\
 c_i^{ab}
 &=\sum_{j\ne i}\log\frac{1-p_{aj}}{1-p_{bj}}.
\end{align}
The oracle incident-edge log-likelihood ratio is
\begin{equation}
 L_i^{ab}=A_iw^{ab}+c_i^{ab},\label{eq:oracle-llr}
\end{equation}
where $A_i$ is row $i$ of $A$.  Its diagonal coordinate contributes zero.

\begin{proposition}[Exact preservation of retained contrasts]
\label{prop:exact-preservation}
Let $U$ contain any orthonormal collection of eigenvectors of the
symmetric matrix $A$, let $\Lambda$ contain their eigenvalues, and set
$\Pi=UU^\top$ and $\widetilde A=U\Lambda U^\top$.
Then $\widetilde A=A\Pi$ and, for every vector $w$,
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde A_iw-A_iw=A_i(\Pi-\mathrm{Id})w.\label{eq:preservation-error}
\end{equation}
In particular, $w\in\operatorname{col}(U)$ implies exact preservation.
Moreover, if $P$ is nonsingular, then the rank-$K$ comparison matrix
$ZPZ^\top$ has column space
$\operatorname{col}(Z)$, and every $w^{ab}$ belongs to this population
signal space.
\end{proposition}

The proposition explains the likelihood interpretation without requiring
the eigendecomposition itself to compute $\log P$.  The eigendecomposition
retains directions; the likelihood decoder assigns the appropriate
contrast coefficients to those directions.  It also states the precise
limitation: the empirical retained space need not equal the population
space.  The actual no-self-loop mean is
$\mathbb E[A\mid z]=ZPZ^\top-\operatorname{diag}(P_{z_i z_i})$;
the rank-$K$ statement concerns $ZPZ^\top$, not the no-self-loop mean.

The oracle Poisson contrast is
$v^{ab}_j=\log(p_{aj}/p_{bj})$, with score
\begin{equation}
 L_{i,\mathrm P}^{ab}
 =\sum_{j\ne i}A_{ij}v^{ab}_j
  -\sum_{j\ne i}(P_{a,z_j}-P_{b,z_j}).
 \label{eq:oracle-poisson}
\end{equation}
The following result makes its use in a sparse working score precise.

\begin{lemma}[Sparse Bernoulli-to-Poisson correction]
\label{lem:poisson-correction}
There is a constant $C$ depending only on the fixed normalized block probabilities such that, for
all sufficiently large $n$, every $i$, and every $a\ne b$,
\begin{equation}
 |L_i^{ab}-L_{i,\mathrm P}^{ab}|
 \le C\{(d_n/n)\deg(i)+d_n^2/n\}.\label{eq:poisson-error}
\end{equation}
For every fixed $H>0$ there is a constant $D_H$ such that
$\Pr\{\deg(i)>D_Hd_n\}\le e^{-Hd_n}$ uniformly in $i$.
Consequently, the correction is $o(d_n)$ outside an event of probability
$e^{-Hd_n}$.  In logarithmic density its bound on that event is
$O(\log^2 n/n)=o(1)$.
\end{lemma}

\subsection{The oracle benchmark}

We use Zhou--Li's profile and Chernoff notation. For $\alpha\in[0,1]$, define
\begin{align}
 D_\alpha(p_{a,-i}\Vert p_{b,-i})
 &=-\sum_{j\ne i}\log\!\left[
 p_{aj}^{1-\alpha}p_{bj}^{\alpha}
 +(1-p_{aj})^{1-\alpha}(1-p_{bj})^\alpha\right],\label{eq:chernoff-information}\\
 \alpha^*_{i,ab}
 &\in\argmax_{\alpha\in[0,1]}D_\alpha(p_{a,-i}\Vert p_{b,-i}),
 \qquad D^*_{i,ab}=D_{\alpha^*_{i,ab}}(p_{a,-i}\Vert p_{b,-i}),\\
 I_n&=\min_{i,\ a\ne b}D^*_{i,ab}.
 \label{eq:exact-information}
\end{align}
Here $p_{a,-i}$ omits the absent self-loop coordinate. The notation $I_n$ is our shorthand for the minimum exact pairwise information; it is not a second definition of a rate.

For distinct block rows under the fixed normalized probability model,
$I_n\asymp d_n$. In logarithmic density, $I_n/\log n$ converges to a
positive finite limit. Zhou and Li~\cite{zhouli2020} studied optimal error
rates for SBM community detection. We use their notation and the leading
information scale, without restating their refined rate.

\begin{lemma}[Leading oracle error exponent]
\label{lem:oracle-exponent}
Consider the binary experiment in which $z_i\in\{a,b\}$ has equal prior
probabilities and all other labels and $P$ are known.  Its Bayes risk
$R_{i,ab}^{\mathrm{or}}$ satisfies, uniformly in $i$,
\begin{equation}
 R_{i,ab}^{\mathrm{or}}=\exp\{-(1+o(1))D^*_{i,ab}\}.
 \label{eq:oracle-risk}
\end{equation}
The same leading exponent holds for fixed prior probabilities bounded
away from zero and one.
\end{lemma}

\subsection{Transfer to estimated spectral scores}

The theorem below accommodates scores estimated from the entire graph,
so it does not assume independence of a node's score and its incident
edges.  It makes the required rare-event control explicit.

\begin{theorem}[Likelihood-ratio exponent transfer]
\label{thm:exponent-transfer}
Let $\widehat\ell_i(a)$ be estimated community scores, after one common
alignment of their labels with the population labels, and classify by
$\widehat z_i\in\arg\max_a\widehat\ell_i(a)$.
Suppose there are $\tau_n=o(d_n)$ and $\delta_n\ge0$ such that, for every
$i$, an event $G_i$ satisfies
\begin{align}
 \Pr(G_i^c)&\le\delta_n,\label{eq:transfer-failure}\\
 \max_{a\ne b}
 |\widehat\ell_i(a)-\widehat\ell_i(b)-L_i^{ab}|
 &\le\tau_n\quad\text{on }G_i,\label{eq:transfer-score}\\
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{-\log\delta_n}{I_n}&\ge1,
 \label{eq:transfer-tail}
\end{align}
where $-\log0=+\infty$.
Then the permutation-invariant error fraction obeys
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb E\,\Mis(\widehat z,z)
 \le\exp\{-(1+o(1))I_n\}.
 \label{eq:conditional-rate}
\end{equation}
When $d_n=\log n$ and $\liminf_{n\to\infty}I_n/\log n>1$, this implies
$\Pr\{\Mis(\widehat z,z)>0\}\to0$.
\end{theorem}

The condition on $\delta_n$ is essential.  An error approximation that
holds with probability $1-o(1)$ can still fail much more frequently than
the oracle makes an error, and therefore need not preserve its exponent.
The theorem asserts an upper bound on the recovery error.  In the binary
oracle experiment, data processing and Lemma~\ref{lem:oracle-exponent}
also give the matching leading lower bound for any decoder using only
the graph's spectral summary.

For the working scores actually fitted in Section~2, set
$\widehat w^{ab}_j=\log(\widehat p_{aj}/\widehat p_{bj})$ and
$\widehat c^{ab}=-\sum_j(\widehat p_{aj}-\widehat p_{bj})
+\log(\widehat\pi_a/\widehat\pi_b)$.
Writing $q_i=\widetilde A_i+(q_i-\widetilde A_i)$ yields the exact
decomposition
\begin{align}
 \widehat\ell_i(a)-\widehat\ell_i(b)-L_i^{ab}
 ={}& A_i(\Pi-\mathrm{Id})w^{ab}\notag\\
 &+\widetilde A_i(\widehat w^{ab}-w^{ab})\notag\\
 &+(q_i-\widetilde A_i)\widehat w^{ab}
   +(\widehat c^{ab}-c_i^{ab}).\label{eq:four-errors}
\end{align}
Thus projection, estimated contrasts, clipping, and offsets must all be
controlled at the scale in Theorem~\ref{thm:exponent-transfer}.
The sum includes the reconstructed diagonal used by the implemented
working score; its discrepancy with the oracle's excluded diagonal is
included in the displayed decomposition.  Neither average reconstruction accuracy
nor objective convergence removes these terms.


\subsection{A local recovery theorem for the empirical spectral fit}
The next results remove the empirical-projection and parameter-fitting conditions from the preceding abstract transfer statement in logarithmic density. This local statement temporarily assumes reliable initial responsibilities. The end-to-end results below establish that assumption for the original two-community growing branch and for the complete Algorithm~1.

Write $N=Z^\top Z$ and $\Pi_*=ZN^{-1}Z^\top$. For a fixed $c_0>0$ satisfying $c_0<\min_{a,b}nP_{ab}/d_n$, let
\[
 q^0=\max\{A\Pi_*,c_0\frac{d_n}{n}\},\qquad
 q=\max\{U\Lambda U^\top,c_0\frac{d_n}{n}\},
\]
where the maximum is entrywise. The matrix $q^0$ is an analysis comparison using the true memberships; it is not an input to the algorithm.

\begin{lemma}[Full-graph spectral approximation]
\label{lem:full-spectral}
Under the fixed normalized full-rank probability model with $d_n=\log n$, for every fixed $H>0$ there are a constant $C_H$ and a deterministic sequence $a_n\to0$ such that, with probability at least $1-O(n^{-H})$,
\begin{align}
 \max_i\|q_i-q_i^0\|_1&\le a_nd_n,&
 c_0\frac{d_n}{n}\le q_{ij}&\le C_H\frac{d_n}{n},\label{eq:full-spectral-close}\\
 \frac1{nd_n}\sum_i\|q_i-p_{z_i,*}\|_1&\le a_n.
 \label{eq:average-profile-close}
\end{align}
The reconstruction uses the empirical eigenpairs of the whole graph. No row-independent replacement or extra leave-one-out computation is required by the algorithm.
\end{lemma}

The first-order entrywise eigenspace theorem of Abbe et al.~\citep{abbe2020}, together with the arbitrary polynomial-confidence spectral norm bound of Lei and Rinaldo~\citep{leirinaldo2015}, supplies the spectral inputs. The appendix verifies their assumptions for the present general full-rank SBM and derives the displayed row-profile bounds.

\begin{theorem}[Local empirical spectral EM attains the leading exponent]
\label{thm:local-spectral-em}
Use the model and admissible fixed floor of Lemma~\ref{lem:full-spectral}. Let the initial responsibilities $R^{(0)}=(R^{(0)}_{ia})$ have nonnegative rows summing to one. They may depend on the entire graph. Suppose that, after a common label alignment, for deterministic $\gamma_n\to0$ and $\delta_n\ge0$,
\begin{equation}
 \Pr\!\left\{\frac1n\sum_{i,a}|R^{(0)}_{ia}-Z_{ia}|>\gamma_n\right\}
 \le\delta_n,\qquad
 \liminf\frac{-\log\delta_n}{I_n}\ge1.
 \label{eq:local-initialization}
\end{equation}
Perform at least one responsibility-weighted profile M-step on $q$, followed by the score classification in \eqref{eq:decode}. Then
\begin{equation}
 \E\Mis(\widehat z,z)\le e^{-(1+o(1))I_n}.
 \label{eq:local-em-rate}
\end{equation}
The same bound holds after any further exact EM updates and any finite, possibly data-dependent stopping time after the first M-step. The weights are updated either unconstrained or by the exact fixed floored-simplex optimizer. If a component has zero responsibility mass, define its profile by any fixed positive fallback and use positive weights; on the weak-start event this case does not occur for large $n$. When $\liminf_{n\to\infty}I_n/\log n>1$, the output exactly recovers all labels with probability tending to one.
\end{theorem}

This theorem proves the statistical behavior of the actual full-graph spectral fitting stage from a sufficiently reliable weak start. It handles positive clipping and the freely fitted profiles rather than assuming that their LLR errors are already small. An initialization proof must establish \eqref{eq:local-initialization}: average weak consistency with an unspecified $1-o(1)$ probability is insufficient. Theorems~\ref{thm:two-community-growing} and~\ref{thm:certified-likelihood} provide the required reliability.

\begin{corollary}[A fully provable spectral warm-start variant]
\label{cor:provable-warmstart}
Replace the growing initialization by a fixed-factor approximate $K$-means clustering of the rows of $U$, computed with failure probability $O(n^{-H})$ for any prescribed fixed $H>\limsup_{n\to\infty}I_n/\log n$. Convert its labels to hard responsibilities and apply the profile EM decoder. This variant uses only the retained eigenpairs and satisfies \eqref{eq:local-em-rate}.
One polynomial-time implementation uses $O(\log n)$ independent $K$-means++ seedings and chooses the least squared-error solution.
\end{corollary}

This corollary is an alternative initialization. The main end-to-end result below uses likelihood residuals instead. The archived ten-start Lloyd baseline is distinct from either logarithmically amplified initialization.


\subsection{End-to-end recovery by likelihood initialization}
\label{sec:end-to-end}
The following results remove the weak-start assumption from the algorithmic guarantee.

\begin{theorem}[Original growing decoder for two communities]
\label{thm:two-community-growing}
Under the model and admissible floor of Lemma~\ref{lem:full-spectral}, suppose $K=2$. The original global-mean growing branch of Section~2.3, followed by at least one exact profile and weight M-step, satisfies
\[
 \E\Mis(\widehat z,z)\le e^{-(1+o(1))I_n}.
\]
This includes its evaluated finite stopping rule, since the first M-step is mandatory. When $\liminf_{n\to\infty}I_n/\log n>1$, it exactly recovers all labels with probability tending to one. No likelihood restarts or pure-candidate assumption are needed.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Complete likelihood decoder attains the leading exponent]
\label{thm:certified-likelihood}
Under the same model for every fixed $K\ge2$, Algorithm~1 satisfies
\[
 \E\Mis(\widehat z,z)\le e^{-(1+o(1))I_n}.
\]
When $\liminf_{n\to\infty}I_n/\log n>1$, it exactly recovers all labels with probability tending to one. The initialization, selection, and refinement access only the retained eigenpairs. Each candidate fit may run any prescribed finite number of exact EM updates, with exact unconstrained or floored-simplex weight updates; no convergence to a global mixture maximum is assumed. A polynomial iteration bound gives a polynomial-time algorithm.
\end{theorem}

The second theorem retains the original growing fit as a candidate and proves the likelihood-residual safeguard. It does not claim that the growing branch without that safeguard has been proved for general $K$. The restart count $\lceil\log n\rceil$ needs no knowledge of $P$, its separation constants, or the desired confidence power.

\subsection{A sufficient independent-row projection condition}

The next theorem proves a usable bridge between subspace estimation and
rare-event LLR accuracy.  It is a result about a row-independent
projection, not an unconditional guarantee for the full-graph fitted
algorithm.  Leave-one-out projections provide a natural setting for this
analysis.

\begin{theorem}[Row-independent spectral projection]
\label{thm:independent-projection}
Fix $i$ and restrict vectors and projectors to coordinates $j\ne i$.
Let $\Pi_i$ be a random orthogonal projector independent of
$(A_{ij})_{j\ne i}$, and let $\Pi_{*,i}$ project onto the membership space
of the other nodes.  Suppose that on an event $H_i$, measurable with
respect to the data generating $\Pi_i$,
\begin{equation}
 \|(\mathrm{Id}-\Pi_i)\Pi_{*,i}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\eta_n\to0,
 \qquad \max_{j\ne i}(\Pi_i)_{jj}\le\frac{L}{n},
 \label{eq:projection-conditions}
\end{equation}
for a constant $L$, and that
$\Pr(H_i^c)\le\gamma_n$.  Define
\begin{equation}
 L_{i,\Pi}^{ab}=A_{i,-i}\Pi_iw_{-i}^{ab}+c_i^{ab}.
\end{equation}
There are deterministic $\tau_n=o(d_n)$ and $h_n\to\infty$ such that
\begin{equation}
 \Pr\Big\{\max_{a\ne b}|L_{i,\Pi}^{ab}-L_i^{ab}|>\tau_n\Big\}
 \le\gamma_n+2K^2e^{-h_nd_n}.
 \label{eq:independent-projection-tail}
\end{equation}
Consequently, if
$\liminf(-\log\gamma_n)/I_n\ge1$ uniformly in $i$, classifiers based
on these oracle-parameter projected scores satisfy
\eqref{eq:conditional-rate}.
\end{theorem}

The proof uses a quadratic population bias and a Bennett tail bound.
It requires bounded projector leverage, rather than a vanishing maximum
coordinate error.  Its independence assumption is explicit: forming
$A_{i,-i}\Pi_i$ directly is not an additional step of our post-compression
algorithm.  To apply the theorem to that algorithm, one must further
control the difference between its empirical full-graph projection and
an independent-row construction, and then the remaining terms in
\eqref{eq:four-errors}.  Lemma~\ref{lem:full-spectral} and Theorem~\ref{thm:local-spectral-em} provide a different full-graph route in logarithmic density. The independent-row result remains useful for representations outside that setting.
