\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Spectral compression can preserve the likelihood information needed for nearly optimal community recovery. In a full-rank SBM, the membership subspace contains the block-constant likelihood contrasts. First-order analysis of the empirical adjacency eigenpairs makes this relation quantitative when the minimum expected degree is $\Omega(\log n)$, and an admissible spectral-scale positivity floor preserves the leading Chernoff exponent. Expected degrees can vary across communities, and the sparse block probabilities need not have a fixed normalized shape.

For every fixed $K\ge2$, Algorithm~1 combines global-mean growing initialization with deterministic repeated component replacement and a final EM update. The stopping certificate establishes weakly correct responsibilities, and the local refinement theorem gives
\[
 \mathbb E\,\Mis(\widehat z,z)\le e^{-(1+o(1))I_n}.
\]
Initialization and likelihood selection are part of the proof. The guarantee matches the leading oracle exponent and yields exact recovery above the corresponding logarithmic information threshold. The original single-pass growing procedure is a separate algorithm; its two-community guarantee is recorded in the appendix.

The paired 240-graph experiments evaluate the original growing and historical parameter-replacement procedures. They support the usefulness of all-node likelihood gains and likelihood-accepted repairs in resolving severe initialization failures. The new repeated-replacement algorithm has additional implementation checks, but a broad accuracy benchmark remains to be carried out. Reducing the cost of full profile reconstruction and establishing a general fixed-$K$ theorem for growing initialization alone are further questions.
