\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A full-rank SBM organizes its likelihood information in the membership subspace. First-order spectral linearization makes this observation quantitative for the empirical whole-graph reconstruction at logarithmic density: its row profiles approximate oracle block-count compression to $o(\log n)$ in $\ell_1$. A fixed admissible positive floor can be handled directly in the Chernoff moment calculation. Weak responsibilities then give sufficiently accurate fitted profiles, and the EM basin remains invariant through further updates.

These results prove the leading bound $e^{-(1+o(1))I_n}$ for the empirical spectral decoder from a sufficiently reliable weak start. A provable spectral warm-start variant gives an end-to-end corollary using only the retained eigenpairs. The proposed growing likelihood initializer avoids a subset-size parameter and performs well in the paired experiments, but its high-probability entrance into the weak basin is not proved here. Successful finite-sample initialization and monotone objective updates do not supply that probability bound.

The main remaining theoretical task in logarithmic density is therefore the growing initializer's entrance guarantee. Below logarithmic density, calibrated spectral concentration must also be established for the chosen regularization. The current dense profile implementation additionally needs computational improvement for larger networks. These are distinct extensions of the proved local decoding result.
