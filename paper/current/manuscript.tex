\begin{abstract}
Spectral clustering compresses a network into a small number of eigenpairs, whereas likelihood-ratio classification uses block-dependent logarithmic contrasts. We prove that a decoder using only the retained adjacency eigenpairs attains the leading Chernoff error exponent for every fixed community order $K$. We consider a stochastic block model with positive community proportions, block probabilities comparable to $p=\max_{a,b}P_{ab}$, and a uniformly conditioned $K$-dimensional signal. The density satisfies $p=\Omega(\log n/n)$ and $p=o(1)$. The procedure starts at the global reconstructed profile mean, grows components by their all-node likelihood gain, and jointly refits after each addition. It then repeats global-gain component replacements with uniformly initialized proposal weights until no likelihood gain reaches an explicit vanishing relative threshold. This deterministic stopping certificate ensures coverage of all $K$ true profiles and weakly consistent responsibilities. One final EM update and likelihood-ratio decoding give $\mathbb E\,\Mis(\widehat z,z)\le e^{-(1+o(1))I_n}$, without an assumed weak initializer. The proof controls the dependent full-graph spectral reconstruction at the information-scale failure probability and establishes an invariant local EM basin. Archived experiments on 240 graphs evaluate the growing branch and a historical single replacement pass; the repeated certified procedure has separate implementation checks.
\end{abstract}
