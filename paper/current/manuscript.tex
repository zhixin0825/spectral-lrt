\begin{abstract}
Spectral clustering compresses a network into a small number of eigenpairs, whereas likelihood-ratio classification uses block-dependent logarithmic contrasts. We study how to decode the latter information from the former representation. For a stochastic block model with a full-rank connectivity matrix, the population signal space contains every block-constant likelihood contrast. An exact projection identity explains why spectral compression can preserve the linear part of an oracle likelihood ratio. We propose a spectral-only procedure that selects an initial profile on a high-likelihood subset, adds profiles according to their joint gain on all nodes, and fits a positive-profile working mixture by alternating updates. We prove a conditional recovery theorem with error bound $e^{-(1+o(1))I_n}$, together with exact contrast-preservation, sparse likelihood approximation, greedy-selection, and objective-convergence results. The recovery theorem requires likelihood-score errors and their exceptional-event probabilities to be controlled at the oracle error scale; establishing these conditions for the complete empirical procedure remains unresolved. On two paired collections totaling 240 simulated graphs, global-gain seeding or likelihood-accepted parameter replacement eliminates two severe initialization failures and reduces mean misclassification relative to the tested spectral $k$-means baseline. We also formulate the lower-density extension and identify the additional calibration required by regularized spectral representations.
\end{abstract}
