\section{Spectral likelihood-ratio decoding}
\label{sec:algorithm}

\subsection{Model and retained information}
Let $A$ be the adjacency matrix of an undirected graph without self-loops. Conditional on community labels $z=(z_1,\ldots,z_n)\in[K]^n$, its upper-triangular entries are independent and
\begin{equation}
 A_{ij}\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(\rho_n B_{z_i z_j}),\quad i<j,
 \qquad A_{ji}=A_{ij},\quad A_{ii}=0.
\label{eq:sbm}
\end{equation}
The number $K$ is fixed and known. Write $Z_{ia}=\one\{z_i=a\}$, $n_a=\sum_i Z_{ia}$, and $\pi_{a,n}=n_a/n$. Unless specified otherwise, $B$ is a fixed symmetric full-rank matrix, $0<b_-\leq B_{ab}\leq b_+<\infty$, and $\pi_{a,n}\to\pi_a>0$. We begin with $\rho_n=\log n/n$ and put $d_n=n\rho_n$. Full rank need not imply positive definiteness; disassortative signal directions are retained.

The actual mean matrix and its rank-$K$ comparison are
\begin{equation}
 P=\E[A\mid z]=P^\circ-\diag(\rho_n B_{z_i z_i}),
 \qquad P^\circ=\rho_n ZBZ^\top.
\label{eq:mean}
\end{equation}
The small diagonal correction matters for exact identities. The representation consists of the $K$ empirical eigenpairs of $A$ with largest absolute eigenvalues:
\begin{equation}
 AU=U\Lambda,\qquad U^\top U=I_K,\qquad
 \widetilde P=U\Lambda U^\top.
\label{eq:representation}
\end{equation}
All subsequent fitting accesses only $(U,\Lambda)$. This restriction excludes an additional edge-based refinement. It also excludes computing separate leave-one-out eigendecompositions as an undeclared algorithmic step. In the logarithmic regime we use the adjacency eigenpairs without degree regularization or separately stored degrees.

\subsection{Positive profiles and a working likelihood}
We construct the positive profiles
\begin{equation}
 q_{ij}=\max\{\widetilde P_{ij},\varepsilon_n\},\qquad
 \varepsilon_n=10^{-4}\log n/n.
\label{eq:profiles}
\end{equation}
The evaluated implementation retains the reconstructed diagonal. For $\mu\in(0,\infty)^n$ define
\begin{equation}
 \ell_i(\mu)=\sum_{j=1}^n q_{ij}\log\mu_j-\sum_{j=1}^n\mu_j.
\label{eq:working_score}
\end{equation}
Terms independent of $\mu$ have been omitted. This convention fixes the first-seed subset scores: restoring observation-only Gamma constants can change which subset wins, even though it does not change mixture fitting or subsequent global gains. Although this is the algebraic form of a Poisson log likelihood, $q_{ij}$ are dependent, generally noninteger quantities. We therefore use it as a \emph{working} score, rather than assert that the spectral observations follow an independent Poisson model.

For profiles $\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_K$ and weights $\omega\in\Delta_K$, the working mixture objective is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L(\omega,\mu)=\sum_{i=1}^n\log\!\left(\sum_{a=1}^K\omega_a e^{\ell_i(\mu_a)}\right).
\label{eq:mixture}
\end{equation}
The final label is
\begin{equation}
 \widehat z_i=\argmax_{a\in[K]}\{\ell_i(\mu_a)+\log\omega_a\}.
\label{eq:decode}
\end{equation}
Thus classification uses pairwise working likelihood ratios, with estimated prior weights determining the thresholds. We evaluate the permutation-invariant mislabeled fraction
\begin{equation}
 r(\widehat z,z)=\min_{\sigma\in\mathfrak S_K}\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\one\{\sigma(\widehat z_i)\ne z_i\},
\end{equation}
where $\mathfrak S_K$ is the set of label permutations. The unrestricted profiles are $n$-dimensional weighted means; the implementation does not impose the exact SBM constraint $\mu_{aj}=\rho_n B_{a,z_j}$.

\subsection{Subset-first, global-gain initialization}
Every observed profile $q_c$ is a candidate parameter. The first seed uses a restricted-subset score. Let
\begin{equation}
 m=\min\left\{\left\lfloor\frac{n}{1.5K}\right\rfloor,n-K\right\},
 \qquad T_c\subset[n]\setminus\{c\},\quad |T_c|=m,
\label{eq:seed_subset}
\end{equation}
where $T_c$ contains the nodes having largest values of $\ell_i(q_c)$. Choose
\begin{equation}
 c_1\in\argmax_c\sum_{i\in T_c}\ell_i(q_c),\qquad \mu_1=q_{c_1}.
\label{eq:first_seed}
\end{equation}
This step looks for a candidate that fits a substantial subset rather than compromises across the whole graph. The candidate itself is excluded from its evaluation subset. Ties are broken deterministically.

Subsequent steps keep every node and assess how much a new profile improves their joint explanation. With $h_i^{(t-1)}=\max_{a<t}\ell_i(\mu_a)$, choose
\begin{equation}
 c_t\in\argmax_c\Delta(c\mid\mu_{1:t-1}),\qquad
 \Delta(c\mid\mu_{1:t-1})=\sum_i\bigl(\ell_i(q_c)-h_i^{(t-1)}\bigr)_+,
 \qquad \mu_t=q_{c_t}.
\label{eq:gain}
\end{equation}
No subset-size constraint is imposed after the first seed, and no nodes are removed. Selecting a profile already represented in the current set contributes zero gain. A mixed profile can still have positive gain, so this rule does not guarantee that every seed is a community center. Its purpose is to make repeated coverage compete against the improvement available in omitted regions.

This is a deterministic likelihood-gain analogue of the residual-coverage principle behind $k$-means++ \citep{arthur2007}. Unlike $D^2$ sampling, it selects the largest gain rather than sampling proportionally to a distance. Proposition~\ref{prop:greedy-gain} proves a standard submodular guarantee relative to the fixed first seed, and Proposition~\ref{prop:noiseless-seeding} proves complete coverage in the noiseless positive-profile model.

\subsection{Alternating fitting and parameter replacement}
After $K$ seeds have been selected, initialize $\omega_a=1/K$ and perform soft updates
\begin{align}
 \tau_{ia}&=\frac{\omega_a e^{\ell_i(\mu_a)}}{\sum_b\omega_b e^{\ell_i(\mu_b)}},
 &N_a&=\sum_i\tau_{ia},\label{eq:responsibilities}\\
 \mu_{aj}^{\mathrm{new}}&=\frac{\sum_i\tau_{ia}q_{ij}}{N_a},
 &\omega_a^{\mathrm{new}}&=N_a/n.
\label{eq:mstep}
\end{align}
The responsibilities are computed using log-sum-exp for numerical stability. Positive $q$ ensures that positive-weight components have positive updated means. The evaluated implementation constrains $\omega_a\geq10^{-8}$ and replaces the unconstrained weight update in \eqref{eq:mstep} by the exact optimizer of $\sum_aN_a\log\omega_a$ on that floored simplex. If no floor is active, this reduces to $N_a/n$. Both exact versions maximize the variational M-step, giving objective monotonicity and boundedness. Proposition~\ref{prop:em-monotonicity} proves objective convergence; it does not prove global optimality or correct community recovery.

\begin{center}
\fbox{\begin{minipage}{0.93\linewidth}
\textbf{Algorithm 1: Spectral-only likelihood-gain decoding}\smallskip
\begin{enumerate}
\item Input $K$ and the retained eigenpairs $(U,\Lambda)$; form $q$ by \eqref{eq:profiles}.
\item Select the first profile by \eqref{eq:first_seed}, with $m=\lfloor n/(1.5K)\rfloor$.
\item For $t=2,\ldots,K$, add the candidate maximizing \eqref{eq:gain} on all $n$ nodes, keeping the previously selected profiles fixed.
\item Run the joint updates \eqref{eq:responsibilities}--\eqref{eq:mstep} to the declared objective tolerance or iteration limit.
\item Output the labels in \eqref{eq:decode}, the fitted profiles, weights, and objective trace.
\end{enumerate}
\end{minipage}}
\end{center}

We also evaluate an alternative stabilization of the earlier partial-peeling initialization. After fitting, remove each component in turn; choose a replacement profile by its global gain relative to the remaining fitted profiles, and run a fresh fit. Accept the new fit only if its final working mixture objective increases. The evaluated replacement pass tests the $K$ possible component removals and selects an improving trial. This is an alternative branch with additional fitting cost; the results below do not assert that the combination of Algorithm~1 and replacement was evaluated.

The numerical experiments use the exact floored-simplex weight update, finite stopping tolerances, and an iteration cap. Objective convergence in exact arithmetic does not replace checking the finite-run stopping rule or floating-point error. We report those numerical checks separately.

\subsection{Why the representation can retain an LLR}
Suppose temporarily that $B$ and the other labels are known. For testing $z_i=a$ against $z_i=b$, define, for $j\ne i$,
\begin{equation}
 w_j^{ab}=\log\frac{B_{a,z_j}(1-\rho_n B_{b,z_j})}{B_{b,z_j}(1-\rho_n B_{a,z_j})},
 \qquad c_i^{ab}=\sum_{j\ne i}\log\frac{1-\rho_n B_{a,z_j}}{1-\rho_n B_{b,z_j}}.
\label{eq:oracle_weights}
\end{equation}
The oracle Bernoulli LLR is $L_i^{ab}=\sum_{j\ne i}A_{ij}w_j^{ab}+c_i^{ab}$. Extend $w_i^{ab}$ by the same block formula. Because $A_{ii}=0$, this extension does not change the oracle linear statistic. The contrast vector is block constant, hence belongs to $\col(Z)$.

The fundamental identity is
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde P=A\Pi,\qquad \Pi=UU^\top,
 \qquad (\widetilde P_i-A_i)w^{ab}=A_i(\Pi-I)w^{ab}.
\label{eq:projection_identity}
\end{equation}
Here $I$ denotes the identity matrix. If $w^{ab}$ lies in the retained empirical space, the linear statistic is preserved exactly. At the population level, full rank gives $\col(P^\circ)=\col(Z)$, so every contrast has the required span membership. The spectral decomposition need not itself optimize a likelihood: it retains a linear space containing the directions in which the likelihood acts.

The executable score additionally involves clipping, estimated profiles, mixture thresholds, and a retained reconstructed diagonal. These perturbations require statistical control. In particular, neither low-rank reconstruction error nor objective convergence alone establishes that \eqref{eq:decode} achieves the oracle error exponent. Section~\ref{sec:main-results} states an explicit sufficient theorem for that conclusion.

\subsection{Computational cost}
Forming the full positive reconstruction stores $O(n^2)$ numbers. Caching all candidate scores uses an $n$-by-$n$ matrix and, by direct multiplication, $O(n^3)$ arithmetic. Each subsequent greedy scan costs $O(n^2)$, and one EM iteration costs $O(Kn^2)$. These costs are additional to computing the eigenpairs. The implementation is practical at the tested $n\leq2000$ but does not yet justify a large-network speed claim. Candidate subsampling and blocked score evaluation are natural approximations, whose statistical and computational performance require separate validation.
