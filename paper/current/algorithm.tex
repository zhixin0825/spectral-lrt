\section{Spectral likelihood-ratio decoding}
\label{sec:algorithm}

\subsection{Model and retained information}
Let $A$ be the adjacency matrix of an undirected graph without self-loops. Conditional on community labels $z=(z_1,\ldots,z_n)\in[K]^n$, its upper-triangular entries are independent and
\begin{equation}
 A_{ij}\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(P_{z_i z_j}),\quad i<j,
 \qquad A_{ji}=A_{ij},\quad A_{ii}=0.
\label{eq:sbm}
\end{equation}
We follow the SBM notation of Zhou and Li~\citep{zhouli2020}: $P=P_n\in(0,1)^{K\times K}$ is the symmetric block probability matrix, $p_{aj}=P_{a,z_j}$, and $p_{a*}$ is the connection profile of community $a$. The known number $K\ge2$ is any fixed integer. Write $Z_{ia}=\one\{z_i=a\}$, $N=Z^\top Z=\diag(n_a)$, and $n_a/n\to\pi_a>0$. Define $P_{\min}=\min_{a,b}P_{ab}$ and $P_{\max}=\max_{a,b}P_{ab}$. The main results assume, for fixed positive constants $c_r,c_s,c_d$,
\begin{equation}
 P_{\min}\ge c_rP_{\max},\qquad P_{\max}\to0,\qquad
 \sigma_{\min}(N^{1/2}PN^{1/2})\ge c_s nP_{\max},
 \label{eq:model-conditions}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
 \min_i\E[\deg(i)\mid z]\ge c_d\log n,\qquad
 \E[\deg(i)\mid z]=\sum_{b=1}^K(n_b-\one\{z_i=b\})P_{z_i b}.
 \label{eq:degree-condition}
\end{equation}
The density condition is a lower bound. Expected degrees may differ across communities and may grow faster than $\log n$; neither $P$ nor its normalized shape is fixed. The conditioning assumption in \eqref{eq:model-conditions} supplies the signal separation needed by the retained $K$-dimensional representation. It permits negative signal eigenvalues.

The $n\times n$ mean matrix differs from the rank-$K$ matrix $ZPZ^\top$ only on its diagonal:
\begin{equation}
 \E[A\mid z]=ZPZ^\top-\diag(P_{z_i z_i}).
\label{eq:mean}
\end{equation}
The small diagonal correction matters for exact identities. The representation consists of the $K$ empirical eigenpairs of $A$ with largest absolute eigenvalues:
\begin{equation}
 AU=U\Lambda,\qquad U^\top U=I_K,\qquad
 \widetilde A=U\Lambda U^\top.
\label{eq:representation}
\end{equation}
All subsequent fitting accesses only $(U,\Lambda)$. This restriction excludes an additional edge-based refinement. It also excludes computing separate leave-one-out eigendecompositions as an undeclared algorithmic step. The representation is computed from the original adjacency matrix.

\subsection{Spectral profile scores}
A signed low-rank reconstruction can contain nonpositive entries. To define the logarithms used in candidate scoring, set
\begin{equation}
 q_{ij}=\max\{\widetilde A_{ij},\varepsilon_n\},\qquad
 \varepsilon_n=\frac{c_0}{n}\max\{\|\Lambda\|_{\mathrm{op}},1\},\qquad 0<c_0<c_r/2.
\label{eq:profiles}
\end{equation}
The floor is computed from the retained eigenvalues. On the spectral event in Lemma~\ref{lem:full-spectral}, it is a fixed positive fraction of $P_{\max}$ and lies below all true block probabilities. It makes the profile logarithms well defined. The adjacency eigendecomposition in \eqref{eq:representation} uses $A$ as given. The reconstruction retains its diagonal. For a candidate profile $\widehat p\in(0,\infty)^n$, define
\begin{equation}
 \ell_i(\widehat p)=\sum_{j=1}^n q_{ij}\log\widehat p_j-\sum_{j=1}^n\widehat p_j.
\label{eq:working_score}
\end{equation}
The score is motivated by the first-order sparse expansion of a Bernoulli log likelihood: $\log(1-p)=-p+O(p^2)$. Lemma~\ref{lem:sparse-expansion} controls the resulting pairwise approximation. Because $q$ is a dependent spectral reconstruction, \eqref{eq:working_score} defines a working fitting criterion. It is not the exact likelihood of the original graph. Terms depending only on $q_i$ do not affect candidate comparisons or fitting.

For profiles $\widehat p_1,\ldots,\widehat p_K$ and weights $\widehat\pi\in\Delta_K$, the working mixture objective is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L(\widehat\pi,\widehat p)=\sum_{i=1}^n\log\!\left(\sum_{a=1}^K\widehat\pi_a e^{\ell_i(\widehat p_a)}\right).
\label{eq:mixture}
\end{equation}
The final label is
\begin{equation}
 \widehat z_i=\argmax_{a\in[K]}\{\ell_i(\widehat p_a)+\log\widehat\pi_a\}.
\label{eq:decode}
\end{equation}
Thus classification uses pairwise working likelihood ratios, with estimated prior weights determining the thresholds. We evaluate the permutation-invariant mislabeled fraction
\begin{equation}
 \Mis(\widehat z,z)=\min_{\sigma\in\mathfrak S_K}\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\one\{\sigma(\widehat z_i)\ne z_i\},
\end{equation}
where $\mathfrak S_K$ is the set of label permutations. The fitted profiles $\widehat p_{a*}$ are responsibility-weighted means of the observed profiles; the implementation does not impose the exact SBM constraint $\widehat p_{aj}=P_{a,z_j}$. Although these profiles have $n$ coordinates, every M-step keeps them in the row convex hull of $q$; Proposition~\ref{prop:profile-hull} records this restriction.

\subsection{Global-mean start and growing likelihood gains}
\label{sec:growing-branch}
The procedure starts with the global profile mean,
\begin{equation}
 \widehat p^{(1)}_1=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n q_i,\qquad \widehat\pi^{(1)}_1=1.
 \label{eq:first_seed}
\end{equation}
This is the fitted one-component solution, rather than a node chosen using a top-fraction subset. It requires no subset-size parameter and removes no observations.

At stage $t=2,\ldots,K$, let $\widehat p^{(t-1)}_1,\ldots,\widehat p^{(t-1)}_{t-1}$ be the profiles fitted at the preceding stage and set
$h_i^{(t-1)}=\max_{a<t}\ell_i(\widehat p^{(t-1)}_a)$.
For a node candidate $c$ not previously added, define
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_t(c)=\sum_{i=1}^n\bigl(\ell_i(q_c)-h_i^{(t-1)}\bigr)_+,
 \qquad c_t\in\argmax_c\Delta_t(c).
 \label{eq:gain}
\end{equation}
Append $q_{c_t}$ to the current $t-1$ fitted profiles, initialize the $t$ weights uniformly, and fit all $t$ profiles jointly on all $n$ observations by the updates below. The fitted endpoint supplies the next stage's profiles. A global mean counts as the first component, so only $K-1$ node candidates are added. Previously chosen node IDs are excluded from subsequent candidate scans, and ties are broken deterministically.

The all-node gain rewards profiles that improve the fit to observations. The growing endpoint initializes the following fixed-$K$ replacement procedure. Its guarantee is established by a stopping certificate for the common objective.

\subsection{Repeated global-gain replacement}
\label{sec:likelihood-safeguard}
Define the profile divergence
\begin{equation}
 D(x\Vert y)=\sum_j\{x_j\log(x_j/y_j)-x_j+y_j\},\qquad
 T_n=\sum_{i,j}q_{ij}.
 \label{eq:repair-threshold}
\end{equation}
The divergence equals the score loss $\ell_x(x)-\ell_x(y)$. Starting at the $K$-component growing endpoint, evaluate these proposals in each round:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Refit the current profiles with weights initialized at $1/K$.
\item For each component $r$, remove it and choose a row $q_{c_r}$ maximizing
\[
 \sum_i\left(\ell_i(q_c)-\max_{a\ne r}\ell_i(\widehat p_a)\right)_+.
\]
Replace profile $r$ by $q_{c_r}$, initialize all weights at $1/K$, and jointly refit. The candidate scan includes all $n$ rows, including previously selected nodes.
\end{enumerate}
Each proposal may use any prescribed finite number of exact EM updates, including zero. Compare all endpoints and the current fit by the same $\mathcal L$. Accept the best proposal if its increase is at least $T_n/\sqrt{\log n}$, and repeat. Stop if no proposal attains that increase. For a deterministic iteration limit, stop after $\lceil(\log n)^2\rceil$ accepted rounds. This cap is eventually inactive on the spectral event.

At the chosen endpoint, compute the E-step responsibilities and perform one additional profile and weight M-step before decoding. A zero-mass component retains its previous profile. The extra uniform-weight proposal and uniform starts for replacements are part of the certificate; they prevent fitted component weights from hiding a poorly covered population profile.

Theorem~\ref{thm:certified-likelihood} proves this deterministic procedure for every fixed $K$. The key observation is that with $K$ true profiles and $K$ fitted components, a missing true profile can improve a one-component replacement: either its nearest component serves only that profile, or another component is unused by all nearest-profile assignments. Rejection of all global-gain replacements consequently forces coverage of every true profile. The proof controls the soft mixture objective as well as the hard profile divergence.

\subsection{Alternating fitting and parameter replacement}
At a stage with $s$ components, initialize $\widehat\pi_a=1/s$ and perform soft updates (replace $K$ by $s$ in the displayed sums)
\begin{align}
 \tau_{ia}&=\frac{\widehat\pi_a e^{\ell_i(\widehat p_a)}}{\sum_b\widehat\pi_b e^{\ell_i(\widehat p_b)}},
 &N_a&=\sum_i\tau_{ia},\label{eq:responsibilities}\\
 \widehat p_{aj}^{\mathrm{new}}&=\frac{\sum_i\tau_{ia}q_{ij}}{N_a},
 &\widehat\pi_a^{\mathrm{new}}&=N_a/n.
\label{eq:mstep}
\end{align}
The responsibilities are computed using log-sum-exp for numerical stability. Positive $q$ ensures that positive-weight components have positive updated means. An optional fixed weight floor $f\in[0,1/K)$ replaces the unconstrained weight update in \eqref{eq:mstep} by the exact optimizer of $\sum_aN_a\log\widehat\pi_a$ on that floored simplex. If no floor is active, this reduces to $N_a/n$. The tested floor is given in Appendix~\ref{app:implementation}. Both exact versions maximize the variational M-step, giving objective monotonicity and boundedness within that stage. Proposition~\ref{prop:em-monotonicity} proves convergence of the objective within each fit; it does not prove global optimality or correct community recovery. Because the implementation reinitializes weights when it adds a component, we do not assert monotonicity between stages.

\begin{center}
\fbox{\begin{minipage}{0.93\linewidth}
\textbf{Algorithm 1: Spectral likelihood decoding with repeated replacement}\smallskip
\begin{enumerate}
\item Input $K$ and the retained eigenpairs $(U,\Lambda)$; form $q$ by \eqref{eq:profiles}.
\item Obtain the global-mean growing fit by \eqref{eq:first_seed}, \eqref{eq:gain}, and the joint EM updates.
\item Evaluate the uniform-weight refit and all $K$ global-gain replacements described in Section~\ref{sec:likelihood-safeguard}.
\item Accept the best proposal if its likelihood gain is at least $T_n/\sqrt{\log n}$ and return to step 3. Stop on rejection or after $\lceil(\log n)^2\rceil$ accepted rounds.
\item Compute one E-step and one profile and weight M-step. Decode by \eqref{eq:decode}, or continue exact EM before decoding.
\item Output labels, fitted profiles, weights, and the recorded objectives.
\end{enumerate}
\end{minipage}}
\end{center}

The archived experiments evaluate the original growing branch and a historical single replacement pass after partial peeling. The repeated procedure in Algorithm~1 additionally resets proposal weights uniformly, evaluates a uniform-weight refit, and iterates to the specified gain threshold. Its implementation checks are reported separately.

The numerical experiments use the exact floored-simplex weight update, finite stopping tolerances, and an iteration cap. Objective convergence in exact arithmetic does not replace checking the finite-run stopping rule or floating-point error. We report those numerical checks separately.

\subsection{Why the representation can retain an LLR}
Suppose temporarily that $P$ and the other labels are known. For testing $z_i=a$ against $z_i=b$, define, for $j\ne i$,
\begin{equation}
 w_j^{ab}=\log\frac{p_{aj}(1-p_{bj})}{p_{bj}(1-p_{aj})},
 \qquad c_i^{ab}=\sum_{j\ne i}\log\frac{1-p_{aj}}{1-p_{bj}}.
\label{eq:oracle_weights}
\end{equation}
The oracle Bernoulli LLR is $L_i^{ab}=\sum_{j\ne i}A_{ij}w_j^{ab}+c_i^{ab}$. Extend $w_i^{ab}$ by the same block formula. Because $A_{ii}=0$, this extension does not change the oracle linear statistic. The contrast vector is block constant, hence belongs to $\col(Z)$.

The fundamental identity is
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde A=A\Pi,\qquad \Pi=UU^\top,
 \qquad (\widetilde A_i-A_i)w^{ab}=A_i(\Pi-\mathrm{Id})w^{ab}.
\label{eq:projection_identity}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathrm{Id}$ denotes the identity matrix. If $w^{ab}$ lies in the retained empirical space, the linear statistic is preserved exactly. At the population level, full rank gives $\col(ZPZ^\top)=\col(Z)$, so every contrast has the required span membership. The spectral decomposition need not itself optimize a likelihood: it retains a linear space containing the directions in which the likelihood acts.

The executable score additionally involves clipping, estimated profiles, mixture thresholds, and a retained reconstructed diagonal. These perturbations require statistical control. A matrix reconstruction bound alone does not establish the oracle exponent; the local fitting theorem uses the stronger row-profile approximation, the clipping moment bound, and a weak-initialization condition. Theorem~\ref{thm:local-spectral-em} supplies the fitting guarantee. Theorem~\ref{thm:certified-likelihood} establishes its initialization condition for Algorithm~1 for every fixed $K$.

\subsection{Computational cost}
Forming the full positive reconstruction stores $O(n^2)$ numbers. Caching all candidate scores uses an $n$-by-$n$ matrix and, by direct multiplication, $O(n^3)$ arithmetic. Each subsequent greedy scan costs $O(n^2)$, and one EM iteration costs $O(Kn^2)$. Algorithm~1 accepts $O(\sqrt{\log n})$ replacement rounds on the spectral event and has a deterministic $\lceil(\log n)^2\rceil$ cap. Each round evaluates $K+1$ fits and $K$ candidate scans, so a prescribed polynomial EM iteration bound gives polynomial total cost. These costs are additional to computing the eigenpairs. The implementation is practical at the tested $n\leq2000$ but does not yet justify a large-network speed claim. Candidate subsampling and blocked score evaluation are natural approximations, whose statistical and computational performance require separate validation.
