\section{Spectral likelihood-ratio decoding}
\label{sec:algorithm}

\subsection{Model and retained information}
Let $A$ be the adjacency matrix of an undirected graph without self-loops. Conditional on community labels $z=(z_1,\ldots,z_n)\in[K]^n$, its upper-triangular entries are independent and
\begin{equation}
 A_{ij}\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(P_{z_i z_j}),\quad i<j,
 \qquad A_{ji}=A_{ij},\quad A_{ii}=0.
\label{eq:sbm}
\end{equation}
We follow the SBM notation of Zhou and Li~\citep{zhouli2020}: $P\in(0,1)^{K\times K}$ is the block probability matrix, $p_{kj}=P_{k,z_j}$, and $p_{k*}=(p_{k1},\ldots,p_{kn})$ is the connection profile of community $k$. We parameterize $P=\rho_n B$ to separate density from a fixed connectivity shape. The number $K\ge2$ is fixed and known. Write $Z_{ia}=\one\{z_i=a\}$, $n_a=\sum_i Z_{ia}$, and $\pi_{a,n}=n_a/n$. Unless specified otherwise, $B$ is a fixed symmetric full-rank matrix, $0<b_-\leq B_{ab}\leq b_+<\infty$, and $\pi_{a,n}\to\pi_a>0$. We begin with $\rho_n=\log n/n$ and put $d_n=n\rho_n$. Full rank need not imply positive definiteness; disassortative signal directions are retained.

We reserve $P$ for the $K\times K$ block probabilities. The $n\times n$ mean matrix and its rank-$K$ comparison are
\begin{equation}
 \Omega=\E[A\mid z]=\Omega^\circ-\diag(\rho_n B_{z_i z_i}),
 \qquad \Omega^\circ=ZPZ^\top.
\label{eq:mean}
\end{equation}
The small diagonal correction matters for exact identities. The representation consists of the $K$ empirical eigenpairs of $A$ with largest absolute eigenvalues:
\begin{equation}
 AU=U\Lambda,\qquad U^\top U=I_K,\qquad
 \widetilde A=U\Lambda U^\top.
\label{eq:representation}
\end{equation}
All subsequent fitting accesses only $(U,\Lambda)$. This restriction excludes an additional edge-based refinement. It also excludes computing separate leave-one-out eigendecompositions as an undeclared algorithmic step. In the logarithmic regime we use the adjacency eigenpairs without degree regularization or separately stored degrees.

\subsection{Positive profiles and a working likelihood}
We construct the positive profiles
\begin{equation}
 q_{ij}=\max\{\widetilde A_{ij},\varepsilon_n\},\qquad
 \varepsilon_n=c_0\rho_n,\qquad c_0=10^{-4}.
\label{eq:profiles}
\end{equation}
The evaluated implementation retains the reconstructed diagonal. The logarithmic-density local recovery results assume $c_0<b_-$; the raw oracle results do not need this additional floor margin. For $\widehat p\in(0,\infty)^n$ define
\begin{equation}
 \ell_i(\widehat p)=\sum_{j=1}^n q_{ij}\log\widehat p_j-\sum_{j=1}^n\widehat p_j.
\label{eq:working_score}
\end{equation}
Observation-only terms, including Gamma constants, have been omitted. They cancel from the all-node gains and responsibility comparisons and do not change the mixture maximizer. Although this is the algebraic form of a Poisson log likelihood, $q_{ij}$ are dependent, generally noninteger quantities. We therefore use it as a \emph{working} score, rather than assert that the spectral observations follow an independent Poisson model.

For profiles $\widehat p_1,\ldots,\widehat p_K$ and weights $\widehat\pi\in\Delta_K$, the working mixture objective is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L(\widehat\pi,\widehat p)=\sum_{i=1}^n\log\!\left(\sum_{a=1}^K\widehat\pi_a e^{\ell_i(\widehat p_a)}\right).
\label{eq:mixture}
\end{equation}
The final label is
\begin{equation}
 \widehat z_i=\argmax_{a\in[K]}\{\ell_i(\widehat p_a)+\log\widehat\pi_a\}.
\label{eq:decode}
\end{equation}
Thus classification uses pairwise working likelihood ratios, with estimated prior weights determining the thresholds. We evaluate the permutation-invariant mislabeled fraction
\begin{equation}
 \Mis(\widehat z,z)=\min_{\sigma\in\mathfrak S_K}\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\one\{\sigma(\widehat z_i)\ne z_i\},
\end{equation}
where $\mathfrak S_K$ is the set of label permutations. The fitted profiles $\widehat p_{a*}$ are responsibility-weighted means of the observed profiles; the implementation does not impose the exact SBM constraint $\widehat p_{aj}=P_{a,z_j}$. Although these profiles have $n$ coordinates, every M-step keeps them in the row convex hull of $q$; Proposition~\ref{prop:profile-hull} records this restriction.

\subsection{Global-mean start and growing likelihood gains}
\label{sec:growing-branch}
The procedure starts with the global profile mean,
\begin{equation}
 \widehat p^{(1)}_1=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n q_i,\qquad \widehat\pi^{(1)}_1=1.
 \label{eq:first_seed}
\end{equation}
This is the fitted one-component solution, rather than a node chosen using a top-fraction subset. It requires no subset-size parameter and removes no observations.

At stage $t=2,\ldots,K$, let $\widehat p^{(t-1)}_1,\ldots,\widehat p^{(t-1)}_{t-1}$ be the profiles fitted at the preceding stage and set
$h_i^{(t-1)}=\max_{a<t}\ell_i(\widehat p^{(t-1)}_a)$.
For a node candidate $c$ not previously added, define
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_t(c)=\sum_{i=1}^n\bigl(\ell_i(q_c)-h_i^{(t-1)}\bigr)_+,
 \qquad c_t\in\argmax_c\Delta_t(c).
 \label{eq:gain}
\end{equation}
Append $q_{c_t}$ to the current $t-1$ fitted profiles, initialize the $t$ weights uniformly, and fit all $t$ profiles jointly on all $n$ observations by the updates below. The fitted endpoint supplies the next stage's profiles. A global mean counts as the first component, so only $K-1$ node candidates are added. Previously chosen node IDs are excluded from subsequent candidate scans, and ties are broken deterministically.

The all-node gain rewards a candidate for explaining evidence not already well fitted. This is a deterministic analogue of the coverage principle behind $k$-means++~\citep{arthur2007}, with likelihood gains replacing squared Euclidean distances. It does not guarantee that a selected node is a population community center.

For fixed profiles, Proposition~\ref{prop:greedy-gain} gives a submodular greedy bound and Proposition~\ref{prop:noiseless-seeding} gives noiseless coverage from a node-profile first seed. They describe the frozen-profile control, not the complete growing algorithm: the latter starts from a mean and changes its profiles between additions. The original empirical primary branch is the growing procedure specified here. It supplies one candidate fit for the complete decoder below; the additional likelihood restarts make the general-$K$ guarantee independent of its basin dynamics.


\subsection{Likelihood-residual restarts and the complete decoder}
\label{sec:likelihood-safeguard}
For general $K$, we supplement the growing fit with $L_n=\lceil\log n\rceil$ independent likelihood-residual initializations. Define the Poisson deviance
\[
 D_{\mathrm P}(x\Vert y)=\sum_j\{x_j\log(x_j/y_j)-x_j+y_j\}.
\]
Each restart chooses its first node uniformly. Given a set $C$ of selected nodes, it chooses the next node with probability proportional to
\[
 r_i(C)=\min_{c\in C}D_{\mathrm P}(q_i\Vert q_c).
\]
Already selected nodes have zero sampling mass. If all residuals vanish, choose an unselected node uniformly. Select all $K$ seeds before fitting; initialize the profiles at their $q$ rows and weights at $1/K$, and run the same exact joint EM updates as for the growing fit. The random streams are independent conditional on $q$.

Compare the endpoints of these $L_n$ fits and the original growing fit using exactly the same objective $\mathcal L$ in \eqref{eq:mixture}. Keep the largest value, compute its E-step responsibilities, perform one additional profile and weight M-step, and decode. This final update is part of the algorithm, even when an earlier fit stopped at its iteration cap. Further exact updates are optional. All profiles remain convex combinations of the same positive $q$ rows; a zero-mass component retains its previous profile.

This is a likelihood-based restart safeguard, not a Euclidean warm start. It is an explicit addition to the original growing branch. Theorem~\ref{thm:certified-likelihood} proves the complete general-$K$ algorithm without an initialization assumption. For $K=2$, Theorem~\ref{thm:two-community-growing} shows that the growing branch alone already has the target guarantee.

\subsection{Alternating fitting and parameter replacement}
At a stage with $s$ components, initialize $\widehat\pi_a=1/s$ and perform soft updates (replace $K$ by $s$ in the displayed sums)
\begin{align}
 \tau_{ia}&=\frac{\widehat\pi_a e^{\ell_i(\widehat p_a)}}{\sum_b\widehat\pi_b e^{\ell_i(\widehat p_b)}},
 &N_a&=\sum_i\tau_{ia},\label{eq:responsibilities}\\
 \widehat p_{aj}^{\mathrm{new}}&=\frac{\sum_i\tau_{ia}q_{ij}}{N_a},
 &\widehat\pi_a^{\mathrm{new}}&=N_a/n.
\label{eq:mstep}
\end{align}
The responsibilities are computed using log-sum-exp for numerical stability. Positive $q$ ensures that positive-weight components have positive updated means. The evaluated implementation constrains $\widehat\pi_a\geq10^{-8}$ and replaces the unconstrained weight update in \eqref{eq:mstep} by the exact optimizer of $\sum_aN_a\log\widehat\pi_a$ on that floored simplex. If no floor is active, this reduces to $N_a/n$. Both exact versions maximize the variational M-step, giving objective monotonicity and boundedness within that stage. Proposition~\ref{prop:em-monotonicity} proves convergence of the objective within each fit; it does not prove global optimality or correct community recovery. Because the implementation reinitializes weights when it adds a component, we do not assert monotonicity between stages.

\begin{center}
\fbox{\begin{minipage}{0.93\linewidth}
\textbf{Algorithm 1: Spectral likelihood decoding with a restart safeguard}\smallskip
\begin{enumerate}
\item Input $K$ and the retained eigenpairs $(U,\Lambda)$; form $q$ by \eqref{eq:profiles}.
\item Obtain the global-mean growing fit by \eqref{eq:first_seed}, \eqref{eq:gain}, and the joint EM updates.
\item Independently run $L_n=\lceil\log n\rceil$ likelihood-residual initializations as in Section~\ref{sec:likelihood-safeguard}; select all $K$ seeds before each joint fit.
\item Select the largest final $\mathcal L$ across the growing fit and all restarts.
\item Compute one E-step and one profile and weight M-step on the selected fit. Decode by \eqref{eq:decode}, or continue exact EM before decoding.
\item Output labels, fitted profiles, weights, and the recorded objectives.
\end{enumerate}
\end{minipage}}
\end{center}

We also evaluate an alternative stabilization of the earlier partial-peeling initialization. After fitting, remove each component in turn; choose a replacement profile by its global gain relative to the remaining fitted profiles, and run a fresh fit. Accept the new fit only if its final working mixture objective increases. The evaluated replacement pass tests the $K$ possible component removals and selects an improving trial. This is an alternative branch with additional fitting cost; the results below do not assert that the combination of Algorithm~1 and replacement was evaluated.

The numerical experiments use the exact floored-simplex weight update, finite stopping tolerances, and an iteration cap. Objective convergence in exact arithmetic does not replace checking the finite-run stopping rule or floating-point error. We report those numerical checks separately.

\subsection{Why the representation can retain an LLR}
Suppose temporarily that $B$ and the other labels are known. For testing $z_i=a$ against $z_i=b$, define, for $j\ne i$,
\begin{equation}
 w_j^{ab}=\log\frac{p_{aj}(1-p_{bj})}{p_{bj}(1-p_{aj})},
 \qquad c_i^{ab}=\sum_{j\ne i}\log\frac{1-p_{aj}}{1-p_{bj}}.
\label{eq:oracle_weights}
\end{equation}
The oracle Bernoulli LLR is $L_i^{ab}=\sum_{j\ne i}A_{ij}w_j^{ab}+c_i^{ab}$. Extend $w_i^{ab}$ by the same block formula. Because $A_{ii}=0$, this extension does not change the oracle linear statistic. The contrast vector is block constant, hence belongs to $\col(Z)$.

The fundamental identity is
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde A=A\Pi,\qquad \Pi=UU^\top,
 \qquad (\widetilde A_i-A_i)w^{ab}=A_i(\Pi-\mathrm{Id})w^{ab}.
\label{eq:projection_identity}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathrm{Id}$ denotes the identity matrix. If $w^{ab}$ lies in the retained empirical space, the linear statistic is preserved exactly. At the population level, full rank gives $\col(\Omega^\circ)=\col(Z)$, so every contrast has the required span membership. The spectral decomposition need not itself optimize a likelihood: it retains a linear space containing the directions in which the likelihood acts.

The executable score additionally involves clipping, estimated profiles, mixture thresholds, and a retained reconstructed diagonal. These perturbations require statistical control. A matrix reconstruction bound alone does not establish the oracle exponent; the local fitting theorem uses the stronger row-profile approximation, the clipping moment bound, and a weak-initialization condition. Theorem~\ref{thm:local-spectral-em} supplies the fitting guarantee. Theorem~\ref{thm:two-community-growing} establishes its initialization condition for the original growing branch when $K=2$, and Theorem~\ref{thm:certified-likelihood} establishes it for the complete Algorithm~1 for every fixed $K$.

\subsection{Computational cost}
Forming the full positive reconstruction stores $O(n^2)$ numbers. Caching all candidate scores uses an $n$-by-$n$ matrix and, by direct multiplication, $O(n^3)$ arithmetic. Each subsequent greedy scan costs $O(n^2)$, and one EM iteration costs $O(Kn^2)$. Algorithm~1 runs $O(\log n)$ additional fits, so its total remains polynomial for a prescribed polynomial iteration bound. These costs are additional to computing the eigenpairs. The implementation is practical at the tested $n\leq2000$ but does not yet justify a large-network speed claim. Candidate subsampling and blocked score evaluation are natural approximations, whose statistical and computational performance require separate validation.
