\section{Extension below logarithmic density}
\label{sec:sparse}

\subsection{Recovery target}
Consider $d_n=n\rho_n\to\infty$ with $d_n=o(\log n)$, keeping $K$, positive limiting community proportions, and $B$ fixed. The pairwise population profiles remain distinct, and the leading oracle information is proportional to $d_n$. The appropriate target is a vanishing fraction of errors with bound
\begin{equation}
 \E \Mis(\widehat z,z)\leq e^{-(1+o(1))I_n},
 \qquad I_n=d_nJ_*+o(d_n).
\label{eq:sparse_rate}
\end{equation}
For consistency the right-hand side tends to zero. It generally does not yield exact recovery when $d_n=o(\log n)$. The distinction concerns the recovery objective, rather than a change in the meaning of the likelihood-ratio benchmark.

The score-transfer theorem in Section~\ref{sec:main-results} applies at this density whenever its score-error conditions hold. The sparse Bernoulli-to-Poisson approximation remains valid because its exponent-scale correction is $O(n\rho_n^2)=o(d_n)$. These facts provide a precise route for extension. They do not by themselves control the empirical eigenspace.

\subsection{Representation and calibration}
Low-degree concentration is more delicate: a few atypically high-degree vertices can produce localized empirical directions. Regularization can stabilize a spectral initializer, but a regularized representation must also retain the directions used for likelihood classification. Existing regularized spectral consistency results \citep{zhouamini2019} concern recovery or concentration under their stated conditions; they do not automatically imply exponentially accurate spectral likelihood decoding.

For example, suppose a positive diagonal normalization $D$ is used and the retained eigenpairs satisfy
\begin{equation}
 M=D^{-1/2}AD^{-1/2},\qquad MV=V\Gamma,\qquad \Pi_M=VV^\top.
\end{equation}
The reconstruction in the original scale is
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde A_D=D^{1/2}V\Gamma V^\top D^{1/2}
 =A D^{-1/2}\Pi_M D^{1/2}.
\label{eq:normalized_reconstruction}
\end{equation}
Consequently $\widetilde A_D w=Aw$ whenever $D^{1/2}w\in\col(V)$. This exact identity exhibits the needed transformed contrast. The normalization factors must be retained with the eigenpairs to perform this reconstruction. A normalized embedding without its scale factors is not covered by this identity.

Trimming is a different operation. If $M=A-T$ is obtained by modifying entries, its retained reconstruction is $M\Pi_M$, and
\begin{equation}
 A_iw-(M\Pi_M)_iw=T_iw+M_i(\mathrm{Id}-\Pi_M)w.
\label{eq:trim_error}
\end{equation}
Both the removed-edge contrast and projection error must be controlled at the recovery scale. This prevents interpreting every regularized spectral embedding as automatically equivalent to the original-graph LRT.

\subsection{A conditional extension}
\begin{corollary}[Lower-density transfer]
\label{cor:sparse}
Under the fixed-$B$ model with $d_n\to\infty$, $\rho_n\to0$, and positive limiting proportions, let a spectral decoder, possibly calibrated as in \eqref{eq:normalized_reconstruction}, satisfy the score approximation and exceptional-event bounds of Theorem~\ref{thm:exponent-transfer}. Then its expected misclassification rate satisfies \eqref{eq:sparse_rate}. If $d_n=o(\log n)$, this statement establishes almost exact recovery, without an exact-recovery conclusion.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The Chernoff expansion and score-transfer proof use $d_n\to\infty$ and $n\rho_n^2=o(d_n)$, which follow here from $\rho_n\to0$. Substitute the density into Theorem~\ref{thm:exponent-transfer}. Since the minimum divergence is strictly positive for distinct profiles, $I_n\to\infty$ and the expected fraction of errors tends to zero.
\end{proof}

Verifying this corollary for a specified regularizer and fitting procedure remains a separate statistical task. The present 240-graph evaluation uses the unregularized logarithmic-density representation. Bounded expected degree, where detection has a different objective and threshold, is outside the scope of these results.
