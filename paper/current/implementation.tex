\subsection{Experimental entry points and retained outputs}
The numerical evidence is archived in the repository
\begin{center}
\url{https://github.com/zhixin0825/spectral-lrt}.
\end{center}
Algorithm~1 is implemented in \texttt{residual\_seed.py}. Its method is
\begin{center}
\texttt{Work.iterative\_certified\_growing()}.
\end{center}
The public API defaults to \texttt{method='global\_gain\_certified'}. It starts with the global-mean growing fit and then iterates deterministic component replacement. Each round fits the current profiles from uniform weights and, for each component removal, fits the globally selected replacement from uniform weights. Every node ID participates in each replacement scan. The largest trial objective is accepted only if its improvement is at least $T/\sqrt{\log n}$, where $T=\sum_{ij}q_{ij}$. The accepted-round cap is $\lceil(\log n)^2\rceil$, followed by one mandatory final E/M update. Under the theorem's hypotheses, the stopping certificate is reached in $O(\sqrt{\log n})$ accepted rounds, before the cap. The earlier residual-restart implementation is exposed separately as \texttt{method='global\_gain\_restarts'}. The archived primary benchmark remains the original growing branch. The principal comparison is in
\path{experiments/20261009_residual_likelihood_seeding/}.

\path{experiments/20261009_partial_likelihood_peeling/}.
The original growing branch corresponds to the recorded \texttt{growing\_global\_gain\_EM} arm and the API option \texttt{method='global\_gain'}. It starts at the global profile mean, adds $K-1$ node candidates, and refits after every addition. The \texttt{global\_gain\_no\_update} arm is a separate frozen-profile control with a top-subset first seed. Identical final error counts do not identify the algorithms.

The manuscript's two CSV summaries retain batch-specific, combined, and model-specific results. The diagnostic figure uses saved coordinates and fitted labels rather than a new simulation. Full spectral arrays and objective traces are stored in the previously archived numerical records; the source reports describe their restoration. \subsection{Logarithm and weight floors}
The current implementation sets
\[
 q_{ij}=\max\{(U\Lambda U^\top)_{ij},\varepsilon_n\},\qquad
 \varepsilon_n=10^{-4}\frac{\max\{\|\Lambda\|_{\mathrm{op}},1\}}n,
\]
and uses the exact weight update with floor $f=10^{-8}$. The spectral scale is computed from the retained eigenvalues. The theorem requires the chosen fixed floor constant to be sufficiently small relative to the block-probability comparison constants. On the spectral approximation event, this gives a floor of order $p$ strictly below every $P_{ab}$. The value $10^{-4}$ does not supply such a guarantee for arbitrarily small $\min_{a,b}P_{ab}/p$. This operation defines positive logarithm arguments after spectral reconstruction. Profiles are positive vectors, and finite-sample reconstructed coordinates are not constrained to lie below one.

The archived 240-graph results used the earlier floor $10^{-4}\log n/n$. Those recorded runs and their summaries are retained as historical evidence. Their settings should not be substituted for the adaptive floor of Algorithm~1 when testing densities above logarithmic order.

The API default \texttt{method='global\_gain\_certified'} executes Algorithm~1. The historical replacement branch is \texttt{method='repair'}, the original growing branch is \texttt{method='global\_gain'}, and the earlier random-restart combination is \texttt{method='global\_gain\_restarts'}. These options implement different procedures.

\subsection{Historical subset controls}
These controls are retained for reproducibility of earlier experiments and are not steps of Algorithm~1. In the frozen global-gain control, the first candidate maximizes the sum of its largest
$m_0=\min\{\lfloor2n/(3K)\rfloor,n-K\}$ scores over other nodes, excluding itself. In peeling, when $M$ candidates and $r$ components remain, the selected subset contains
$\min\{\lfloor2M/(3K)\rfloor,M-r\}$ other nodes. Only these historical controls require a subset-size rule; the primary growing algorithm uses the global mean and all-node gains throughout.

\subsection{Floored-simplex weight updates}
For a fixed floor $f\in[0,1/K)$, the weight M-step solves
\begin{equation}
 \max_{\widehat\pi_a\geq f,\,\sum_a\widehat\pi_a=1}\sum_aN_a\log\widehat\pi_a.
\end{equation}
Its active-set solution starts with every component free. If the free set is $S$, assign mass $1-f(K-|S|)$ proportionally to the $N_a$, $a\in S$, and fix any weight below $f$ at the floor. Repeat on the remaining free set. The KKT conditions give
\begin{equation}
 \widehat\pi_a=\max\{f,N_a/\lambda\},\qquad \sum_a\widehat\pi_a=1
\end{equation}
for a suitable multiplier $\lambda$. The active-set procedure enforces this equation; it is not a naive clipping followed by renormalization. The tested function \texttt{\_floored\_weights} implements this exact constrained update. Since the E-step and mean M-step are unchanged, the variational proof applies on the floored-simplex parameter domain.

\subsection{Archived stopping rule}
The 240-graph profile fits stop only after hard labels are unchanged, maximum relative mean change and absolute weight change are at most $10^{-3}$, and the objective increment is at most $10^{-6}(1+|\mathcal L-G|)$ in absolute value. Here $G=\sum_{ij}\log\Gamma(q_{ij}+1)$ is a cached observation-only constant used solely to scale the numerical stopping tolerance. It does not enter scoring, responsibilities, M-steps or fit selection. The update cap is 300. These historical numerical choices were preserved in this revision.

\subsection{An explicit error decomposition}
For fitted components matched to labels $a,b$, put
\begin{equation}
 \widehat w^{ab}_j=\log(\widehat p_{aj}/\widehat p_{bj}),\qquad
 \widehat c^{ab}=-\sum_j(\widehat p_{aj}-\widehat p_{bj})+\log(\widehat\pi_a/\widehat\pi_b).
\end{equation}
If $\lambda^{ab}$ is the chosen oracle prior log ratio, the empirical comparison has the decomposition
\begin{align}
 \widehat T_i^{ab}-(L_i^{ab}+\lambda^{ab})
 &=A_i(\Pi-\mathrm{Id})w^{ab}
   +(q_i-\widetilde A_i)w^{ab}\nonumber\\
 &\quad+q_i(\widehat w^{ab}-w^{ab})
   +\widehat c^{ab}-c_i^{ab}-\lambda^{ab}.
\label{eq:error_decomposition}
\end{align}
The terms correspond to empirical projection, positivity clipping, estimated contrasts, and intercept calibration. The reconstructed diagonal is included in these terms; the oracle excludes it through $A_{ii}=0$ and the definition of $c_i^{ab}$. If the exact Bernoulli contrast is replaced by the first-order sparse contrast, Lemma~\ref{lem:sparse-expansion} accounts for the additional Taylor remainder. Formula~\eqref{eq:error_decomposition} is exact and gives concrete quantities for checking the transfer conditions. Theorem~\ref{thm:local-spectral-em} controls the empirical fitting stage through a comparison with clipped oracle block-count scores. It does not require a separate rare-event $o(np)$ bound on the raw clipping term in this decomposition. Algorithm~1 has an end-to-end proof for every fixed $K\ge2$ under \eqref{eq:density-condition}--\eqref{eq:model-conditions}. Its deterministic repeated-replacement certificate supplies the local theorem's initialization requirement.
