\title{Rate-Optimal Community Detection by Spectral Likelihood Decoding}
\author{Zhixin Zhou}
\date{October 18, 2026}
\begin{document}
\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
The oracle likelihood-ratio test for a stochastic block model classifies a node using its edge counts to the true communities. We study how much of this evidence is retained by a spectral representation together with the observed degrees. We first give the simpler unregularized construction: for every fixed number of communities and $p=\Omega(\log n/n)$, the spectral likelihood decoder achieves the Zhou--Li benchmark $\mathbb E\Mis\le C\eta^*$ under comparable, uniformly well-conditioned block probabilities. Here $\eta^*$ is the largest pairwise total variation affinity, equivalent under these assumptions to $e^{-I_n}/\sqrt{np}$, and $p=\max_{a,b}P_{ab}$. The proof controls likelihood tails after spectral projection, clipping and fitting, including the oracle polynomial factor. We then introduce the general algorithm retaining $(D,U,\Lambda)$, where $D=A\mathbf1$ and the eigenpairs come from a degree-regularized adjacency matrix. Exact matrix identities allow its reconstructed block counts to sum to each observed degree. Initialization and refinement use one complete Bernoulli row criterion, calculated entirely from the retained information. A sharp transfer proposition gives the same $C\eta^*$ conclusion under explicit conditional tail hypotheses without imposing logarithmic expected degree; verifying these hypotheses for the full regularized algorithm remains open. Empirical reconstructed counts need not equal the original integers, and the comparison permits a constant-factor change in classification risk.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

The likelihood-ratio test gives a natural reference point for community detection in a stochastic block model (SBM). We use community detection as the broad task name, and recovery to describe the accuracy of the estimated labels. If the other labels and the block probabilities are known, classifying a node requires only its edge counts to the communities. Each count is weighted by the appropriate log-odds contrast, and the contribution of absent edges supplies the remaining term. The statistical question in this paper is how accurately this familiar test can be implemented from observed degrees and a small collection of spectral eigenpairs. We introduce the easier unregularized case first, where the eigenpairs alone suffice for the proved constant-factor oracle comparison. Section~\ref{sec:degree-spectral} then gives the general construction using both degrees and regularized spectral information, including the regime below logarithmic expected degree.

We treat the partition $z\in[K]^n$ as a fixed unknown parameter. The graph has independent upper-triangular Bernoulli edges with probabilities $P_{z_i z_j}$ and no self-loops \cite{holland1983}. Write $p=\max_{a,b}P_{ab}$. Our model uses the size bound $n_a\ge n/(\beta K)$, block probabilities comparable to $p$ and bounded away from one, and a quantitative full-rank condition on $P$. For the unregularized theorem we additionally assume $p=\Omega(\log n/n)$. The general algorithm retains degrees and regularized eigenpairs and is defined also when $np\to\infty$ grows more slowly than $\log n$. Neither the normalized block matrix nor the community proportions need converge. After compression, initialization and refinement use only the retained statistic: $(U,\Lambda)$ in the first case and $(D,U,\Lambda)$ in the general case.

\paragraph{The oracle test and the information retained by projection.}
Let $Z$ be the membership matrix. The entries of $AZ$ are the node-to-community edge counts. The oracle Bernoulli log-likelihood ratio between labels $a$ and $b$ is a linear combination of these counts plus a known nonedge term: its weight vector is constant on every community. When $P$ has full rank, the signal space of $ZPZ^\top$ is exactly $\operatorname{col}(Z)$. If $\Pi$ is the projector onto this population space, then
\[
 (A\Pi)Z=AZ.
\]
Thus an oracle projection would preserve the counts exactly. Its real-valued entries are simply community averages, from which the integer sums can be recovered by multiplication. This observation explains why a subspace selected by a quadratic approximation criterion can still carry the evidence used by a likelihood-ratio test.

The algorithm uses an estimated subspace rather than $\Pi$. Its signed rank-$K$ reconstruction is $\widetilde A=A\widehat\Pi$, where $\widehat\Pi$ is the projector calculated from the retained eigenvectors. Consequently its community sums differ from the observed counts by $A(\widehat\Pi-\Pi)Z$. These reconstructed sums are generally nonintegers; our analysis does not place each of them within $1/2$ of the corresponding count or recover the counts by rounding. The relevant criterion is instead how their errors affect classification. Relative count consistency alone does not control errors at the oracle risk scale. We instead control the likelihood tails at the scale required by the sharp bound of Zhou and Li.

\paragraph{The rate to be preserved.}
For the exact incident-edge Bernoulli test, let $I_{i,ab}$ be its Chernoff information and let $I_n$ be the minimum over admissible nodes and distinct labels. Under our model bounds, $I_{i,ab}\asymp np$, the optimizing Chernoff parameter stays away from both endpoints, and the variance under the optimizing exponential tilt is of order $np$. The sharp Chernoff bounds of Zhou and Li \cite{zhouli2020} therefore give the pairwise oracle error scale
\[
 \frac{e^{-I_{i,ab}}}{\sqrt{np}}.
\]
Our first theorem proves, for the complete unregularized algorithm,
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb E\,\Mis(\widehat z,z)
 \le C\eta^*.
 \label{eq:intro-sharp-rate}
\end{equation}
Here $\eta^*$ is the maximum pairwise total variation affinity, defined in \eqref{eq:oracle-affinity}, and $C$ depends only on the fixed model and algorithm bounds. Under our assumptions, $\eta^*\asymp e^{-I_n}/\sqrt{np}$. Throughout, rate optimality refers to this constant-factor comparison with the Zhou--Li oracle scale. It does not assert an error ratio converging to one, exact recovery of the original edge counts, or statistical sufficiency of the retained spectrum for all inference on $z$. We also do not prove a new global minimax lower bound.

\paragraph{The easier unregularized decoder.}
We retain the $K$ eigenpairs of the original adjacency matrix with largest absolute eigenvalues, allowing signal eigenvalues of either sign. Clipping their reconstruction gives connection profiles $q_i$ in a probability interval whose endpoints are determined by the retained eigenvalues. A complete Bernoulli row score compares these profiles to fitted connection profiles and includes the absent-edge term. This term matters for our density range: Section~\ref{sec:main-results} gives a dense example in which a sparse-score substitute loses a fixed fraction of the oracle exponent even with known parameters.

The same Bernoulli row score drives global-mean growing, component replacement and EM refinement. Growing adds an observed profile according to its total positive likelihood gain and jointly refits the current components. The algorithm then tests all deletion positions and their best observed replacement candidates for up to $\lceil\log n\rceil$ rounds, retaining the fit with the greatest common working mixture likelihood. This construction supplies a deterministic initialization guarantee without requiring a Euclidean initializer or a global optimum of an EM fit. The gain used to select a candidate and the soft mixture objective aggregate the common row score differently; their relation is part of the proof. One mandatory final E-step and profile/weight M-step precedes classification.

\paragraph{Why the spectral loss is small enough.}
Two parts of the argument connect this algorithm to the oracle test. First, a population replacement removes at least $1/K$ of the current hard fitting loss. A fitted profile unused by the true classes can be replaced without harming them; if every fitted profile is used, matching is a bijection and the profile assigned the largest loss can be replaced. Good observed candidates transfer this contraction to the empirical working objective. Its resulting small-loss certificate implies matched profile consistency and, more strongly, an E-step responsibility error of order $1/(np)$. The algorithm's existing final M-step therefore reaches the parameter accuracy required by the sharp likelihood analysis. No additional refinement step is needed for this objective-certified endpoint.

Second, we compare the spectral classification evidence to the oracle incident-edge evidence in the tail where errors occur. Conditioning on the graph with one vertex deleted gives an arrowhead expansion of the full-graph spectral reconstruction. It expresses each relevant spectral row sum as a linear function of the independent incident edges plus a bounded remainder. Replacing the population weights by the estimated projected weights changes the conditional log moment generating function by only a bounded amount at the oracle tilt. Clipping and parameter fitting require additional bounds; they cannot be discarded solely because the reconstruction is consistent. A finite collection of clipping branches and a local concentration bound under exponential tilting preserve the $(np)^{-1/2}$ factor. Exponential-confidence spectral events ensure that exceptional graphs are negligible even when $np$ is much larger than $\log n$.

These comparisons allow a constant-factor change in the error probability. They do not require every spectral count to equal an observed integer or every classification decision to agree with the oracle. The substantive conclusion is that the remaining perturbation is small enough at the testing boundary to retain the full rate in \eqref{eq:intro-sharp-rate}.

\paragraph{Relation to existing recovery methods.}
Abbe and Sandon \cite{abbe2015} characterize exact recovery in the general logarithmic-density SBM through Chernoff--Hellinger divergence and provide efficient algorithms reaching the threshold. Likelihood refinement after a spectral initializer is also well established. Amini, Chen, Bickel and Levina \cite{amini2013} fit mixtures to blockwise counts by pseudo-likelihood. Gao, Ma, Zhang and Zhou \cite{gao2017} obtain optimal misclassification performance from a weakly consistent initializer and penalized local likelihood refinement. Zhou and Amini \cite{zhouamini2020} analyze spectral initialization and pseudo-likelihood classification in bipartite recovery, together with EM-type updates. A recent preprint of Wang \cite{wang2026vem} studies spectrally initialized variational EM in a general SBM and obtains a leading Chernoff exponent while estimating parameters and reusing the graph. Our retained information consists of degrees and spectral eigenpairs. The general refinement uses this compressed statistic, with no subsequent access to individual adjacency entries.

Spectral methods themselves attain optimal recovery guarantees in important settings. Abbe, Fan, Wang and Zhong \cite{abbe2020} give entrywise eigenvector approximations and establish optimal thresholds and misclassification exponents for an untrimmed spectral method in the equal-sized symmetric two-community SBM at logarithmic density. For homogeneous planted-partition models with multiple possibly unequal communities, Zhang \cite{zhang2024} obtains matching algorithm-specific exponents for trimming followed by eigenvalue-weighted spectral clustering and $K$-means; that exponent is distinguished from the information-theoretic exponent. Suwan et al.\ \cite{suwan2016} use Gaussian-mixture limits of adjacency spectral embedding for empirical Bayes membership inference. These works motivate statistical decoding of spectral representations. Our focus is the sharp rare-event comparison with the Bernoulli oracle for general full-rank block matrices. The data-driven regularization and consistency analysis of Zhou and Amini \cite{zhouamini2019} motivates the general construction in Section~\ref{sec:degree-spectral}. Our unconditional sharp theorem is proved first under $p=\Omega(\log n/n)$; the later section states exactly which conditional likelihood-tail bounds extend the sharp comparison below that range.

\paragraph{Degrees and the general construction.}
Let $D=A\mathbf1$ and let $W$ be the positive diagonal regularizer determined by $D$. If $WAWU=U\Lambda$, then
\[
 A(WU)=W^{-1}U\Lambda,\qquad A\mathbf1=D.
\]
Thus $(D,U,\Lambda)$ gives the exact action of $A$ on the augmented space spanned by $\mathbf1$ and $WU$. Section~\ref{sec:degree-spectral} uses this action to construct degree-preserving row evidence and block counts. The constant-direction part of every likelihood contrast is then retained exactly. The same Bernoulli row criterion drives likelihood initialization and EM refinement. The general algorithm and its exact identities require no logarithmic density bound; a complete sparse sharp-rate theorem additionally requires the conditional tail hypotheses stated there.

\paragraph{Algorithmic scope and numerical evidence.}
The unregularized theorem covers the complete growing-and-replacement algorithm and every fixed $K$. General-$K$ convergence of growing alone is not established. A five-community population example illustrates the possible loss of a class when growing uses only one EM update per stage. The numerical section reports a 60-graph benchmark of the complete Bernoulli implementation and, separately, the earlier 240-graph sparse-score experiments. The complete benchmark shows no additional classification improvement from replacement in that collection and exposes finite-sample difficulties at weak signals. It complements the theorem without establishing oracle risk ratios or uniform empirical superiority over $K$-means.

Section~\ref{sec:algorithm} introduces the simpler unregularized algorithm and its relation to the oracle test. Section~\ref{sec:main-results} proves its sharp guarantee. Section~\ref{sec:degree-spectral} introduces the general degree-plus-spectrum algorithm and its sharp transfer criterion. Section~\ref{sec:experiments} reports the experiments, and Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes. Proofs and implementation details are in the appendix.

\section{The unregularized case: from oracle likelihood ratios to spectral decoding}
\label{sec:algorithm}

\subsection{Model and retained information}
Let $A$ be the adjacency matrix of an undirected graph without self-loops. For a fixed unknown partition $z\in[K]^n$, the upper-triangular entries are independent, with
\begin{equation}
 A_{ij}\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(P_{z_i z_j}),\quad i<j,
 \qquad A_{ji}=A_{ij},\quad A_{ii}=0.
 \label{eq:sbm}
\end{equation}
We follow Zhou and Li~\citep{zhouli2020}: $P$ is the symmetric block probability matrix, $p_{aj}=P_{a,z_j}$, and $p_{a*}$ is the connection profile of community $a$. Write $Z_{ia}=\one\{z_i=a\}$, $n_a=\sum_iZ_{ia}$, $\pi_{a,n}=n_a/n$, and
\[
 p=\max_{a,b}P_{ab}.
\]
The known community number $K\ge2$ is any fixed integer. For this unregularized case, the model and density conditions are, for a fixed constant $\beta\ge1$ and fixed positive constants $\kappa,\eta$,
\begin{align}
 n_a&\ge\frac{n}{\beta K},\qquad P_{ab}\ge\kappa p\quad(a,b\in[K]),\qquad
 \sigma_{\min}(P)\ge\kappa p,
 \label{eq:model-conditions}\\
 p&\le1-\eta,\qquad p=\Omega(\log n/n).
 \label{eq:density-condition}
\end{align}
We use the conventional imbalance parameter $\beta$, with the normalization $n/(\beta K)$ common in SBM recovery results~\citep{zhouli2020,gao2017}. Only this lower size bound is required; a separate upper balance bound is unnecessary. Writing $n/\beta$ instead would absorb the fixed factor $K$ into $\beta$. These conditions are used for the unregularized sharp theorem. Section~\ref{sec:degree-spectral} treats the general retained statistic and replaces the logarithmic density condition by $np\to\infty$ in its sparse-rate target. We may take $\kappa\le1$. Neither $P/p$ nor the community proportions need converge. The probability matrix may vary with $n$ within these bounds. In particular, there is no requirement that $(n/\log n)P$ be fixed.

For $z_i=a$, the expected degree is
\begin{equation}
 \E[\deg(i)\mid z]
   =\sum_b(n_b-\one\{a=b\})P_{ab}.
 \label{eq:expected-degree}
\end{equation}
These expectations lie between $\kappa(n-1)p$ and $np$ and may differ across communities. Thus they are uniformly of order $np$, and \eqref{eq:density-condition} implies that all expected degrees are $\Omega(\log n)$. The density condition permits intermediate densities and constant edge probabilities. The probability-comparability and singular-value conditions ensure a uniformly separated, well-conditioned signal; degree alone does not imply identifiability.

Retain the $K$ eigenpairs of the original adjacency matrix with largest absolute eigenvalues:
\begin{equation}
 AU=U\Lambda,\qquad U^\top U=I_K,\qquad
 \widetilde A=U\Lambda U^\top.
 \label{eq:representation}
\end{equation}
Both signs of signal eigenvalues are allowed. In this unregularized case, every subsequent operation uses only $(U,\Lambda)$, including initialization, fitting, replacement and classification. The degree vector is retained explicitly for the general construction in Section~\ref{sec:degree-spectral}.

\subsection{The oracle LRT depends on community edge counts}
For testing $z_i=a$ against $z_i=b$ with the other labels and $P$ known, put
\begin{equation}
 w_j^{ab}=\log\frac{p_{aj}(1-p_{bj})}{p_{bj}(1-p_{aj})},\qquad
 c_i^{ab}=\sum_{j\ne i}\log\frac{1-p_{aj}}{1-p_{bj}}.
 \label{eq:oracle-contrast}
\end{equation}
The exact incident-edge Bernoulli log-likelihood ratio is
\begin{equation}
 L_i^{ab}=A_iw^{ab}+c_i^{ab}
   =\sum_{c=1}^K b_{ic}w_c^{ab}+c_i^{ab},\qquad
 b_{ic}=\sum_{j:z_j=c}A_{ij},
 \label{eq:oracle-count-lrt}
\end{equation}
where $w_c^{ab}=\log\{P_{ac}(1-P_{bc})/[P_{bc}(1-P_{ac})]\}$ is the common value of $w_j^{ab}$ in community $c$. Thus the oracle LRT needs $K$ community edge counts for a node, rather than the individual locations of its edges within each community. Following Amini, Chen, Bickel and Levina~\citep[Section~2.1]{amini2013}, for a candidate labeling $e$ write $b_{ic}(e)=\sum_j A_{ij}\one\{e_j=c\}$; here $b_{ic}=b_{ic}(z)$. The matching spectral count is $\widetilde b_{ic}(e)=\sum_j\widetilde A_{ij}\one\{e_j=c\}$. These are node-to-block counts. Total ordered counts between two blocks are entries of $Z(e)^\top A Z(e)$; an entry on its diagonal counts each undirected edge twice. The self-loop coordinate contributes no edge because $A_{ii}=0$. Knowledge of the other labels is an oracle device for defining the benchmark; the decoder below is not supplied those labels.

\subsection{What spectral compression retains and what it can lose}
The population membership projector and its spectral estimate are
\[
 \Pi=Z(Z^\top Z)^{-1}Z^\top,\qquad \widehat\Pi=UU^\top.
\]
Under the full-rank condition on $P$, the signal space $\operatorname{col}(ZPZ^\top)$ equals $\operatorname{col}(Z)$. It therefore contains every community indicator and every oracle weight vector $w^{ab}$. This is the algebraic reason for using a spectral representation to approximate the LRT.

\begin{proposition}[Oracle projection and empirical spectral compression]
\label{prop:exact-preservation}
For the oracle projector, $\Pi Z=Z$ and
\begin{equation}
 (A\Pi)Z=AZ,\qquad Z^\top(A\Pi)Z=Z^\top AZ.
 \label{eq:oracle-count-preservation}
\end{equation}
For the retained empirical eigenpairs,
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde A=A\widehat\Pi,\qquad
 \widetilde A_iw-A_iw=A_i(\widehat\Pi-\mathrm{Id})w
 \label{eq:projection-identity}
\end{equation}
for every $w\in\mathbb R^n$. In particular,
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde b_{ic}:=\sum_{j:z_j=c}\widetilde A_{ij},\qquad
 \widetilde b_{ic}-b_{ic}
   =A_i(\widehat\Pi-\Pi)\one_{\{z=c\}}.
 \label{eq:empirical-count-error}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The oracle identities follow from $\Pi Z=Z$. With $N=Z^\top Z$, the factorization
\[
 ZPZ^\top=(ZN^{-1/2})(N^{1/2}PN^{1/2})(ZN^{-1/2})^\top
\]
identifies the signal space. Finally, $AU=U\Lambda$ gives $A\widehat\Pi=U\Lambda U^\top$ and the remaining identities.
\end{proof}

The distinction between the two projectors is statistical. For $j$ in community $c$, the real-valued oracle entry is $(A\Pi)_{ij}=b_{ic}/n_c$: it stores the integer count as a block average, including its random fluctuation. The empirical quantity $\widetilde b_{ic}$ need not be an integer, and our result does not assert that its error is below $1/2$ or that rounding recovers $b_{ic}$. Small subspace error alone would not imply either assertion.

Instead, we control the loss relevant to the LRT. Substituting $\widetilde b_{ic}$ for $b_{ic}$ perturbs a weighted likelihood contrast. Clipping and fitted parameters introduce further changes. The proof controls these changes through conditional exponential moments and concentration under the oracle Chernoff tilt, where classification errors occur. This control is strong enough to preserve the oracle error rate up to a constant factor, including its $(np)^{-1/2}$ factor. It does not require exact recovery of each count. Ordinary consistency of the counts relative to their means, or a score approximation holding only for typical graphs, would not suffice for this rare-event comparison.

The actual mean is
\begin{equation}
 \E[A\mid z]=ZPZ^\top-\operatorname{diag}(P_{z_i z_i}).
 \label{eq:mean}
\end{equation}
The analysis includes this absent-diagonal correction and estimates the empirical signal space from the same graph used for classification. The oracle counts and projectors above are comparison tools, not additional inputs to the algorithm.

\subsection{Observed profiles and the complete Bernoulli score}
To define logarithms of reconstructed probabilities, set
\begin{equation}
 s=\max\{1,\|\Lambda\|_{\mathrm{op}}\},\qquad
 \varepsilon_n=c_0s/n,\qquad
 q_{ij}=\operatorname{clip}(\widetilde A_{ij},\varepsilon_n,1-\varepsilon_n),
 \label{eq:profiles}
\end{equation}
where $0<c_0<\min\{\kappa,\eta,1\}/8$ is a fixed algorithm parameter and $\operatorname{clip}(x,l,u)=\min\{u,\max\{l,x\}\}$. The admissibility of $c_0$ is an explicit model condition on this chosen logarithm floor. The scale $s$ and the floor are calculated from the retained eigenvalues. The reconstructed diagonal is retained. Since $s\le n$ for an adjacency matrix and $c_0<1/8$, the interval is nonempty even on exceptional graphs.

For $\widehat p\in(0,1)^n$, the working score is
\begin{equation}
 \ell_i(\widehat p)
 =\sum_{j=1}^n\{q_{ij}\log\widehat p_j
           +(1-q_{ij})\log(1-\widehat p_j)\}.
 \label{eq:working-score}
\end{equation}
The second term retains the information in absent edges at every density. The $q_i$ are dependent fractional profiles, so this is a working fitting criterion rather than the exact likelihood of independent edge observations. Its likelihood contrasts approximate the oracle contrasts at the error-probability scale established in Section~\ref{sec:main-results}.
For fitted profiles $\widehat p_1,\ldots,\widehat p_K$ and weights $\widehat\pi\in\Delta_K$, fit
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L(\widehat\pi,\widehat p)
 =\sum_i\log\sum_a\widehat\pi_a e^{\ell_i(\widehat p_a)},\qquad
 \widehat z_i=\argmax_a\{\ell_i(\widehat p_a)+\log\widehat\pi_a\}.
 \label{eq:mixture-and-decode}
\end{equation}
The output error is the permutation-invariant fraction
$\Mis(\widehat z,z)=\min_{\sigma\in\mathfrak S_K}n^{-1}\sum_i\one\{\sigma(\widehat z_i)\ne z_i\}$.

\subsection{Growing initialization and exact updates}
\label{sec:growing-branch}
Start one profile at the global mean $n^{-1}\sum_iq_i$. At stage $t=2,\ldots,K$, if the existing profiles are $\widehat p_1,\ldots,\widehat p_{t-1}$, compute
\begin{equation}
 h_i=\max_{a<t}\ell_i(\widehat p_a),\qquad
 \Delta(c)=\sum_i(\ell_i(q_c)-h_i)_+.
 \label{eq:growing-gain}
\end{equation}
Append a node profile maximizing this all-node gain, excluding previously added node IDs, initialize the $t$ weights uniformly, and jointly fit all $t$ profiles. No observations are removed. The fitted profiles supply the next stage. Initialization and refinement use the same complete nodewise score \eqref{eq:working-score}. The positive hard gain selects candidate profiles, while the soft mixture objective \eqref{eq:mixture-and-decode} compares fitted models; these are different aggregations of the same nodewise score.

At any component count, the exact E/M updates are
\begin{align}
 R_{ia}&=\frac{\widehat\pi_a e^{\ell_i(\widehat p_a)}}
                  {\sum_b\widehat\pi_b e^{\ell_i(\widehat p_b)}},
 &N_a&=\sum_iR_{ia},\label{eq:responsibilities}\\
 \widehat p_{aj}^{\mathrm{new}}&=\frac{\sum_iR_{ia}q_{ij}}{N_a},
 &\widehat\pi_a^{\mathrm{new}}&=N_a/n.
 \label{eq:mstep}
\end{align}
A component of zero responsibility mass retains its previous profile. Each fit performs at least one M-step. Further exact updates may use any prescribed finite iteration bound and any stopping rule within that bound. All means remain convex combinations of the same $q$ rows, and each E/M pair increases the same working objective. The update formula is unchanged by including the nonedge term: maximizing a weighted Bernoulli cross-entropy still gives the weighted mean.

\subsection{Repeated likelihood replacement for every fixed \texorpdfstring{$K$}{K}}
\label{sec:replacement}
Repeated component replacement supplies a fitting certificate for every fixed $K$. Set $T_n=\lceil\log n\rceil$. In each round, refer all trials to the same current fit. For each component $r\in[K]$, delete its profile and compute
\begin{equation}
 h_i^{(-r)}=\max_{a\ne r}\ell_i(\widehat p_a),\qquad
 c_r\in\argmax_{c\in[n]}\sum_i(\ell_i(q_c)-h_i^{(-r)})_+.
 \label{eq:replacement-gain}
\end{equation}
Replace profile $r$ by $q_{c_r}$, initialize the trial's weights uniformly, and run a finite exact EM fit. The candidate scan here includes every node ID, including previously used IDs. Compare the $K$ trial endpoints and the unchanged current fit using $\mathcal L$, and keep the largest value. Ties are deterministic, with the current fit retained on an objective tie. The algorithm may stop after a full round with no improving trial; the contraction below certifies sufficiently small loss at that point.

After the replacement rounds, compute the selected fit's responsibilities and perform one mandatory profile and weight M-step before decoding. The objective certificate makes this E-step's total responsibility error $O(n/(np))$ after a common label matching. The following M-step then makes fitted likelihood contrasts accurate enough for the constant-factor oracle rate. Additional exact EM updates after this step are allowed.

\begin{center}
\fbox{\begin{minipage}{0.93\linewidth}
\textbf{Algorithm 1: Spectral likelihood with repeated parameter replacement}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Form $q$ from the retained eigenpairs using \eqref{eq:profiles}.
\item Obtain $K$ fitted profiles by global-mean growing and joint EM.
\item For $T_n=\lceil\log n\rceil$ rounds, test all $K$ deletion positions; choose each replacement node by \eqref{eq:replacement-gain}, jointly fit it from uniform weights, and retain the greatest working likelihood across the trials and the old fit.
\item Perform one final E-step and profile/weight M-step, then classify by \eqref{eq:mixture-and-decode}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{minipage}}
\end{center}

The replacement certificate controls all $K$ fitted profiles simultaneously, including unequal communities and signal eigenvalues of either sign. It supplies the initialization needed for the sharp likelihood-tail comparison without assuming that growing alone reaches the correct basin. The general-$K$ theorem concerns Algorithm~1 with its stated replacement budget and final E/M update.

\subsection{Computational cost}
Forming and storing $q$ requires $O(n^2)$ storage. The complete node-candidate score matrix can be cached using $O(n^3)$ arithmetic and $O(n^2)$ storage. With current component scores cached, all $K$ deletion scans in a round cost $O(Kn^2+K^2n)$: obtain the minimum and second minimum divergences of each row to handle every deletion. If each trial uses at most $J$ EM updates, its fitting cost is $O(JKn^2)$. The $O(\log n)$ rounds therefore cost $O(JK^2n^2\log n)$ in addition to the candidate cache and growing fits. The direct implementation is polynomial in $n$ for fixed $K$ and polynomial $J$. It does not yet provide a subquadratic storage claim.


\section{Sharp guarantees for the unregularized case}
\label{sec:main-results}

We compare the spectral decoder with the familiar oracle likelihood-ratio test. The issue is how much the empirical spectral compression changes the testing error, rather than whether its real-valued block sums recover the original integer counts. Throughout this section, the fixed parameter $z$ and the block matrix $P$ satisfy \eqref{eq:model-conditions}--\eqref{eq:density-condition}, and $c_0$ satisfies the condition following \eqref{eq:profiles}.

\subsection{The oracle benchmark and the main guarantee}
For $a\ne b$, the oracle reveals $P$ and all labels other than that of vertex $i$, and tests the two product-Bernoulli laws with connection profiles $p_{a,-i}$ and $p_{b,-i}$. Their exact Chernoff information is
\begin{align}
 D_\alpha(p_{a,-i}\Vert p_{b,-i})
  &=-\sum_{j\ne i}\log\{p_{aj}^{1-\alpha}p_{bj}^{\alpha}
           +(1-p_{aj})^{1-\alpha}(1-p_{bj})^{\alpha}\},
  \label{eq:chernoff-information}\\
 D^*_{i,ab}&=\max_{\alpha\in[0,1]}
                 D_\alpha(p_{a,-i}\Vert p_{b,-i}),
 &I_n&=\min_{i,\ a\ne b}D^*_{i,ab}.
 \label{eq:exact-information}
\end{align}
The absent self-loop is excluded. Under our assumptions, $D^*_{i,ab}\asymp np$, its maximizing parameter is bounded away from zero and one, and the likelihood ratio has tilted variance of order $np$. Following Zhou and Li~\citep{zhouli2020}, let $\varphi_{i,a}$ be the product-Bernoulli mass function with profile $p_{a,-i}$, and define its total variation affinity with the alternative by
\begin{equation}
 \eta_{i,ab}=\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^{n-1}}
       \min\{\varphi_{i,a}(x),\varphi_{i,b}(x)\},\qquad
 \eta^*=\max_{i,a\ne b}\eta_{i,ab}.
 \label{eq:oracle-affinity}
\end{equation}
The equal-prior binary oracle risk is exactly $R_{i,ab}^{\mathrm{or}}=\eta_{i,ab}/2$. Their sharp Chernoff bounds give
\begin{equation}
 \eta_{i,ab}\asymp\frac{e^{-D^*_{i,ab}}}{\sqrt{np}},\qquad
 \eta^*\asymp\frac{e^{-I_n}}{\sqrt{np}}.
 \label{eq:oracle-risk}
\end{equation}
All comparisons are uniform over the stated model bounds. Fixed binary priors bounded away from zero and one change only their constants. Appendix~\ref{app:uniform-density-bounds} verifies the uniform conditions used in this oracle comparison.

\begin{theorem}[Spectral likelihood decoding preserves the oracle rate]
\label{thm:main-recovery}
For every fixed $K\ge2$, Algorithm~1 satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \E\Mis(\widehat z,z)
 \le C\eta^*.
 \label{eq:main-rate}
\end{equation}
The constant depends only on the fixed model bounds, the density lower-bound constant, $K$, and the admissible $c_0$. Initialization, fitting and classification use only the retained eigenpairs, and no weak-initialization assumption is imposed. Growing and replacement trials may use any finite number of exact EM updates, with at least one M-step per fit. The mandatory final E/M update is sufficient. Any additional finite sequence of exact E/M updates, including a finite data-dependent stopping rule, preserves the bound. Polynomial iteration caps give a polynomial-time algorithm.

In particular,
\[
 \PP\{\Mis(\widehat z,z)>0\}
 \le Cn\eta^*,
\]
so exact recovery holds whenever $n\eta^*\to0$.
\end{theorem}

The theorem permits $p\to0$, intermediate densities, and constant edge probabilities below one. It does not require convergence of $P/p$ or of the community proportions. The comparison in \eqref{eq:main-rate} concerns the order of the optimal binary testing error. It allows a constant-factor change in risk, and proves neither recovery of every integer edge count nor a risk ratio tending to one. We also make no new global minimax lower-bound claim for an unspecified parameter class.

\paragraph{The sharp oracle affinity.}
More generally, the sharp Chernoff comparison of Zhou and Li has the form
\begin{equation}
 \eta_{i,ab}\asymp
 \frac{e^{-D^*_{i,ab}}}
 {\alpha^*_{i,ab}(1-\alpha^*_{i,ab})\sqrt{V_{i,ab}}},
 \label{eq:general-oracle-prefactor}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha^*_{i,ab}$ maximizes the Chernoff divergence and
$V_{i,ab}$ is the variance of the log likelihood ratio under the
optimizing exponential tilt. This comparison requires the common-support,
third-moment and effective-variance conditions in their sharp bound.
Our assumptions ensure these conditions uniformly, with
$\alpha^*_{i,ab}$ bounded away from zero and one and $V_{i,ab}\asymp np$.
Formula~\eqref{eq:general-oracle-prefactor} describes the more general
oracle benchmark; extending the spectral guarantee beyond our model
conditions requires a separate perturbation analysis.

\begin{corollary}[Planted-partition consequences of the oracle bound]
\label{cor:planted-partition-rate}
Suppose, in addition to the assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm:main-recovery},
that $P_{aa}=p_{\mathrm{in}}$ and $P_{ab}=p_{\mathrm{out}}$ for $a\ne b$.
Let
\begin{equation}
 I^*=-2\log\!\left\{
 \sqrt{p_{\mathrm{in}}p_{\mathrm{out}}}
 +\sqrt{(1-p_{\mathrm{in}})(1-p_{\mathrm{out}})}
 \right\}
 \label{eq:renyi-half-information}
\end{equation}
be the Bernoulli R\'enyi divergence of order $1/2$.
For equal community sizes $n/K$,
\begin{equation}
 \E\Mis(\widehat z,z)
 \le \frac{C}{\sqrt{np}}\exp\!\left\{-\frac{nI^*}{K}\right\}.
 \label{eq:balanced-planted-partition-rate}
\end{equation}
More generally, for $K\ge3$ the size bound $n_a\ge n/(\beta K)$ gives
\[
 \E\Mis(\widehat z,z)
 \le \frac{C}{\sqrt{np}}\exp\!\left\{-\frac{nI^*}{\beta K}\right\}.
\]
For $K=2$, the bound is $C\exp\{-nI^*/2\}/\sqrt{np}$;
its exponent does not involve $\beta$. These are consequences of the same
Zhou--Li affinity bound, specialized to the planted-partition model.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For equal community sizes, the two differing blocks interchange
$p_{\mathrm{in}}$ and $p_{\mathrm{out}}$, so the Chernoff optimizer
before removing the self-loop coordinate is $1/2$ and its information is
$nI^*/K$. Removing one coordinate changes this information by $O(1)$
uniformly under the model conditions. In general, evaluating the
Chernoff divergence at $1/2$ gives
$D^*_{i,ab}\ge(n_a+n_b)I^*/2-O(1)$.
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:main-recovery}, using $n_a+n_b\ge2n/(\beta K)$,
and $n_a+n_b=n$ when $K=2$.
The information $I^*$ is the usual R\'enyi quantity in the community
detection literature~\citep{gao2017}; the displayed bounds retain the
polynomial factor in the Zhou--Li oracle scale.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Likelihood-based initialization supplies the required accuracy}
For fitted profiles $\mathcal C=(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_K)$ define
\begin{align}
 D(x\Vert y)&=\sum_j\left\{x_j\log\frac{x_j}{y_j}
                  +(1-x_j)\log\frac{1-x_j}{1-y_j}\right\},
 \label{eq:profile-divergence}\\
 G(\mathcal C)&=\sum_i\min_kD(q_i\Vert\mu_k),
 &G_*(\mathcal C)&=\sum_an_a\min_kD(p_{a*}\Vert\mu_k),\notag\\
 \mathcal F(\widehat\pi,\mathcal C)
    &=-\sum_i\log\sum_k\widehat\pi_k e^{-D(q_i\Vert\mu_k)}.
 \label{eq:soft-loss}
\end{align}
Writing $S_q=\sum_i\ell_i(q_i)$, the exact identities and inequalities
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L=S_q-\mathcal F,\qquad
 G\le\mathcal F,\qquad
 \mathcal F(K^{-1}\one,\mathcal C)\le G+n\log K
 \label{eq:soft-hard}
\end{equation}
connect the algorithm's objective selection to a fitting-loss certificate.

\begin{lemma}[Population replacement contraction]
\label{lem:population-replacement}
For every list of $K$ fitted profiles, some replacement by a true profile satisfies
\begin{equation}
 G_*(\mathcal C^{r\leftarrow p_{a*}})
 \le(1-1/K)G_*(\mathcal C).
 \label{eq:population-contraction}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose a minimizing fitted profile for each true class and a class contributing at least $G_*/K$. If the chosen-profile map is not onto, delete an unused profile; otherwise the map is a bijection and delete the chosen profile of this class. Inserting its true profile removes that class's loss, while all other classes retain a minimizing old profile.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Empirical replacement gives a low-loss certificate]
\label{thm:replacement-contraction}
On the spectral event in Lemma~\ref{lem:full-spectral}, the replacement rounds of Algorithm~1 satisfy, uniformly in the round,
\begin{align}
 \mathcal F_{t+1}
 &\le(1-1/K)\mathcal F_t+Cn\sqrt{np}+n\log K,
 \label{eq:replacement-contraction}\\
 \frac{\mathcal F_{T_n}}{n^2p}
 &\le C(1-1/K)^{T_n}
       +\frac{C}{\sqrt{np}}+\frac{K\log K}{np}=o(1),
 \qquad T_n=\lceil\log n\rceil.
 \label{eq:replacement-weak-loss}
\end{align}
The statement holds for every starting fit in the common profile box and all simplex weights, including zero weights. A complete round without improvement also certifies $\mathcal F=o(n^2p)$. No trial is required to converge to a stationary point or a global optimum.
\end{theorem}

\begin{lemma}[Low loss certifies the refined responsibility radius]
\label{lem:low-loss-responsibilities}
On the same spectral event, consider any fit with exactly $K$ profiles in the common box and $\mathcal F\le\xi_n n^2p$, where $\xi_n\to0$ is deterministic and uniform. After a common permutation, its profiles satisfy $\max_a\|\mu_a-p_{a*}\|_1=o(np)$, its weights satisfy $-\log\min_a\widehat\pi_a=o(np)$, and its own exact E-step satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \frac1n\sum_{i,a}|R_{ia}-Z_{ia}|\le\frac{C}{np}.
 \label{eq:weak-responsibilities}
\end{equation}
These conclusions hold simultaneously for all such fits, including data-dependent fits.
\end{lemma}

The objective certificate is stronger than merely weak recovery. It makes the selected fit's existing final E-step accurate to $O((np)^{-1})$, so the mandatory final M-step already meets the sharp likelihood comparison below. Repeated replacement supplies this certificate regardless of the preceding growing trajectory. The proof does not assert general-$K$ convergence of growing alone.

\subsection{How much spectral compression changes the LRT}
Let $M_0=ZPZ^\top$, $M=\E[A\mid z]$, $\Pi=Z(Z^\top Z)^{-1}Z^\top$, and $\widehat\Pi=UU^\top$.

\begin{lemma}[Spectral estimates with sufficient confidence]
\label{lem:full-spectral}
For every fixed $H>0$, there is an event $G_H$ with $\PP(G_H^c)\le C_H e^{-Hnp}$ on which
\begin{align}
 \|A-M\|_{\mathrm{op}}&\le C_H\sqrt{np},&
 \|U\|_{2\to\infty}&\le C_H/\sqrt n,&
 \|\widehat\Pi-\Pi\|_{\mathrm{op}}&\le C_H/\sqrt{np},
 \label{eq:spectral-input}\\
 \|\widetilde A-M_0\|_{\mathrm F}^2
   +\|q-M_0\|_{\mathrm F}^2&\le C_Hnp,&
 \max_i\deg(i)&\le C_Hnp,&
 \kappa np/4&\le s\le2np.
 \label{eq:general-profile-errors}
\end{align}
The retained eigenvalues have magnitudes of order $np$, and the true and observed profiles lie in a common box
\begin{equation}
 [c_Hp,\,\min\{C_Hp,1-b_H\}]^n,
 \qquad b_H>0.
 \label{eq:general-profile-box}
\end{equation}
Every true probability is a distance at least $c_Hp$ from both clipping endpoints. Each graph obtained by deleting a specified vertex has the corresponding norm, eigenspace and row-norm bounds, with failure probability at most $C_H e^{-Hnp}$ and constants uniform in the deleted vertex.
\end{lemma}

The exponential confidence is essential at densities where $I_n\gg\log n$. An arbitrary fixed inverse-polynomial failure probability could dominate the target risk. Appendix~\ref{app:general-full-spectral} verifies this confidence using Bandeira and van Handel~\citep[Corollary 3.12 and Remark 3.13]{bandeiravhandel2016} and Abbe et al.~\citep[Theorem 2.1 and Lemma 7]{abbe2020}.

The link to the oracle LRT uses a vertex-deleted graph only in the proof. Conditional on $B=A_{-i,-i}$, write $x=A_{-i,i}$ and let $\Pi_B$ be the retained projector of $B$. A deterministic arrowhead expansion gives, for every bounded weight vector $w$,
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde A_iw=x^\top\Pi_Bw_{-i}+O(1)
 \label{eq:main-arrowhead}
\end{equation}
on the degree and vertex-deleted spectral events. For a bounded block-constant $w$ and every fixed bounded tilt parameter, the difference between the conditional log moment generating functions of $x^\top\Pi_Bw_{-i}$ and $x^\top w_{-i}$ is $O(1)$. This is the relevant sense in which the lost edge-count information is small: at the testing boundary it changes the exponential tail by at most a constant factor. It need not make individual projected counts accurate to less than $1/2$.

Clipping and fitted parameters require further control. Once the responsibilities have error $O((np)^{-1})$, fitting changes the true-profile spectral score by at most $C(1+\mathcal G)$, where $\mathcal G$ is a normalized global block fluctuation with bounded exponential moments. Clipping is handled by at most $2^K$ linear branches, rather than by claiming a uniform bounded difference between the final score and the raw LRT. Every branch that can cause an error has tilted variance of order $np$. Its probability in any interval of fixed length is $O((np)^{-1/2})$, which preserves the oracle prefactor as well as the Chernoff exponent.

\begin{theorem}[Local refinement from weak responsibilities]
\label{thm:local-spectral-em}
Suppose a spectrum-based responsibility matrix has aligned average error at most a deterministic $\gamma_n\to0$ outside an event of probability at most $C_H e^{-Hnp}$, for sufficiently large fixed $H$. One M-step, one E-step and a second M-step, followed by classification, give \eqref{eq:main-rate}. If its aligned responsibility error is already at most $C/(np)$, a single M-step suffices. The conclusion remains valid after additional finite exact E/M updates and finite data-dependent stopping rules.
\end{theorem}

For Algorithm~1 the low-loss certificate gives the stronger premise directly, so its stated final E/M update suffices. The proof in Appendices~\ref{app:replacement-proof} and~\ref{app:general-local-em} bounds the effect of compression on the LRT tail, including its $(np)^{-1/2}$ prefactor. This establishes \eqref{eq:main-rate} without recovering the integer edge counts.

\subsection{Why the complete nonedge term matters}
The sparse score used in earlier experiments is
$\sum_jq_{ij}\log\widehat p_j-\sum_j\widehat p_j$.
When $p\to0$, its oracle contrast differs from the complete Bernoulli contrast by
$O\{p\deg(i)+np^2\}=o(np)$
on the appropriate degree event. This approximation alone does not establish the Zhou--Li bound in \eqref{eq:main-rate}.

At constant probabilities, dropping the nonedge term changes the testing rate. In the balanced model
\[
 P=\begin{pmatrix}0.8&0.2\\0.2&0.2\end{pmatrix},
\]
only edges to the first block distinguish the hypotheses. For $m$ such potential neighbors, the exact LRT threshold is $N/m=1/2$, with information $0.22314355\,m$. The sparse score instead uses $N/m=0.6/\log4$, whose smaller directional error exponent is $0.13905314\,m$. Algorithm~1 retains the complete Bernoulli score throughout the density range.

\section{The general degree-plus-spectrum algorithm}
\label{sec:degree-spectral}

The preceding sections first treat the easier case in which the raw adjacency
spectrum can be used directly. The general representation retains the degree
vector as well as a regularized spectrum. Keeping the degrees supplies the
constant direction in a likelihood contrast exactly; regularization addresses
the influence of high-degree vertices on the estimated contrast directions.
This section specifies that algorithm and the sharp testing comparison it
requires. The exact identities and updates below do not require a logarithmic
lower bound on the expected degree. For its statistical analysis we retain the
balance, probability-comparability, full-rank, and upper-probability bounds in
\eqref{eq:model-conditions}, with $p\le1-\eta$, and consider
\begin{equation}
 np\longrightarrow\infty
 \label{eq:general-density}
\end{equation}
in place of $np=\Omega(\log n)$. Theorem~\ref{thm:main-recovery} remains the
proved unconditional sharp guarantee for Algorithm~1 under its stated
logarithmic-density assumption; its proof does not automatically establish
the corresponding guarantee for the algorithm of this section.

\subsection{Degree-preserving spectral compression}
Write
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 D=A\one,\qquad \overline D=\frac1n\sum_iD_i,\qquad
 \tau=2\overline D,\\
 W=\diag(w_1,\ldots,w_n),\qquad
 w_i=\begin{cases}
 \min\{1,\tau/D_i\},&D_i>0,\\
 1,&D_i=0.
 \end{cases}
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:degree-regularization}
\end{equation}
Every diagonal entry of $W$ is positive: a graph with an edge has
$\overline D>0$, and the all-zero graph has $W=I$. Form the softly
degree-capped matrix $A_W=WAW$, and retain its $K$ orthonormal eigenvectors
with largest absolute eigenvalues, together with their signed eigenvalues:
\begin{equation}
 A_WU=U\Lambda,\qquad U^\top U=I_K.
 \label{eq:general-eigenpairs}
\end{equation}
After this preprocessing, only $(D,U,\Lambda)$ are retained. In particular,
$W$ is recoverable from $D$, and every subsequent operation uses these
retained quantities.

Define the two $n\times(K+1)$ matrices
\begin{equation}
 X=[\one,\,WU],\qquad
 Y=[D,\,W^{-1}U\Lambda],\qquad
 R=YX^\dagger,
 \label{eq:general-reconstruction}
\end{equation}
where $X^\dagger$ is the Moore--Penrose inverse. This definition allows a
linearly dependent column, for example when the constant direction already
belongs to $\operatorname{col}(WU)$. A rank-revealing factorization can be
used in its computation. The matrix $R$ is a reconstruction of the edge
evidence, rather than an estimated matrix of independent edge probabilities.

\begin{proposition}[Exact degree and block-count identities]
\label{prop:degree-count-identities}
For every adjacency matrix, the retained data in
\eqref{eq:general-reconstruction} determine
\begin{equation}
 AX=Y,\qquad R=A\Pi_X,\qquad R\one=D,
 \qquad \Pi_X=XX^\dagger.
 \label{eq:degree-identities}
\end{equation}
For any candidate partition $e$, let $Z(e)$ be its membership matrix and set
$\widetilde b(e)=RZ(e)$. Its rows obey
\begin{equation}
 \sum_c\widetilde b_{ic}(e)=D_i,\qquad
 \widetilde b(e)-b(e)=-A(I-\Pi_X)Z(e),\qquad b(e)=AZ(e).
 \label{eq:general-block-counts}
\end{equation}
If $\operatorname{col}(Z(e))\subseteq\operatorname{col}(X)$, then
$\widetilde b(e)=b(e)$ exactly. In particular this conclusion applies to
the true partition whenever its membership space is contained in the
retained augmented space.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Multiplying \eqref{eq:general-eigenpairs} by $W^{-1}$ gives
$A(WU)=W^{-1}U\Lambda$, while $A\one=D$. Hence $AX=Y$ and
$YX^\dagger=AXX^\dagger=A\Pi_X$. Since $\one\in\operatorname{col}(X)$,
the orthogonal projector $\Pi_X$ fixes $\one$, proving the degree identity.
Multiplication by $Z(e)$ gives the count identity and its error formula.
If $\Pi_XZ(e)=Z(e)$, the error is zero.
\end{proof}

The degree identity is exact even when the empirical subspace does not
contain the true membership space. The separate condition for exact block
counts is not asserted to hold for the empirical eigenvectors. In general,
$\widetilde b_{ic}(e)$ is real-valued and may lie outside its binomial count
range; no rounding guarantee follows. What the degree addition restores
unconditionally is the sum of these counts, namely the observed degree.
Also, $R=A\Pi_X$ need not be symmetric. Symmetrizing it would generally
change its degree vector to $(D+\Pi_XD)/2$. Neither symmetrization nor
entrywise clipping of $R$ is part of the construction.

\subsection{One Bernoulli working criterion for growing and refinement}
Set
\begin{equation}
 \varepsilon_D=\frac{c_0\max\{1,\overline D\}}n,
 \qquad
 \widehat p_a\in[\varepsilon_D,1-\varepsilon_D]^n,
 \label{eq:degree-profile-floor}
\end{equation}
using the same admissible fixed $c_0$ as in \eqref{eq:profiles}. For
$n\ge2$, $\overline D\le n-1$ makes this interval nonempty. The complete
Bernoulli working row score is
\begin{equation}
 \ell_i^D(\widehat p)
 =\sum_{j=1}^n\{R_{ij}\log\widehat p_j
             +(1-R_{ij})\log(1-\widehat p_j)\}.
 \label{eq:degree-row-score}
\end{equation}
Every logarithm is well-defined because the fitted profiles are bounded
away from zero and one. The data coefficients $R_{ij}$ themselves are not
clipped. Thus \eqref{eq:degree-row-score} is an algebraic Bernoulli fitting
criterion, not a probability density for signed observations. As with
Algorithm~1, its reconstructed diagonal is retained; comparison to the
loopless oracle must account for that coordinate in the score remainder.

For $v_j=\log\{\widehat p_j/(1-\widehat p_j)\}$ and
$\overline v=n^{-1}\sum_jv_j$, the degree contribution is explicit:
\begin{equation}
 \ell_i^D(\widehat p)
   =D_i\overline v+R_i(v-\overline v\one)
      +\sum_j\log(1-\widehat p_j).
 \label{eq:degree-contrast-decomposition}
\end{equation}
The first term retains the total-degree evidence exactly. Only the
centered contrast directions are approximated by the augmented spectral
space. The last term keeps the nonedge contribution. Adding the degree
vector does not by itself control the error in the centered contrasts at
the oracle testing boundary.

Use the mixture objective and decoder in \eqref{eq:mixture-and-decode},
with $\ell_i^D$ replacing $\ell_i$. Candidate profiles and the initial
global mean are, respectively,
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{clip}(R_c,\varepsilon_D,1-\varepsilon_D),
 \qquad
 \operatorname{clip}\left(\frac1n\sum_iR_i,
                     \varepsilon_D,1-\varepsilon_D\right).
 \label{eq:degree-candidates}
\end{equation}
Growing and replacement use the same positive likelihood gains as in
\eqref{eq:growing-gain} and \eqref{eq:replacement-gain}, evaluated with
this score and these candidates. They require no $L^2$ or $K$-means
initialization. The E-step and weight update are unchanged. To avoid
confusing responsibilities with the reconstructed matrix $R$, denote the
responsibilities here by $r_{ia}$ and write $N_a=\sum_i r_{ia}$. The exact
constrained profile M-step is
\begin{equation}
 \widehat p_{aj}^{\mathrm{new}}
  =\operatorname{clip}\left(
      \frac{\sum_i r_{ia}R_{ij}}{N_a},
      \varepsilon_D,1-\varepsilon_D\right),
 \qquad \widehat\pi_a^{\mathrm{new}}=N_a/n.
 \label{eq:degree-mstep}
\end{equation}
A zero-mass component retains its previous profile. This is an exact
maximizer even when the weighted average is outside $[0,1]$: the derivative
of its scalar objective has the sign of
$\sum_i r_{ia}R_{ij}/N_a-\widehat p_{aj}$. Consequently each E/M pair
increases the same working mixture objective. This algebraic monotonicity
does not establish the replacement contraction or the statistical fitting
certificate of Section~\ref{sec:main-results}; those require separate
bounds for the new representation.

\begin{center}
\fbox{\begin{minipage}{0.93\linewidth}
\textbf{Algorithm 2: Degree-plus-spectrum likelihood decoding}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Compute $D$, $W$, and the retained eigenpairs of $WAW$ using
\eqref{eq:degree-regularization}--\eqref{eq:general-eigenpairs}. Retain only
$(D,U,\Lambda)$.
\item Form $X$, $Y$, and the degree-preserving reconstruction $R=YX^\dagger$.
Keep $R$ unchanged; constrain fitted and candidate profiles using
\eqref{eq:degree-profile-floor}--\eqref{eq:degree-candidates}.
\item Apply the global-mean growing, joint E/M fits, and
$\lceil\log n\rceil$ replacement rounds of Algorithm~1, using
\eqref{eq:degree-row-score} and \eqref{eq:degree-mstep} throughout.
\item Perform the mandatory final E-step and constrained profile/weight
M-step, then decode by the fitted weighted row scores.
\end{enumerate}
\end{minipage}}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Relation to a center-based block-count decoder.}
The earlier degree-assisted coordinate construction can also be written
using an invertible $K\times K$ matrix $H$ of candidate block coordinates:
\[
 b^{(0)}=W^{-1}U\Lambda H^{-1},\qquad
 b=b^{(0)}+(D-b^{(0)}\one)\pi^\top,
 \qquad \pi^\top\one=1.
\]
For example, blockwise averages of the rows of $WU$ supply candidate rows
of $H$ when that matrix is nonsingular, and estimated block proportions
supply $\pi$. The correction makes $b\one=D$. It depends on the chosen
coordinates and proportions; it is not asserted to equal the canonical
projected counts $RZ(e)$ in \eqref{eq:general-block-counts}. Algorithm~2
uses the latter reconstruction and the common free-profile row criterion,
so no inverse of an estimated block-coordinate matrix is required.

\subsection{The sharp testing comparison needed below logarithmic degree}
The desired conclusion is the same Zhou--Li bound
$\E\Mis\le C\eta^*$, rather than a different rate for the sparse case.
Here $\eta^*$ retains its exact definition in \eqref{eq:oracle-affinity}.
The following sufficient comparison isolates the missing analytic task.
It applies to a fitted decoder, including Algorithm~2 if its conditions
can be verified, without imposing $np\gtrsim\log n$.

\begin{proposition}[A sufficient sharp-transfer comparison]
\label{prop:general-sharp-transfer}
Assume \eqref{eq:model-conditions}, $p\le1-\eta$, and
\eqref{eq:general-density}. Suppose the fitted components can be matched
to the true labels by a common permutation. For each true label $a=z_i$
and each $b\ne a$, let $\widehat L_i^{ab}$ be their fitted score gap,
including the fitted log-weight contrast, under this matching. Put
$B=A_{-i,-i}$ and $x=A_{i,-i}$. All the conditions below are uniform in
$(i,a,b)$; their constants are fixed.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item There is a $B$-measurable good event $G$ and a possibly
full-graph event $E$ with
$\PP_a(G^c)+\PP_a(E^c)\le C\eta^*$. On $G\cap E$,
\[
 \widehat L_i^{ab}=S_B(x)+\rho_B(x),\qquad
 S_B(x)=u(B)+\sum_{j\ne i}x_jv_j(B),\qquad
 |\rho_B(x)|\le h(B),
\]
where $h\ge0$, $u$, and $v$ are $B$-measurable.
\item Let $\alpha=\alpha^*_{i,ab}$ be the oracle Chernoff optimizer. On
$G$, the conditional incident-row log moment generating function satisfies
\[
 \psi_B(\alpha)=\log\E_a[e^{-\alpha S_B(x)}\mid B]
       \le-D^*_{i,ab}+c(B).
\]
The conditional expectation here is over the full product-Bernoulli
incident-row law, without conditioning on $E$.
\item Under the conditional tilted product law
\[
 Q_B(x)=\exp\{-\alpha S_B(x)-\psi_B(\alpha)\}
                 \varphi_{i,a}(x),
\]
one has, on $G$,
\[
 \sup_{t\in\R}Q_B\{S_B(x)\in[t,t+1]\}
       \le \frac{C}{\sqrt{np}}.
\]
\item The remainder and log moment generating function cost obey
$\E[\one_G\exp\{c(B)+\alpha h(B)\}]\le C$.
\end{enumerate}
Then the decoder satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \E\Mis(\widehat z,z)\le C\eta^*.
 \label{eq:general-conditional-rate}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The uniform model bounds imply that $\alpha$ stays away from zero and one
and that the oracle affinity comparison in \eqref{eq:oracle-risk} holds
whenever $np\to\infty$; these oracle facts do not require uniform
raw-adjacency concentration. On $G\cap E$, a wrong pairwise comparison
requires $S_B\le h(B)$. After dropping the indicator of $E$, exponential
tilting and a decomposition into the intervals
$(h-k-1,h-k]$, $k=0,1,\ldots$, give
\begin{align*}
 \PP_a\{S_B\le h(B)\mid B\}
 &=e^{\psi_B(\alpha)}
      \E_{Q_B}[e^{\alpha S_B}\one\{S_B\le h(B)\}]\\
 &\le \frac{C e^{-D^*_{i,ab}+c(B)+\alpha h(B)}}{\sqrt{np}}
           \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}e^{-\alpha k}.
\end{align*}
The geometric sum is uniformly bounded. Integrating over $B\in G$,
using (iv), and adding the exceptional probabilities proves a bound by
$C\eta_{i,ab}+C\eta^*\le C\eta^*$ for the pairwise error. A union bound
over the fixed number of competing labels and averaging over vertices
prove \eqref{eq:general-conditional-rate}. The same common matching can
be used for all vertices; taking the minimum over permutations in
$\Mis$ can only reduce the error.
\end{proof}

The proposition asks for an affine leave-one-out representation, control
of its tilted log moment generating function, a local concentration
bound, and an exponential-moment bound on the remaining score error.
These conditions concern the retained evidence near the testing boundary;
they do not assume the desired misclassification bound. Crucially, the
degree-dependent matrix $W$, inverse reweighting, fitted profiles, and
profile constraints must all be included when verifying them. The
raw-spectrum proof and an earlier degree-calibration construction do not
verify these conditions for the full regularized Algorithm~2 under
\eqref{eq:general-density}. Thus this section restores the general
degree-plus-spectrum algorithm and a precise sharp-rate target, while
keeping the proved logarithmic-density theorem distinct from the remaining
sparse-case analysis.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
The experiments address finite-sample decoding and initialization. They do not assume exact reconstruction of the integer community edge counts, and they do not estimate an asymptotic ratio to the oracle risk. We report two distinct numerical collections. A 60-graph benchmark evaluates
the current full-Bernoulli Algorithm~1: global-mean growing followed by
at most $\lceil\log n\rceil$ rounds of deterministic parameter replacement
and a mandatory final M-step. The earlier 240-graph collection evaluates
historical sparse-score procedures and is preserved under its original
protocol. The two collections use different scores, clipping rules, and
fitting budgets; their results are reported separately. We also give an
exactly certified population example explaining why a guarantee for growing
alone cannot permit arbitrary finite intermediate EM budgets.

\subsection{Current full-Bernoulli decoder: design and comparisons}
The full-score design contains $n=320$ vertices and
$K\in\{2,3,4,5,6\}$, three matrix families, two density settings, and two
independent graph seeds per cell, giving $5\times3\times2\times2=60$ graphs.
The families are a balanced assortative model, an unequal-size
disassortative model, and an unequal-size weak general model. Their block
matrices are entrywise positive, symmetric, and full rank. The second family
has negative signal eigenvalues; the third includes directions with weak
finite-sample separation. The exact matrices, block sizes, and seeds are
fixed in the saved protocol.

Each probability matrix is scaled so that the average expected degree,
computed using the actual integer block sizes and excluding self-loops,
equals either $3\log n$ or $0.18(n-1)$. Expected degrees may differ between
blocks. Both density settings use the same decoder and score; no adjacency
regularization or edge-based refinement is applied.

Every method receives the same $K$ raw adjacency eigenpairs with largest
absolute eigenvalues. The full-score and sparse-score controls use exactly
the same clipped reconstruction $q$. For this audit the clipping endpoints
are $\varepsilon$ and $1-\varepsilon$, where
\[
 \varepsilon=10^{-4}\frac{\max\{1,\max_a|\Lambda_{aa}|\}}n.
\]
The reconstructed diagonal is retained. Thus the score-family comparison
changes the working criterion while keeping the spectral information and
clipping identical. It is not a comparison of graph likelihoods computed
from different observations.

For each score family, the growing fit takes at most 100 EM updates at
each stage, with unchanged labels and soft-loss change at most $10^{-9}n$
required for early stopping. Replacement tests every possible component
removal, chooses its new node profile by the all-node likelihood gain,
resets the trial weights uniformly, and takes one M-step. At most
$\lceil\log 320\rceil=6$ rounds are allowed. The incumbent is retained unless
a trial improves the same soft loss; if none improves it, repeating the
unchanged deterministic round would give the same result. Both the growing
baseline and the replacement decoder receive one final M-step before
classification. The weight updates in this new audit are the unconstrained
exact EM updates. These settings differ from the historical floored-weight
and one-pass replacement controls below.

The Euclidean baselines run ten Lloyd starts on $\sqrt n U$ and
$\sqrt n U\Lambda$, respectively, with at most 300 iterations per start.
Ground truth is used to generate and evaluate the graphs; it is not passed
to fitting, candidate ranking, likelihood selection, or stopping. Detailed
entry points and data locations are given in Appendix~\ref{app:implementation}.

\subsection{A certified finite-stage population failure of growing}
\label{sec:population_growing_failure}
An explicit five-block example distinguishes convergence of an EM objective
from a guarantee for arbitrary finite intermediate fitting budgets. For this
population diagnostic only, write
\begin{equation}
 P=\frac d n
 \begin{pmatrix}
 .0071&.0914&.0119&.0129&.0132\\
 .0914&2.7252&.2538&.2125&.2722\\
 .0119&.2538&.0285&.0276&.0300\\
 .0129&.2125&.0276&.0294&.0289\\
 .0132&.2722&.0300&.0289&.0321
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \pi=(.02,.57,.065,.22,.125).
 \label{eq:population_growing_example}
\end{equation}
Here $d\to\infty$ controls the probability scale of this example, with
$d/n\to0$; it introduces no additional assumption on the main model and
does not require equal block degrees. The displayed shape matrix is positive
definite: the leading principal determinants after multiplying it by
$10^4$ are exactly $71$, $1099496$, $22194480$, $49511806$, and $22302154$.

Use population block profiles, the archived sparse working score, the exact
weight update with floor $10^{-8}$, and one M-step after each added center.
With block indices starting at one, the growing procedure selects blocks
$4,1,5,4$. Its limiting centers are
\[
 (p_{2*},p_{4*},p_{1*},m_{35},p_{4*}),\qquad
 m_{35}=\frac{\pi_3p_{3*}+\pi_5p_{5*}}{\pi_3+\pi_5}.
\]
The fourth center merges blocks 3 and 5, while two centers duplicate block
4. Consequently the limiting mislabeled fraction is $13/200=0.065$.
After the penultimate M-step, block 3 has changed its preferred component,
but the previous mixed center still contains it. The next global gain then
selects block 4 again. The old mixed component has exponentially small
responsibilities whose normalized M-step is itself dominated by block 4;
setting these responsibilities to numerical zero is not needed for the
failure. Excluding selected node IDs also does not exclude other nodes of
the same block.

The limiting candidate comparisons and assignments are certified with exact
rational intervals for logarithms, using an explicit series remainder. The
smallest candidate-selection margin is greater than $7.98\times10^{-7}$;
the vanishing component's soft M-step has a unique dominating block with
margin greater than $7.58\times10^{-4}$. Numerical calculations that normalize
the M-step in log space reproduce error $0.065$ for
$d=10^5,10^6,10^8,10^{10},10^{12}$, including 100 additional final EM updates.
One deterministic replacement round, with one M-step per proposal, removes
a duplicate center and inserts block 3, reducing the error to zero. At
$d=10^6$, the centered per-node working log likelihood rises from
$-17.505006$ to $-1.169355$.

This is a population counterexample to a guarantee allowing just one
intermediate M-step. It does not establish failure when every growing stage
is fitted to convergence: two M-steps per stage succeed in this same example.
It is also not a sampled-graph failure theorem. The complete matrix, strict
comparison certificates, score traces, and successful replacement trials are
in \path{experiments/20261010_general_density/population_audit/}.
The general-$K$ guarantee for Algorithm~1 instead uses the deterministic
replacement argument in Theorem~\ref{thm:main-recovery}, without assuming
that growing alone has already found every community.

\subsection{Historical sparse-score collection: models and protocol}

The earlier 240-graph collection uses the sparse working score and its original fitting settings. It is retained as historical evidence; these graphs were not rerun with the new full-Bernoulli Algorithm~1. For this archived design, let $C^{(0)}$ be a fixed symmetric connectivity matrix and
let $\pi$ be the vector of community proportions. We generate an undirected
SBM without self-loops, with edge probabilities
\begin{equation}
  P_{ab}=\frac\log n{n}C_{ab},\qquad
  C=\frac{\eta}{D_{\min}(C^{(0)},\pi)}C^{(0)},
  \label{eq:experiment_normalization}
\end{equation}
where $\eta\in\{0.8,1.4\}$ and
\begin{equation}
 D_{\min}(C,\pi)=\min_{a\ne b}\max_{0\le t\le1}
  \sum_{c=1}^K\pi_c\left[(1-t)C_{ac}+tC_{bc}
                          -C_{ac}^{1-t}C_{bc}^t\right].
\end{equation}
Thus the limiting minimum Chernoff--Hellinger coefficient equals $\eta$.
The normalization uses the limiting proportions $\pi$; actual group sizes
are $\lfloor n\pi_a\rfloor$, with the remaining vertices assigned to the
last group. Vertex labels are then randomly permuted. Each model is tested
at $n\in\{1000,2000\}$. The six models are
\begin{align*}
 C^{(0)}_{\mathrm{sym2}}&=
 \begin{pmatrix}4&1\\1&4\end{pmatrix},
 &&\pi_{\mathrm{sym2}}=(1/2,1/2),\\[2mm]
 C^{(0)}_{\mathrm{equal3}}&=
 \begin{pmatrix}10&1&2\\1&11&1\\2&1&10\end{pmatrix},
 &&\pi_{\mathrm{equal3}}=(1/3,1/3,1/3),\\[2mm]
 C^{(0)}_{\mathrm{hetero3}}&=
 \begin{pmatrix}6&0.5&0.5\\0.5&12&0.5\\0.5&0.5&24\end{pmatrix},
 &&\pi_{\mathrm{hetero3}}=(1/3,1/3,1/3),\\[2mm]
 C^{(0)}_{\mathrm{unequal3}}&=
 \begin{pmatrix}10&1&1\\1&10&1\\1&1&10\end{pmatrix},
 &&\pi_{\mathrm{unequal3}}=(0.5,0.3,0.2),\\[2mm]
 C^{(0)}_{\mathrm{hier4}}&=
 \begin{pmatrix}12&2&0.5&0.5\\2&12&0.5&0.5\\
                 0.5&0.5&12&2\\0.5&0.5&2&12\end{pmatrix},
 &&\pi_{\mathrm{hier4}}=(1/4,1/4,1/4,1/4),\\[2mm]
 C^{(0)}_{\mathrm{dis3}}&=
 \begin{pmatrix}0.5&8&8\\8&0.5&8\\8&8&0.5\end{pmatrix},
 &&\pi_{\mathrm{dis3}}=(1/3,1/3,1/3).
\end{align*}
These include unequal group sizes, heterogeneous expected degrees,
hierarchical connections, and negative signal eigenvalues.

The design batch contains five graph seeds, $20000,\ldots,20004$, for every
model, size, and signal coefficient, giving $6\times2\times2\times5=120$
graphs. A second batch uses the same models and hyperparameters with fresh
seeds $30000,\ldots,30004$. The algorithms and second-batch seed range were
fixed before fitting the second batch. The second batch therefore validates
initialization choices made using the first batch, rather than introducing
new models. All methods are evaluated on identical eigenpairs within a graph.

We retain the $K$ eigenpairs of the raw adjacency matrix with largest absolute
eigenvalues. The implementation requests $K+1$ eigenpairs using the symmetric
ARPACK solver with tolerance $10^{-8}$ and discards the last one after
sorting by absolute value. After this preprocessing, the fitting routines receive
only $(U,\Lambda)$; adjacency entries, degrees, true labels, and true block
probabilities are unavailable to fitting and model selection.

\subsection{Historical sparse-score implementations and comparisons}

The archived implementation forms positive spectral profiles
\begin{equation}
 q_{ij}=\max\left\{(U\Lambda U^\top)_{ij},
                   10^{-4}\frac\log n{n}\right\},\qquad
 \ell_i^{\mathrm{sp}}(\widehat p)=\sum_{j=1}^n q_{ij}\log\widehat p_j-\sum_{j=1}^n\widehat p_j.
 \label{eq:experimental_working_score}
\end{equation}
The reconstructed diagonal is retained, even though the generated graphs
have zero diagonal. Thus this is a declared positive-profile working score,
not the original Bernoulli graph likelihood. Component means are unrestricted
$n$-dimensional profile means. Every likelihood arm in this historical collection uses the same soft EM
updates and observed working objective
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{sp}}(\widehat\pi,\widehat p)=\sum_{i=1}^n\log\left[
   \sum_{a=1}^K \widehat\pi_a\exp\{\ell_i^{\mathrm{sp}}(\widehat p_a)\}\right].
\end{equation}
EM initializes equal component weights except when refitting a replacement
proposal, where the current weights are retained. The mean update is the
responsibility-weighted average of $q_i$. The weight update exactly maximizes
the EM auxiliary objective on $\{\widehat\pi_a\ge10^{-8},\ \sum_a \widehat\pi_a=1\}$.
No additional floor on means is needed because the observations are positive.
Final labels maximize $\log \widehat\pi_a+\ell_i^{\mathrm{sp}}(\widehat p_a)$.

The historical growing arm uses the global-mean and all-node-gain structure of Section~\ref{sec:growing-branch}, with the archived sparse score and clipping rule above. It is recorded as
\texttt{growing\_global\_gain\_EM}. It starts from the global profile mean,
adds $K-1$ node candidates by their all-node marginal gains, and runs joint
EM after each addition. It requires no top-subset size and removes no nodes.

The frozen-profile control, recorded as \texttt{global\_gain\_no\_update},
starts with the historical top-subset rule and then chooses all remaining
node seeds by all-node gains, keeping the profiles frozen until one final EM
fit. Its exact historical subset sizes are recorded in
Appendix~\ref{app:implementation}. It is a different algorithm despite its
identical recovery errors in these experiments.

We compare it with the following alternatives. \emph{Top-subset peeling}
uses the historical first-seed rule and repeatedly removes the selected candidate and its best-scoring subset, using the archived sizes specified in the appendix.
After $K$ selections, all $n$ observations, including unselected nodes, enter
EM. \emph{Peeling plus replacement} fits this initialization, deletes each
component temporarily, proposes the globally most useful node against the
remaining $K-1$ means, and refits all nodes. All $K$ proposals use the same
original fitted endpoint; the best proposal is accepted only if its final
$\mathcal L_{\mathrm{sp}}$ exceeds the current best by more than $10^{-5}$. This is one
replacement pass, not repeated optimization.

In the historical API, \texttt{method='global\_gain'} invokes the growing arm and does not invoke the frozen-profile control. The following randomized procedures are archived comparison arms, not steps of the current Algorithm~1. \emph{Residual++} samples
the first node uniformly and later nodes proportionally to
\begin{equation}
 d_i=\min_{a\in S}\sum_j
 \left[q_{ij}\log(q_{ij}/\widehat p_{aj})-q_{ij}+\widehat p_{aj}\right].
\end{equation}
This deviance is not squared again. Its greedy variant samples
$2+\lfloor\log K\rfloor$ candidate trials per addition and chooses the trial
that minimizes total residual deviance. We report both one start and ten
starts; the ten-start fit is selected solely by final $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{sp}}$.
An additional control selects one set of Euclidean $k$-means++ seeds in
$X=\sqrt n U\Lambda$ and then runs the same profile EM.
The main Euclidean baseline runs $k$-means in $X$ with ten starts, at most
300 Lloyd iterations per start, and tolerance $10^{-5}$.

All profile EM fits use at most 300 updates and tolerance $10^{-6}$, with label, parameter-change and objective-change checks. The exact archived stopping rule is recorded in Appendix~\ref{app:implementation}.
Randomized seeding uses fixed NumPy seed sequences indexed by graph seed,
method, and restart. Reported error rates minimize label disagreement over
all permutations of the $K$ labels. Averages weight graphs equally; ``exact''
counts graphs with no mislabeled node. True labels are accessed only after
all fitting and likelihood-based selections finish.

\subsection{Historical numerical verification and computational scope}

All returned historical profile fits satisfy the declared stopping rule in the 240-graph
comparison. The 120 original peeling errors are reproduced exactly. The
archive contains 6240 EM traces, including replacement proposals, growing
stages, and restart fits, with 27867 recorded trace points. The smallest
observed objective increment is $-1.17\times10^{-10}$, within floating-point
rounding; no increment below $-10^{-7}$ occurs. All 240 selected ten-start
solutions maximize the same final working likelihood within their restart
set. Replacement accepts a likelihood improvement on 22 design graphs and
25 independent graphs, and never lowers the final objective; most of these
small improvements do not change the classification error. Independent
scalar checks reproduce deviance and global-gain calculations with maximum
absolute errors $7.11\times10^{-15}$ and $1.78\times10^{-15}$, respectively.

The present implementation explicitly reconstructs the $n\times n$ profiles
and caches all singleton-candidate scores. Reconstruction costs
$O(n^2K)$, score precomputation costs $O(n^3)$, and storage costs $O(n^2)$.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

An oracle SBM classifier uses community edge counts through their Bernoulli likelihood contrasts. Our first theorem controls the loss caused by an empirical unregularized spectral reconstruction: for $p=\Omega(\log n/n)$ and the stated model conditions,
\[
 \mathbb E\,\Mis(\widehat z,z)\le C\eta^*.
\]
The proof controls likelihood tails after projection, clipping and fitting, including their local concentration under the oracle exponential tilt. The general-$K$ replacement certificate and the algorithm's existing final E/M update supply the required parameter accuracy. This result permits unequal community sizes and expected degrees, intermediate and dense probabilities, and either sign of signal eigenvalues. It requires neither integer count recovery nor a risk ratio tending to one.

The general algorithm retains both observed degrees and the eigenpairs of $WAW$. The identities $A\mathbf1=D$ and $A(WU)=W^{-1}U\Lambda$ permit exact evaluation of $A$ on their augmented span. Its reconstructed block counts preserve every observed degree, and initialization and refinement use one complete Bernoulli row criterion. This construction is defined below logarithmic expected degree. The general transfer proposition preserves the same Zhou--Li bound once its conditional cumulant, local concentration and fitted-score hypotheses hold. Establishing those hypotheses uniformly for the full regularized algorithm when $np\to\infty$ is the remaining sparse-case proof task; the unconditional theorem of the earlier sections is confined to its stated density range.

The numerical results concern the unregularized implementations and illustrate finite-sample behavior, including weak-signal errors and the absence of a classification gain from replacement in the complete 60-graph collection. The general implementation is checked for its exact retained-information identities and EM objective updates. These checks do not establish a sparse statistical rate. Further questions include reducing the cubic candidate-cache cost and quadratic storage, relaxing the rank condition, and obtaining sharp affinity comparisons at vanishing block probabilities.

\section{Oracle testing and spectral estimates}
\label{app:statistical-proofs}
All probabilities and expectations below are under the SBM with the fixed parameter $z$. Set $C_a=\{j:z_j=a\}$, $M_0=ZPZ^\top$, $M=\E[A\mid z]$, and $\Pi=Z(Z^\top Z)^{-1}Z^\top$. The empirical projector is $\widehat\Pi=UU^\top$. In the proofs $\mu_a$ denotes a fitted profile $\widehat p_a$. Constants may depend on the fixed model bounds, $K$, $c_0$, the density lower-bound constant and a fixed confidence parameter $H$, but not on $n$ or a normalized matrix limit. We sometimes write $d=np$ within an argument.

\subsection{Uniform information and the oracle LRT rate}
\label{app:uniform-density-bounds}
For the fixed pair $a,b$ define blockwise weights and nonedge contributions
\[
 w_c^{ab}=\log\frac{P_{ac}(1-P_{bc})}{P_{bc}(1-P_{ac})},\qquad
 r_c^{ab}=\log\frac{1-P_{ac}}{1-P_{bc}}.
\]
Then the LRT in \eqref{eq:oracle-contrast} is
$L_i^{ab}=\sum_{j\ne i}\{A_{ij}w_{z_j}^{ab}+r_{z_j}^{ab}\}$.
We write $\alpha^*_{i,ab}$ for the minimizer of
$\E_a\exp(-\alpha L_i^{ab})$, so its minimum equals $e^{-D^*_{i,ab}}$.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform oracle constants]\label{lem:oracle}
The bounds $D^*_{i,ab}\asymp np$, $\alpha^*_{i,ab}\in[\alpha_0,1-\alpha_0]$, and tilted variance $\asymp np$ hold uniformly over \eqref{eq:model-conditions}--\eqref{eq:density-condition}. The equal-prior binary oracle error is between constant multiples of $e^{-D^*_{i,ab}}/\sqrt{np}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The odds weights are uniformly bounded and, for $u,v\in[\kappa p,p]$ with $p\le1-\eta$, their absolute difference in logits is between $|u-v|/p$ and $|u-v|/(\kappa\eta p)$. Distinct rows of $P$ are separated in Euclidean norm by at least $\sqrt{2}\sigma_{\min}(P)$. Positive community proportions imply
\begin{equation}
 \min_{a\ne b}\|p_{a*}-p_{b*}\|_1\ge cnp,
 \qquad cnp\le D(p_{a*}\Vert p_{b*})\le Cnp.
 \label{eq:uniform-information-scale}
\end{equation}
Indeed, $\|p_{a*}-p_{b*}\|_2^2\ge cnp^2$ and
$\|p_{a*}-p_{b*}\|_\infty\le p$ give the first inequality. Bernoulli entropy curvature at least $1/p$ on the interval between two true probabilities gives the KL lower bound; its upper bound follows from probability comparability and $1-p\ge\eta$. Thus $\sum_c(n_c-\one\{z_i=c\})(w_c^{ab})^2\asymp n$. At any tilt $0\le\alpha\le1$, its Bernoulli probabilities lie between $P_{ac}$ and $P_{bc}$. Their variances are therefore between constant multiples of $p$, so the second derivative of $\psi(\alpha)=\log\E_a e^{-\alpha L_i^{ab}}$ is between $c_1np$ and $C_1np$. Since $\psi(0)=\psi(1)=0$, integrating this curvature gives information $\asymp np$ and an interior optimizer bounded away from both endpoints. Bounded log-odds weights imply that the sum of tilted third absolute centered moments is at most a constant times the tilted variance. The rate-optimal Chernoff bounds of Zhou and Li~\citep{zhouli2020} then give the two-sided oracle rate.
\end{proof}


\subsection{Exponential-confidence spectral bounds}
\label{app:general-full-spectral}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:full-spectral}]
The actual mean $M$ leaves the membership space invariant. In its orthonormal membership basis its restriction is
\[
 (Z^\top Z)^{1/2}P(Z^\top Z)^{1/2}-\operatorname{diag}(P_{11},\ldots,P_{KK});
\]
on the orthogonal complement its eigenvalues are the numbers $-P_{aa}$. Consequently the $K$ signal eigenvalues have magnitudes between $c_1np$ and $C_1np$, their projector is exactly $\Pi$, and their population row norms are $O(n^{-1/2})$.

The bounded-entry matrix norm inequality of Bandeira and van Handel~\citep[Corollary~3.12 and Remark~3.13]{bandeiravhandel2016} (for centered, not necessarily symmetrically distributed entries) gives, after absorbing $\sqrt{\log n}$ into $\sqrt{np}$,
\[
 \PP\{\|A-M\|_{\mathrm{op}}>C\sqrt{np}+t\}\le n e^{-t^2/C}.
\]
Taking $t=C_H\sqrt{np}$ provides any prescribed fixed confidence $e^{-Hnp}$, since $\log n\le c^{-1}np$. A degree Chernoff bound with a sufficiently large fixed multiplier gives the maximum-degree assertion at the same confidence.

For completeness, we verify the confidence parameter in the entrywise eigenspace theorem of Abbe et al.~\citep{abbe2020}, rather than replacing an exponential confidence by a polynomial one. In their Theorem~2.1 use
\[
 \gamma=\frac{C_H}{\sqrt{np}},\qquad
 \varphi_H(x)=\frac{C_H}{1\vee\log(1/x)}\ (x>0),\qquad \varphi_H(0)=0,
 \quad \delta_0,\delta_1\le e^{-Hnp}.
\]
Their Bernoulli row inequality (Lemma~7) holds for every fixed nonnegative row-tail parameter. Choose that parameter sufficiently larger than $H+c^{-1}+1$, where $np\ge c\log n$. It gives per-row failure at most $\delta_1/n$. Applied to each of the fixed number of columns, the monotonicity of $\varphi_H$ and of $\varphi_H(x)/x$ verifies their matrix row-concentration assumption. Factors depending on $K$ and the signal gap are included in $C_H$. Row/column independence follows from the independent upper-triangular edges. Their mean-row condition follows from $\|M\|_{2\to\infty}\le np/\sqrt n$ and $np\le n$. Their smallness condition holds eventually because $\gamma\to0$ and $\varphi_H(\gamma)\to0$.

Apply the theorem separately to the positive and negative signal groups, each separated by a gap of order $np$ from its complement. Its row-norm bound gives $\|U\|_{2\to\infty}\le C_H/\sqrt n$. The theorem does not require $M$ to have exactly rank $K$, so the no-loop diagonal correction is retained. The operator norm event and the signal gap give the asserted projector bound by the usual subspace perturbation inequality. A fixed number of union bounds is absorbed by increasing the confidence parameter in the preceding steps.

The Perron eigenvalue of $M$ lies between its minimum and maximum row sums, which are in $[\kappa(n-1)p,np]$. The norm event gives $\kappa np/4\le s\le2np$ eventually. Thus $\varepsilon_n\asymp p$, every true probability is at distance at least $c'p$ from both clipping endpoints, and
\[
 |\widetilde A_{ij}|\le\|U_i\|_2\|\Lambda\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|U_j\|_2\le C_Hp.
\]
This proves the common box: if $C_Hp\le1/2$ it is bounded away from one directly; otherwise $\varepsilon_n\ge c_Hp\ge c_H/(2C_H)$ gives a fixed upper gap. Removing one vertex preserves all model bounds with adjusted fixed constants, and the same argument proves the vertex-deleted assertions uniformly in $i$. In a per-node risk calculation one only needs the event for that particular deleted vertex.
Finally, best rank-$K$ approximation in operator norm gives
\[
 \|A-\widetilde A\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le\|A-M_0\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le C_H\sqrt{np}.
\]
Since $\widetilde A-M_0$ has rank at most $2K$, this implies
$\|\widetilde A-M_0\|_{\mathrm F}^2\le C_Hnp$.
Clipping toward an interval containing every entry of $M_0$ contracts coordinatewise squared error, and hence gives the stated bound for $q$ as well.
\end{proof}


\section{Likelihood initialization and the refined responsibility radius}
\label{app:replacement-proof}
\subsection{Exact EM and the profile constraint}
For any nonnegative responsibilities with rows summing to one, define
\[
 \mathcal Q(R,\widehat\pi,\widehat p)
 =\sum_{i,a}R_{ia}\{\log\widehat\pi_a+\ell_i(\widehat p_a)-\log R_{ia}\}.
\]
The log-sum inequality gives $\mathcal Q\le\mathcal L$, with equality at the E-step. For fixed $R$, the profile-coordinate objective is
\[
 \left(\sum_iR_{ia}q_{ij}\right)\log\widehat p_{aj}
 +\left(N_a-\sum_iR_{ia}q_{ij}\right)\log(1-\widehat p_{aj}).
\]
Its maximizer for $N_a>0$ is the weighted mean in \eqref{eq:mstep}; maximizing $\sum_aN_a\log\widehat\pi_a$ over the simplex gives $\widehat\pi_a=N_a/n$. Thus every exact E/M pair raises $\mathcal L$ and lowers $\mathcal F$. A zero-mass component contributes nothing to this objective and may retain its previous profile. All nonzero-mass updates are convex combinations of the $q$ rows. These observations also prove that all means remain in the common box on $G_H$.

Since $D(q_i\Vert\widehat p_a)\ge0$, the objective is bounded above by the observation-only saturated score $S_q$. Consequently an infinite exact EM sequence has convergent objective values. This fact alone gives neither a global optimum nor community recovery. The replacement argument below supplies the latter conclusion from a different, finite-round bound.


\subsection{Replacement contracts the common fitting loss}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:replacement-contraction}]
Write $d=np$. The Frobenius bound gives $\sum_i\|q_i-p_{z_i,*}\|_1\le n\|q-M_0\|_{\mathrm F}\le Cn\sqrt d$. Define
\[
 G(\mathcal C)=\sum_i\min_kD(q_i\Vert\mu_k),\qquad
 G_*(\mathcal C)=\sum_an_a\min_kD(p_{a*}\Vert\mu_k).
\]
The scalar divergence derivatives are
\[
 \partial_x D(x\Vert y)=\log\frac{x(1-y)}{y(1-x)},\qquad
 \partial_y D(x\Vert y)=\frac{y-x}{y(1-y)}.
\]
Both are uniformly bounded on the common box. Consequently the profile divergence is uniformly Lipschitz in each argument's $\ell_1$ norm, so $|G-G_*|\le Cn\sqrt d$ simultaneously over every admissible center list. Each true class supplies an observed candidate within $C\sqrt d$ in $\ell_1$ of its true profile.

For any $K$ fitted centers, choose a minimizing center for each true class. A class contributing at least $G_*/K$ exists. If the chosen-center map is not onto, delete an unused center; otherwise it is a bijection, and delete the center chosen by this class. Inserting its true profile removes at least that class's contribution and harms none of the other classes. Thus some ideal replacement decreases population hard loss by at least $G_*/K$. Replacing the ideal profile by its available observed candidate and applying the uniform Lipschitz bounds gives an observed trial with hard loss at most $(1-1/K)G+Cn\sqrt d$.

For each deletion $r$, the saturation identity $\ell_i(\mu)=\ell_i(q_i)-D(q_i\Vert\mu)$ gives
\begin{equation}
 G(\mathcal C^{r\leftarrow q_c})
 =G(\mathcal C\setminus\{\mu_r\})
  -\sum_i(\ell_i(q_c)-h_i^{(-r)})_+.
 \label{eq:gain-hard-loss-identity}
\end{equation}
Thus the implemented maximum positive all-node score gain minimizes exactly the resulting hard loss. All deletions are tested. Uniformly initializing that trial's weights changes its soft loss from hard loss by at most $n\log K$. Exact E/M fitting decreases soft loss; selecting the best trial together with the unchanged fit gives
\[
 \mathcal F_{t+1}\le(1-1/K)\mathcal F_t+Cn\sqrt d+n\log K,
 \qquad \mathcal F_{t+1}\le\mathcal F_t.
\]
Initially $\mathcal F_0\le Cnd$ for arbitrary simplex weights, since all profile divergences are $O(d)$ on the common box. Iterate the recurrence. If a complete round does not improve the fit, the same inequality gives $\mathcal F_t\le K(Cn\sqrt d+n\log K)$ directly.
\end{proof}


\subsection{A deterministic E-step bootstrap}
\begin{lemma}[Frobenius error and responsibility bootstrap]\label{lem:bootstrap}
On the full-graph event of Lemma~\ref{lem:full-spectral},
\[
 \|\widetilde A-M_0\|_{\mathrm F}^2\le Cnp,\qquad \|q-M_0\|_{\mathrm F}^2\le Cnp.
\]
If the fitted profiles have aligned $\ell_1$ error $o(np)$ and $-\log\min_a\widehat\pi_a=o(np)$ (in particular, if the weights are bounded below), their next E-step satisfies
\[
 \frac1n\sum_{i,a}|R^{\rm new}_{ia}-Z_{ia}|\le \frac{C}{np}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Best rank-$K$ approximation in operator norm gives $\|A-\widetilde A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\|A-M_0\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le C\sqrt{np}$. The matrix $\widetilde A-M_0$ has rank at most $2K$, proving the Frobenius bound. True probabilities lie inside the clipping interval, so clipping cannot increase squared coordinate error.

The population gap $D(p_{a*}\Vert p_{b*})$ is at least $c_1np$. On the common box, changing an observation or a parameter changes its score contrast by at most a constant times its $\ell_1$ change. Thus a row with $\|q_i-p_{a*}\|_1\le t_0np$, for sufficiently small fixed $t_0$, has fitted correct-class margin at least $c_2np$ for all large $n$. Its wrong responsibility is at most $(K-1)e^{-c_2np}$. Every other row has squared $\ell_2$ error at least $t_0^2n p^2$. The Frobenius bound limits their number to $C/p$, hence their fraction to $C/(np)$.
\end{proof}

For completeness, let $e=n^{-1}\sum_{l,a}|R_{la}-Z_{la}|$ and assume $e=o(1)$. Then $N_a=n_a+O(ne)$, and for $\bar\mu_a=n_a^{-1}\sum_{l:z_l=a}q_l$ the exact weighted mean identity gives
\[
 \|\mu_a-\bar\mu_a\|_1\le Ce\,np,
 \qquad
 \|\bar\mu_a-p_{a*}\|_1\le C\sqrt{np}.
\]
Indeed, $\max_l\|q_l\|_1\le Cnp$, while the second inequality follows by averaging the Frobenius estimate over a class of size at least $n/(\beta K)$. The updated weights equal $n_a/n+O(e)$ and are bounded below. Thus the first M-step has profile error $o(np)$. Therefore Lemma~\ref{lem:bootstrap} gives $R^{(1)}$ with error $C/(np)$. This refined radius remains invariant: an M-step from this radius has profile error at most $C\sqrt{np}+C=o(np)$ in $\ell_1$. More quantitatively, choose a fixed small responsibility radius so that the resulting parameter error costs at most one quarter of the population margin. Every E-step from that fixed radius has error at most $C_*/(np)$, where $C_*$ is independent of the previous error and of the iteration number. Since $C_*/(np)$ eventually lies inside the fixed radius, induction gives a common bound for all subsequent updates.


\subsection{The selected endpoint already has the required accuracy}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:low-loss-responsibilities}]
Write $d=np$. Since $\mathcal F\ge\sum_i\min_aD(q_i\Vert\mu_a)$ and Bernoulli divergence on the box is at least $\|q_i-\mu_a\|_1^2/(Cd)$,
\[
 \sum_i\min_a\|q_i-\mu_a\|_1^2\le C\xi_n n d^2.
\]
Also $\sum_i\|q_i-p_{z_i,*}\|_1^2\le n\|q-M_0\|_{\mathrm F}^2\le Cnd$. Choose $v_n=\xi_n^{1/4}+d^{-1/4}\to0$. The fractions of rows farther than $v_nd$ from their true profile or from every fitted profile are at most $C/(v_n^2d)$ and $C\xi_n/v_n^2$, respectively; both vanish. Each true class contains a row outside both sets. The $K$ true profiles are separated in $\ell_1$ norm by $c d$, so their nearby fitted profiles must be different. There are exactly $K$ fitted profiles, giving the asserted common matching.

Fix a sufficiently small constant $t_0$. Except for $Cn/d$ rows, the row's $\ell_1$ distance to its true profile is at most $t_0d$, by the Frobenius bound. For every such row in class $a$, matched-center consistency and box Lipschitz bounds imply
\[
 D(q_i\Vert\mu_b)\ge c_1d\quad(b\ne a),\qquad
 D(q_i\Vert\mu_a)\le c_1d/4.
\]
Each class has at least $n/(2\beta K)$ such rows for large $n$. Since each summand of $\mathcal F$ is nonnegative and on these rows is at least $-\log(\widehat\pi_a+e^{-c_1d})$, we obtain
\[
 \widehat\pi_a+e^{-c_1d}\ge e^{-2\beta K\xi_nd},\qquad
 \widehat\pi_a\ge\tfrac12 e^{-2\beta K\xi_nd}
\]
eventually. The latter holds whether or not $\xi_nd$ diverges, since $\xi_n\to0$. Thus $-\log\widehat\pi_a\le2\beta K\xi_nd+\log2=o(d)$. Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:bootstrap} to the fit itself.
\end{proof}


\section{The spectral likelihood tail retains the oracle rate}
The remaining argument compares error tails with the oracle LRT. It controls bounded changes of conditional cumulants and local tilted concentration. It does not round the spectral block sums to recover integer edge counts.
\subsection{Fitted parameters and a bounded-moment score correction}\label{app:general-local-em}
Define the perfect-label fitted mean $\bar\mu_a=n_a^{-1}\sum_{l:z_l=a}q_l$. Let
\[
 \mathcal G=\max_{a,c}\left|\frac{\one_{C_a}^\top(A-M)\one_{C_c}}{\sqrt{n_an_c}}\right|.
\]
For each fixed $t$, $\E e^{t\mathcal G}\le C_t$. Indeed, these are finitely many independent-edge sums with coefficient $O(n^{-1})$ and variance $O(p)$; the usual bounded-variable moment generating function estimate applies to both signs.

\begin{lemma}[Fitted-score comparison]\label{lem:fit}
On the full-graph good event, every M-step with aligned responsibility error at most $C/(np)$ satisfies, uniformly in $i,a,b$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fit-comparison}
 \left|\ell_i(\mu_a)-\ell_i(\mu_b)+\log\frac{\widehat\pi_a}{\widehat\pi_b}
 -\left\{\sum_jq_{ij}w_{z_j}^{ab}+\sum_jr_{z_j}^{ab}\right\}\right|
 \le C(1+\mathcal G).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The mean-fitting identity and the bound $\|q_l\|_1\le Cnp$ show $\|\mu_a-\bar\mu_a\|_1\le C$. Score derivatives in a parameter coordinate are $(q_{ij}-\mu_j)/[\mu_j(1-\mu_j)]$ and are bounded on the common box, so this replacement costs $O(1)$. Updated log-weight ratios are bounded as well.

Write $\delta_a=\bar\mu_a-p_{a*}$. Cauchy--Schwarz and the Frobenius bound give $\|\delta_a\|_2^2\le Cp$. Also
\begin{equation}\label{eq:offspan}
 \|q_i(I-\Pi)\|_2\le C\sqrt p.
\end{equation}
For $\widetilde A$, this follows from row incoherence, signal size $O(np)$, and projector error $O((np)^{-1/2})$. For $q$ it follows because a common scalar 1-Lipschitz map contracts within-block variance: use the pairwise formula for variance in each block.

We next bound each block sum of $\delta_a$. With $R=A-\widetilde A$, one has $R=(I-UU^\top)R(I-UU^\top)$ and $\|R\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le C\sqrt{np}$. Consequently
\[
 \|\Pi R\Pi\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le C\sqrt{np}\,\|\Pi-UU^\top\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2
 \le C/\sqrt{np}.
\]
Thus block-averaging $\widetilde A$ instead of $A$ changes the relevant block sum by at most a constant. Raw block averaging contributes at most $C(\mathcal G+1)$, including the absent diagonal. Finally, if a true probability $u$ is a distance at least $c'p$ from both clipping endpoints, then
\[
 |\operatorname{clip}(x,\varepsilon_n,1-\varepsilon_n)-x|\le\frac{(x-u)^2}{c'p}.
\]
Summing this inequality and averaging over class $a$ gives $\|\bar\mu_a-n_a^{-1}\one_{C_a}^\top \widetilde A\|_1\le C$. We have proved
\begin{equation}\label{eq:blockmean}
 \left|\sum_{j\in C_c}\delta_{aj}\right|\le C(1+\mathcal G).
\end{equation}

Taylor-expand $\ell_i$ in its parameter around $p_{a*}$. Its second derivatives are bounded by $C/p$, so the quadratic remainder is at most $C\|\delta_a\|_2^2/p\le C$. Split the linear term into the true block span and its orthogonal complement. The block part is bounded by \eqref{eq:blockmean}; the derivative coefficient after projection onto the true block span is block constant and bounded. The orthogonal part is bounded by $C\|q_i(I-\Pi)\|_2\|\delta_a\|_2/p\le C$ using \eqref{eq:offspan}. Apply this to both classes.
\end{proof}

\subsection{An exact arrowhead reduction of empirical spectral block sums}
Fix vertex $i$ and condition on $B=A_{-i,-i}$. Its incident-edge vector $x=A_{-i,i}$ remains independent Bernoulli with block-constant probabilities. Let $V,D$ be the retained signal eigenpairs of $B$, write $N=B-VDV^\top$, and let $\Pi_B=VV^\top$. Let $\Pi_0$ be the true block projector on the remaining vertices. On the vertex-deleted good event,
\[
 \|N\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le C\sqrt{np},\quad
 \|V\|_{2\to\infty}\le C/\sqrt n,\quad
 \|\Pi_B-\Pi_0\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le C/\sqrt{np}.
\]
All assertions below about $x$ are deterministic on $\|x\|_1\le D_0np$, with fixed sufficiently large $D_0$.

\begin{lemma}[Arrowhead linearization with bounded remainder]\label{lem:arrowhead}
For every fixed vector $w\in\mathbb R^n$ with $\|w\|_\infty\le W$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:arrowhead}
 \left|\widetilde A_iw-x^\top\Pi_B w_{-i}\right|\le C_W.
\end{equation}
The constant is uniform over all incident-edge vectors satisfying the degree bound.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Permute $i$ to the first coordinate and write $A=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&x^\top\\x&B\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. The difference from $\operatorname{diag}(0,B)$ has norm $\|x\|_2\le\sqrt{D_0np}$. Weyl's inequality consequently gives exactly $K$ eigenvalues of magnitude $\asymp np$, with all remaining eigenvalues $O(\sqrt{np})$. This assertion uses good $B$ and the degree bound alone. The retained eigenvectors can be written $u_r=(t_r,Vy_r+v_r)$ with $v_r\perp V$. The exact eigenvector equations give
\[
 v_r=t_r(\lambda_r-N)^{-1}x_Q,\qquad
 (\lambda_r-h_r)t_r=x_V^\top y_r,
 \quad h_r=x_Q^\top(\lambda_r-N)^{-1}x_Q,
\]
where $x_V=V^\top x$, $x_Q=(I-\Pi_B)x$, and $|h_r|\le C$.

The padded basis $V_0=(0,V^\top)^\top$ has spectral residual $AV_0-V_0D=(x_V^\top,0)^\top$, of norm at most $Cnp/\sqrt n$. For a full-graph bulk eigenvector $h$ and column $V_{0,r}$,
\[
 (\lambda_h-D_r)\langle h,V_{0,r}\rangle
 =\langle h,(AV_0-V_0D)_r\rangle.
\]
The denominator has magnitude at least $c np$, for either signal sign. Sum the squared identity over bulk eigenvectors and the fixed $K$ columns to obtain $\|(I-UU^\top)V_0\|_{\mathrm F}\le C/\sqrt n$. Equality of the two subspace dimensions gives $\|UU^\top-V_0V_0^\top\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le C/\sqrt n$. In particular
\[
 \sum_rt_r^2\le C/n,\qquad
 \left\|\sum_ry_ry_r^\top-I_K\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le C/n.
\]
The latter uses squared principal angles, not the unsquared projector bound.

Put $w_V=V^\top w_{-i}$ and $w_Q=(I-\Pi_B)w_{-i}$. Expanding the retained spectral sum gives
\begin{align*}
 \widetilde A_iw={}&x_V^\top\left(\sum_ry_ry_r^\top\right)w_V
       +\sum_r h_rt_ry_r^\top w_V\\
 &+\sum_r\lambda_rt_r^2
       \{w_i+x_Q^\top(\lambda_r-N)^{-1}w_Q\}.
\end{align*}
The errors relative to $x_V^\top w_V$ are bounded respectively by
\[
 C(np/\sqrt n)n^{-1}\sqrt n=Cp,\qquad Cn^{-1/2}\sqrt n=C,
 \qquad C(np/n)\{1+\sqrt{np}\sqrt n/(np)\}\le C.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:arrowhead} without assuming independence between the full-graph eigenvectors and $x$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Projected block weights]\label{lem:weights}
For any true block-constant vector $v$ on the remaining vertices with bounded entries, let $\beta=(\Pi_B-I)v$. Then
\[
 \|\beta\|_\infty\le C,\quad \|\beta\|_2^2\le C/p,
 \quad \left|\sum_{j\in C_c\setminus\{i\}}\beta_j\right|\le C/p.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The first follows from $V$'s row bound and $\|v\|_2=O(\sqrt n)$, and the second from the projector bound. For the third, since $v\in\operatorname{col}(\Pi_0)$,
\[
 \Pi_0\beta=-\Pi_0(I-\Pi_B)\Pi_0v,
 \qquad\|\Pi_0(I-\Pi_B)\Pi_0\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le\|\Pi_0-\Pi_B\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2\le C/(np).
\]
Take its inner product with a block indicator.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Clipping and the oracle tail prefactor}\label{app:general-clipped-chernoff}
Choose a sufficiently small fixed $h>0$ such that $2c_0+h<\min(\kappa,\eta)/2$, and put
\[
 l=(2c_0+h)p,\qquad u=1-(2c_0+h)p.
\]
For each full block $C_c$ set $T_c=\widetilde A_i\one_{C_c}$ and $Y_c=x^\top\Pi_B\one_{C_c\setminus\{i\}}$.

\begin{lemma}[Deterministic buffered clipping envelopes]\label{lem:clip}
On the full-graph and vertex-deleted good events and the degree event,
\begin{align*}
 \sum_{j\in C_c}q_{ij}&\ge\min(Y_c,n_cu)-C,\\
 \sum_{j\in C_c}q_{ij}&\le\max(Y_c,n_cl)+C.
\end{align*}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $\widetilde A_{ij}=T_c/n_c+v_j$ in block $c$. The $v_j$ sum to zero and \eqref{eq:offspan} for $\widetilde A$ gives $\sum v_j^2\le Cp$. If the block mean is at least $hp$ below an upper clipping endpoint, its upper clipping loss is at most $\sum(v_j-hp)_+\le\sum v_j^2/(4hp)\le C$. If it is above that buffered endpoint, monotonicity gives the same lower envelope with the block mean replaced by the buffered endpoint. The lower endpoint is treated symmetrically. Use $\varepsilon_n\le2c_0p$ and then $|T_c-Y_c|\le C$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:arrowhead}.
\end{proof}

For a fixed pair $a,b$, abbreviate $w_c=w_c^{ab}$, $r_c=r_c^{ab}$, and define
\[
 H(x)=\sum_{w_c\ge0}w_c\min(Y_c,n_cu)
       +\sum_{w_c<0}w_c\max(Y_c,n_cl)+\sum_cn_cr_c.
\]
The true-profile score based on $q$ is at least $H(x)-C$. For each clipping pattern $S\subseteq[K]$, where $S$ denotes the unclipped coordinates, its branch is
\[
 H_S(x)=x^\top\Pi_B v_S+\sum_{c\notin S}w_ck_c+\sum_cn_cr_c,
 \quad (v_S)_j=w_{z_j}\one\{z_j\in S\},
\]
with $k_c=n_cu$ for $w_c\ge0$ and $k_c=n_cl$ for $w_c<0$. One has $H(x)=H_S(x)$ on the appropriate branch region; dropping that region gives an upper bound on each branch's error probability.

\begin{lemma}[Conditional cumulant and concentration bounds]\label{lem:conditional}
Let $\alpha=\alpha^*_{i,ab}$. Uniformly over good $B$ and every branch,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:branch-mgf}
 \E_a[e^{-\alpha H_S(x)}\mid B]\le C e^{-D^*_{i,ab}}.
\end{equation}
If $S$ contains a block with $|w_c|\ge\tau$, for a fixed $\tau>0$, then, for every real $t$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:branch-tail}
 \PP_a\{H_S(x)\le t\mid B\}
 \le\frac{C_\tau e^{-D^*_{i,ab}+\alpha t}}{\sqrt{np}}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
First replace $\Pi_Bv_S$ by $v_S$. Write $m_c=n_c-\one\{z_i=c\}$. The ideal counts in different blocks are independent $\operatorname{Binomial}(m_c,P_{ac})$. For sufficiently large $n$, the lower cutoff satisfies $n_cl\le m_cP_{ac}$, including the deleted vertex's class, since $l<\kappa p/2$ and $n_c\ge n/(\beta K)$. The upper cutoff is above its mean as well. Thus each omitted coordinate satisfies $w_ck_c\ge w_c\E_a\sum_{j\in C_c\setminus\{i\}}x_j$. Jensen's inequality bounds its deterministic exponential factor by that block's full exponential moment. The ideal branch moment is at most $e^{-D^*_{i,ab}+O(p)}$: the branch and oracle intercepts differ by exactly $\sum_c(n_c-m_c)r_c=r_{z_i}=O(p)$.

For the empirical projection put $\beta=(\Pi_B-I)v_S$. Taylor-expand the log moment as a function of its coefficient vector. At the ideal block-constant coefficients its first derivative is a bounded multiple of a block-constant tilted probability, of order $p$. The block-sum bound in Lemma~\ref{lem:weights} makes the linear change $O(1)$. Along the segment to the perturbed coefficients every second derivative is bounded by $Cp$, since all coefficients are bounded. The quadratic change is at most $Cp\|\beta\|_2^2\le C$. This proves \eqref{eq:branch-mgf}.

Under exponential tilting by $e^{-\alpha H_S}$ the coordinates of $x$ are still independent Bernoulli, with probabilities comparable to $p$ and complements bounded below. If a block in $S$ has $|w_c|\ge\tau$, then $\|v_S\|_2^2\ge c_\tau n$, while $\|\beta\|_2^2\le C/p=o(n)$. The variance of $x^\top\Pi_Bv_S$ under this tilted law is therefore between $c_\tau np$ and $Cnp$. Bounded coefficients give a total third absolute centered moment at most a constant times this variance. Berry--Esseen consequently bounds the probability of every interval of length one by $C_\tau/\sqrt{np}$.

Divide $(-\infty,t]$ into intervals $(t-r-1,t-r]$. Changing measure back gives
\[
 \PP_a(H_S\le t\mid B)
 \le \E_a(e^{-\alpha H_S}\mid B)
       \frac{C_\tau e^{\alpha t}}{\sqrt{np}}
       \sum_{r\ge0}e^{-\alpha r}.
\]
The maximizing $\alpha$ is uniformly bounded away from zero. This proves \eqref{eq:branch-tail}; no nonlattice assumption or exact boundary density is used.
\end{proof}

Some branches can have no strongly weighted unclipped coordinate. They cannot cause an error on the graph event. In fact the oracle mean LLR is at least $c_1np$. Omitted coordinates replace their means in the score-increasing direction. On $\|x\|_1\le D_0np$, $|Y_c|\le C D_0np$. Therefore, if every unclipped weight has magnitude less than $\tau$,
\[
 H_S(x)\ge c_1np-K\tau(CD_0+1)np-O(1)\ge c_1np/2
\]
for a sufficiently small fixed $\tau$. Thus all potentially erroneous branches have the concentration required by Lemma~\ref{lem:conditional}.

\subsection{Completing the refined risk bound}
Let $\mathcal G_i$ denote the block fluctuation $\mathcal G$ computed on the vertex-deleted graph $B$, with the corresponding block sizes. On the good graph and degree event,
\[
 \mathcal G\le C(1+\mathcal G_i),\qquad \mathcal G_i\le C\sqrt{np},
 \qquad \E e^{t\mathcal G_i}\le C_t\quad(t\text{ fixed}).
\]
The first bound holds because adding back one row changes a normalized block sum by at most $C\deg(i)/n+Cp\le C$.

After an M-step from the $C/(np)$ responsibility radius, Lemma~\ref{lem:fit} shows that assigning vertex $i$ from $a$ to $b$ implies
\[
 H(x)\le C(1+\mathcal G_i).
\]
First use the full-graph good event only to establish this deterministic containment of the error event. Then remove its indicator from the upper bound; it is not assumed independent of $x$. Conditional on good $B$, branches with only weak unclipped weights are impossible on the degree event for large $n$, since $\mathcal G_i=O(\sqrt{np})$. For every other branch apply \eqref{eq:branch-tail}, with threshold $C(1+\mathcal G_i)$. Integrating over $B$ and using its bounded exponential moment yields
\[
 \PP_a\{\text{aligned assignment }i:a\to b,\ \text{good events}\}
 \le C\frac{e^{-D^*_{i,ab}}}{\sqrt{np}}.
\]
The union over $b\ne a$ and the at most $2^K$ branches costs a fixed constant. The graph, vertex-deleted graph, and degree failures are $C_H e^{-Hnp}$. Since $I_n\le C_I np$, choose fixed $H>C_I+1$; their total contribution is negligible relative to $e^{-I_n}/\sqrt{np}$. The conclusion is uniform over every fitted endpoint in the refined responsibility radius: the same graph event and the same fluctuation $\mathcal G_i$ control them all. Thus a data-dependent number of additional updates requires no union bound over iterations.


\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:local-spectral-em}]
An initial M-step from responsibilities of error $\gamma_n=o(1)$ has profile error $o(np)$ and weights bounded below, by the mean-fitting identity in Appendix~\ref{app:replacement-proof}. Lemma~\ref{lem:bootstrap} makes the next E-step error at most $C/(np)$. The second M-step therefore satisfies Lemma~\ref{lem:fit}, and the conditional tail argument above gives \eqref{eq:main-rate}. If the initial responsibilities already have error $C/(np)$, the first M-step itself meets that comparison.

Choose $H>C_I+1$, where $I_n\le C_I np$, and include the stipulated initialization failure. Since $np\to\infty$, all exceptional probabilities $C_H e^{-Hnp}$ are negligible relative to $e^{-I_n}/\sqrt{np}$. Summing over the fixed number of competing classes and averaging over vertices proves the expectation bound. The invariant radius in Lemma~\ref{lem:bootstrap}, together with the comparison uniform over that radius, covers all further finite exact updates and finite data-dependent stopping rules.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main-recovery}]
Fix the same sufficiently large $H$. On $G_H$, all observed profiles and every convex-combination mean generated by the algorithm lie in the common box. Theorem~\ref{thm:replacement-contraction} gives a deterministic uniform sequence $\xi_n\to0$ such that the selected endpoint has $\mathcal F\le\xi_n n^2p$, regardless of the growing endpoint. Lemma~\ref{lem:low-loss-responsibilities} then shows that the algorithm's mandatory final E-step has error at most $C/(np)$. Its mandatory M-step satisfies the strong-initialization clause of Theorem~\ref{thm:local-spectral-em}. This proves \eqref{eq:main-rate}, including initialization and estimated parameters, with no additional raw-edge access.

Finally $n\Mis$ is an integer, so $\PP(\Mis>0)\le n\E\Mis$, giving the stated exact-recovery condition. The computational cost in Section~\ref{sec:algorithm} gives the polynomial-time assertion with polynomial fitting caps.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Scope of the sparse-score comparison}
For $p\le1/2$, the inequalities
$|\log(1-u)+u|\le Cu^2$ and
$|\log(1-u)-\log(1-v)|\le Cp$, for $u,v\le p$, show that replacing the exact oracle Bernoulli contrast by the sparse contrast changes it by at most
$C\{p\deg(i)+np^2\}$. A degree bound at any fixed confidence $e^{-Hnp}$ makes this $O(np^2)=o(np)$ if $p\to0$. This estimate does not establish the Zhou--Li affinity bound; that requires the bounded-cumulant analysis above. The dense example in the main text follows from the two Bernoulli large-deviation exponents at the stated thresholds.

\subsection{Unregularized implementation}
The source, complete numerical evidence and compiled manuscript are at
\begin{center}
\url{https://github.com/zhixin0825/spectral-lrt}.
\end{center}
Algorithm~1 is implemented by \texttt{SpectralLikelihood.decode()} in
\begin{center}
\small\path{experiments/20261010_general_density/spectral_likelihood.py}.
\end{center}
It accepts an $n\times K$ eigenvector array and a length-$K$ array of the corresponding signed eigenvalues. This unregularized decoder receives neither the adjacency matrix, true labels, true block probabilities nor node degrees. Retaining the top-$|\lambda|$ eigenpairs is its preceding spectral step. The general decoder below additionally retains the observed degree vector.

The default \texttt{score\_family='bernoulli'} evaluates \eqref{eq:working-score}. The floor coefficient defaults to $c_0=10^{-4}$; its theoretical guarantee requires the explicit admissibility condition in Section~\ref{sec:algorithm}, not merely positive block probabilities. The spectral scale is $\max\{1,\max_a|\lambda_a|\}$, and the reconstructed entries are clipped on both sides. The original adjacency is not regularized. The optional \texttt{score\_family='sparse'} is a score control on precisely the same $q$, and is not the default general-density algorithm.

Growing uses at most 100 exact M-steps per stage. A fit stops after at least one M-step if hard labels are unchanged and its absolute soft-loss change is at most $10^{-9}n$. Replacement uses at most $\lceil\log n\rceil$ rounds, tries every component deletion against the same incumbent, initializes each trial's weights uniformly, and uses one M-step per trial by default. A full round with no improvement stops the search. This rule is justified by the contraction certificate in Appendix~\ref{app:replacement-proof}. Calling \texttt{decode()} always executes the final E-step and one profile/weight M-step before returning labels.

The implementation uses the unconstrained simplex weight update. Zero-mass components retain their previous profile. Log-sum-exp arithmetic evaluates responsibilities and the soft divergence loss $F$ without exponentiating very negative raw log-likelihoods. Theoretical divergences are nonnegative; the code clips negative floating-point residue at zero. Spectral block sums are not rounded to integers. Weighted means, including the initial global mean, are clipped back to the same feasible interval solely to correct roundoff in convex combinations. The theorem is an exact-arithmetic result; recorded numerical checks assess implementation accuracy to floating-point precision.

The user may increase either fitting budget. Explicitly requesting fewer replacement rounds is an experimental option and does not automatically retain the stated general-$K$ initialization guarantee. The proven default budget is $\lceil\log n\rceil$, with valid early stopping after a full unsuccessful round.

 \max_{\pi_a\ge f,\,\sum_a\pi_a=1}\sum_aN_a\log\pi_a,
