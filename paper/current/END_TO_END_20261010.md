# General K and density p = Ω(log n/n): completed proof

This revision builds on the concurrently completed sparse general-K threshold-certificate version preserved at commit 452ca1d. Both use deterministic replacement and density `p = Ω(log n/n)`, where `p = max_ab P_ab`. The current theorem keeps the complete Bernoulli nonedge term to include dense probabilities, removes convergence requirements on `P/p` and community proportions, and gives a direct soft-loss geometric contraction. The still earlier fixed-logarithmic-density and random-residual-safeguard versions remain in repository history.

## 1. Precise scope

Write `p = max_ab P_ab`. The actual Bernoulli matrix P is symmetric and may vary with n. The assumptions are `n_a >= pi0*n`, `P_ab >= kappa*p` for every a,b, `sigma_min(P) >= kappa*p`, `p <= 1-eta`, and `p = Ω(log n/n)`, with fixed positive constants. Neither `P/p` nor community proportions need converge. Expected degrees may differ across communities; the uniform probability bounds make each expected degree `Θ(np)`, so the density assumption supplies an `Ω(log n)` lower bound.

The profile floor is adaptive: `epsilon=c0*max(1,||Lambda||op)/n`, with a fixed admissible `0<c0<min(kappa,eta,1)/8`. The eigenpairs come from the original A. All subsequent quantities use only those eigenpairs.

The theorem is for **growing followed by repeated component replacement and a mandatory final M-step**. It is not a proof of unchanged growing alone. Every fit may stop after any finite number of exact EM updates, provided it takes at least one M-step.

## 2. The replacement lemma

Let the K true profiles be `p_a`, the K current profiles be `mu_k`, and define

`G_*(C)=sum_a n_a min_k D(p_a || mu_k)`,

where D is the product-Bernoulli divergence. Select a minimizing center `phi(a)` for each true profile and choose a class a with the largest weighted loss, at least `G_*/K`.

If phi is not onto, an unused center can be deleted without affecting any class's selected representative. If phi is onto, it is a bijection; deleting `phi(a)` leaves every other class's representative available. In both cases, inserting `p_a` sets its loss to zero and cannot increase any other class's loss. Thus

`G_*(C after one suitable replacement) <= (1-1/K) G_*(C)`.

This argument tolerates duplicate centers, nearest-center ties and arbitrary fitted profiles. It uses only nonnegative loss and zero self-loss.

## 3. Transfer to the executable algorithm

On a common spectral event, all observed and fitted profiles lie in a box `[c*p, min(C*p,1-b)]^n`. D is uniformly Lipschitz in each argument's l1 norm there, and

`sum_i ||q_i-p_(z_i)||_1 <= a_n n^2 p`, with deterministic `a_n -> 0`.

This gives `sup_C |G(C)-G_*(C)| <= C a_n n^2 p` simultaneously over all fitted center lists. Every true community supplies an observed candidate with l1 error at most `a_n n p/pi0`.

For each deletion position, maximizing the all-node positive likelihood gain is exactly minimizing the resulting empirical hard loss. Testing all K deletion positions therefore includes a candidate as good as the population replacement, up to the same perturbation error.

The working likelihood equals an observation-only saturation constant minus

`F(pi,C) = -sum_i log sum_k pi_k exp(-D(q_i || mu_k))`.

The exact inequalities are `G<=F` and, under uniform weights, `F<=G+n log K`. Each trial starts with uniform weights; exact EM only decreases F. Selecting the best endpoint and retaining the old fit as a candidate yields

`F_(t+1) <= (1-1/K) F_t + B a_n n^2 p + n log K`.

All profiles in the box have pairwise D bounded by `M n p`, so `F_0<=M n^2 p` for arbitrary simplex weights, including zeros. At `T=ceil(log n)`, division by `n^2 p` gives

`M(1-1/K)^T + KB a_n + K log K/(n p) -> 0`.

A round with no improving trial already gives `F_t <= K(B a_n n^2 p+n log K)`. Thus early stopping is justified by the contraction itself, without an assumption about how later fitting budgets would be chosen.

## 4. Low soft loss gives weak responsibilities

For every deterministic `xi_n -> 0`, the claim is uniform over all fits with `F<=xi_n n^2 p`. Strong convexity gives

`D(x||y) >= ||x-y||_1^2/(2 C n p)`.

The average profile approximation and small hard loss ensure that each of the K positive-proportion true communities has a nearby fitted profile. Quantitative separation and exactly K fitted profiles force a one-to-one matching.

For the exact E-step, the variational identity is

`F_i = sum_k R_ik D(q_i||mu_k) + KL(R_i||pi)`.

Wrongly matched profiles cost a fixed positive multiple of `n p` on all but a vanishing fraction of rows. The identity therefore bounds the average wrong responsibility by o(1), regardless of data dependence or zero mixture weights.

## 5. The density extension needs exponentially reliable spectral bounds

Bandeira–van Handel, Corollary 3.12 and Remark 3.13, give a centered bounded-entry spectral tail that implies `P(||A-EA||>C_H sqrt(np))<=exp(-H np)` for every fixed H, since `np = Ω(log n)`.

AFWZ Theorem 2.1, with its Bernoulli Lemma 7 evaluated at failure `2 exp(-alpha n p)`, supplies the corresponding first-order entrywise approximation. Positive and negative signal groups are treated separately. The exact mean `ZPZ' - diag(P_aa)` preserves the membership subspace; the missing diagonal is not discarded from the proof.

An exact reconstruction identity yields `max_i ||q_i-q_i^0||_1=o(np)`, where `q^0` is the equally clipped oracle block projection. A bounded-differences bound on the total block-count deviation yields the required average error. All bounds hold on one event with failure `C_H exp(-H np)`. This matters when the target risk is exponentially smaller than any fixed power of n.

## 6. Random clipping and local fitting

The clipping threshold is computed from the whole graph, so conditioning on it does not leave incident counts independent. The proof instead uses the deterministic upper bound `epsilon_+=2 c0 p` on the good event.

For each block, the sign of the true logit contrast determines which clipping side can worsen the Chernoff moment. A deterministic one-sided envelope bounds that contribution, and Jensen's inequality costs at most a factor two. Only then are the independent raw block-count moments multiplied. The full moment loses at most `2^K`; the retained reconstructed diagonal adds an `O(p)=o(np)` offset.

Weak responsibilities followed by one exact M-step give fitted profile error `O((gamma_n+a_n)np)`. Uniform derivative bounds on the common box turn this into an `o(np)` contrast error. An invariant-basin argument applies to every later finite exact EM update on the same event, including data-dependent stopping, without an iteration union bound.

The uniform signal assumptions imply `I_n = Θ(np)`. Choose a fixed `H>1+limsup I_n/(np)` so that the spectral failure is negligible at the target scale. The final conclusion is

`E Mis <= exp(-(1+o(1)) I_n)`.

`liminf I_n/log n>1` implies exact recovery by `P(Mis>0)<=n E Mis`.

## 7. Why the full Bernoulli score is a real algorithm change

For sparse probabilities `p -> 0`, the old score's oracle contrast differs from the full one by `O(p*deg(i)+n*p^2)=o(np)`. The requirement is a relative o(I_n) error, not absolute o(1); there is no need for `n*p^2=o(1)`.

For the balanced dense example `P=[[.8,.2],[.2,.2]]`, the true threshold for the informative block is 1/2. Its exponent per potential neighbor is 0.22314355. The sparse score uses `.6/log(4)` and its slower directional exponent is 0.13905314, about 62.32% of the correct exponent. The loss persists at known parameters. The full score removes this obstruction, while its M-step remains the weighted mean.

## 8. Evidence and limits

A K=5 exact rational population certificate shows failure of one-update-per-stage growing, with limiting error 13/200. One replacement round repairs it. It does not prove failure of fully converged growing or constitute a sampled-graph failure theorem.

The 60-graph full-score audit records all six methods, including both score controls and two spectral K-means baselines. Replacement does not change any graph's misclassification. Weak general matrices remain difficult at n=320. The result is reproducibility and finite-sample evidence, not an empirical proof of the asymptotic theorem or a claim of uniform numerical superiority.
