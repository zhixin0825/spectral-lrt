\title{Constant oracle-risk bounds for normalized spectral likelihood\\
\large Regularized Bernoulli SBM and DC-SBM at every diverging degree scale}
\author{Research proof note for the spectral-lrt project}
\date{20 October 2026 --- revision 3}

\begin{abstract}
We give finite classifiers based only on raw degrees and one retained
normalized eigensystem, and prove expected misclassification risk at
most a constant times a precisely specified local oracle risk.
The primary regularized results require \(d=np\to\infty\) and
\(np^2\le C_1\), with no logarithmic degree lower bound. They cover
fixed arbitrary \(K\), comparable group sizes, and general positive
full-rank compact block profiles. A positive empirical degree floor,
exact degree preservation, deterministic radius-count initialization,
and exact block Bernoulli fitting define the algorithm. Ordinary SBM
uses the unprofiled Bernoulli LRT and is compared to its true-parameter
raw-row oracle. Bounded-parameter DC-SBM uses a clamped, normalized
nuisance fit and target profiling, and is compared to the full-edge
profile oracle. Equal block exposures further give comparison to
the oracle supplied with the target's true degree parameter, including
the classical balanced planted partition.
The proof restores the earlier sparse truncated-row-energy mechanism,
upgrades local full-to-LOO and fitted-score errors to \(O(1)\), retains
the sharp \(d^{-1/2}\) prefactor, and pays exceptional rows locally.
Polynomially accurate regularized geometry and exponentially accurate
raw norm bounds cover the two proof ranges without restricting the
theorem at \(\log n\).
Earlier unregularized decoder results are retained separately, including
the ordinary free-profile likelihood schedule beyond bounded \(np^2\);
the new theorem does not silently extend that unchanged schedule.
A two-point counterexample outside the equal-exposure class still
precludes the unrestricted known-target-degree DC oracle claim.
All constants are independent of \(n\); a bounded risk ratio is not
a claim that the ratio converges to one.
\end{abstract}
